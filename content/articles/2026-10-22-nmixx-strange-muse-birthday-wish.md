NMIXX are back, and fast. The JYP Entertainment girl group released their sixth mini album, *Strange Muse*, on Monday, October 19, 2026, at 6 p.m. KST, about five months after *Heavy Serenade*. The title track, **긴 소원** (gin sowon) – "Birthday Wish" in English – entered the Melon Top 100, a major Korean streaming chart, at No. 34 one hour after release. That was the group's best debut position there so far. By midnight it had climbed to No. 5.

## The album

*Strange Muse* has six songs. Its concept is about people inspiring each other as "muses" in MIXXTOPIA, a place where you can show your individuality even when others don't understand it. The title track mixes moombahton, Afro house and future house, then drops into a quiet piano-and-vocal break before a narrated bridge. Member Lily said it was her first time rapping on a title track.

Two firsts make this release stand out:

- **Units**: three pairs each have their own song – Lily and Haewon on "Sing to the Moon," Sullyoon and Bae on "Hot 'n Unbothered," and Jiwoo and Kyujin on "WHIP."
- **Songwriting**: it is the first NMIXX album with member writing credits. Bae co-wrote two songs with Sullyoon, and Kyujin helped write and compose.

Outside Korea, the album reached the top of iTunes album charts in several regions, including Singapore and Hong Kong. On October 21, JYP shared behind-the-scenes photos from the music video to thank fans, called NSWER.

## What's next

The group plans a concert with NSWER in November, and their first world tour, *EPISODE 1: ZERO FRONTIER*, returns for an encore at Inspire Arena in Incheon on November 27–29 (as of October 2026; check official channels for details). In September, NMIXX also won "Sound of the Year," one of the grand prizes, at the 2026 The Fact Music Awards.

## Why it matters for learners

The Korean title is a nice small lesson. [소원](/en/dictionary/?q=소원) (sowon) means "wish," and **긴** (gin) is the form of **길다** (gilda), "to be long," used before a noun. So 긴 소원 is literally "a long wish" – the English title adds the birthday. On a [생일](/en/dictionary/?q=생일) (saengil), birthday, Koreans say **소원 빌어** (sowon bireo) – "make a wish" – just before blowing out the candles. The verb is **빌다** (bilda), to wish or pray.

The same pattern turns any describing verb into a noun modifier: **길다 → 긴** (long), **달다 → 단** (sweet), **멀다 → 먼** (far). Verbs whose stem ends in ㄹ drop it and add ㄴ.

The unit song "Sing to the Moon" also has a Korean title: **너는 달** (neoneun dal), "you are the moon."
