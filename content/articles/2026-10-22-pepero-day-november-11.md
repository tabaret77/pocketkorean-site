Walk into any Korean convenience store in early November and you'll find towers of red boxes, giant gift packs and heart-shaped bundles, all of the same snack: **빼빼로** (ppaeppaero), thin biscuit sticks dipped in chocolate. They're for **빼빼로데이** (ppaeppaero dei), Pepero Day, on 11 November. In 2026 it falls on a Wednesday. It isn't a public holiday, and no one gets the day off, but it's one of the most visible dates of the Korean autumn.

## Why 11 November?

Look at the date written as numbers: 11/11. The four ones look like four thin sticks standing in a row. That's the whole idea.

The snack itself was launched by the confectionery company Lotte in 1983. The most famous origin story traces it to a 1983 report about two middle-school girls in the southeastern Yeongnam region who swapped Pepero on 11 November, wishing each other to grow "tall and slim like a Pepero stick." Nobody has confirmed the story, and some people say the date simply suits the shape. What is certain is that Lotte began promoting the day in 1997, and it grew from there. The name of the snack plays on **빼빼** (ppaeppae), a word for being skinny.

## How people celebrate

- **Friends and classmates** exchange boxes at school, often with a short note.
- **Couples** treat it as a mini Valentine's Day, with bigger gift sets or handmade sticks.
- **Coworkers** may leave a box on each other's desks, the way people share chocolates in an office elsewhere.
- **Shops** go all out. Convenience stores report some of their biggest sales of the year around this date, and you'll see bundles mixed with toys, mugs and character goods.

It's also seen as a light-hearted day for singles, since the ones in 11/11 look like people standing alone.

## The other 11 November: farmers and rice cakes

Not everyone loves Pepero Day. Critics call it a marketing event, and it shares the date with an official commemorative day: **농업인의 날** (nongeobinui nal), Farmers' Day, designated in 1996. The date was chosen because the character for "soil," 土, is made of 十 (ten) and 一 (one).

To support farmers, some schools, offices and rice producers promote **가래떡데이** (garaetteok dei), Garaetteok Day. **가래떡** (garaetteok) are long, white cylinders of rice cake, which also look like the number 1. You might see them handed out at stations or sold in gift boxes, sometimes dipped in honey.

## If you're in Korea on 11 November

- Pick up a box at any convenience store: it's an easy, friendly gift for a language partner, teacher or coworker.
- Look for limited flavours and character packaging, which appear only around this time.
- Try a piece of fresh garaetteok if you see it. It's chewy, mild and very good toasted.
