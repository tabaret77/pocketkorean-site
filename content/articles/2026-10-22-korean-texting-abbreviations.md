Open a Korean group chat and some messages look like a string of letters with no vowels: **ㄱㅅ**, **ㅇㅋ**, **ㄹㅇ**. These are **초성** (choseong) shortcuts. 초성 means the first consonant of a syllable, and Koreans type just those consonants to write a word in two quick taps. You've probably already met **ㅋㅋ** and **ㅇㅇ**. Here are the next ones you'll need.

## How they work

Every Korean syllable starts with a consonant. Take **감사** (gamsa), thanks: 감 starts with **ㄱ**, 사 starts with **ㅅ**. Drop everything else and you get **ㄱㅅ**. Once you see the trick, you can often guess a new shortcut from context. Many are also doubled for extra feeling: **ㄱㅅㄱㅅ**, **ㅊㅋㅊㅋ**.

## Thanks, sorry, congrats

- **ㄱㅅ** – **[감사](/en/dictionary/?q=감사)** (gamsa), thanks. Casual and quick: "thx".
- **ㅈㅅ** – **죄송** (joesong), sorry. Despite coming from a polite word, ㅈㅅ is casual and light, like "sry".
- **ㅊㅋ** – **[축하](/en/dictionary/?q=축하)** (chukha), congrats. **ㅊㅋㅊㅋ** is the standard reply to good news.
- **ㅅㅊ** – **생축** (saengchuk), short for **생일 축하** (saengil chukha), happy birthday.

## Yes, no and let's go

- **ㅇㅋ** – **오케이** (okei), okay.
- **ㄴㄴ** – **노노** (nono), no, no. Often softer than a full "no": "nah, it's fine".
- **ㄱㄱ** – **고고** (gogo), let's go. **ㅇㅋ ㄱㄱ** means "Okay, let's do it."
- **ㄱㄷ** – **기다려** (gidaryeo), wait. Usually **ㄱㄷㄱㄷ**: "hang on".

## Hello and goodbye

- **ㅎㅇ** – **하이** (hai), hi.
- **ㅂㅂ** or **ㅂㅇ** – **바이바이** (baibai) or **바이** (bai), bye.
- **ㅅㄱ** – **수고** (sugo), from **수고해** (sugohae) – "good work". Used to sign off at the end of a group project or a game: "gg, see you".

## Reactions

- **ㄹㅇ** – **리얼** (rieol), from English "real": for real, seriously. **ㄹㅇ 맛있어** (rieol masisseo) – "It's seriously good."
- **ㅇㅈ** – **인정** (injeong), literally "recognition": "agreed", "true", "fair point". Someone makes a good point; you reply ㅇㅈ.
- **ㄷㄷ** – **덜덜** (deoldeol), the sound of trembling. Used for "whoa", "scary" or "impressive": a huge price, a scary story, an amazing performance.
- **ㅇㄷ** – **어디** (eodi), where? As in **ㅇㄷ야?** – "Where are you?"

## Online-forum ones

- **ㅈㄱㄴ** – **제곧내** (jegonnae), short for **제목이 곧 내용** (jemogi got naeyong): "the title says it all". People write it in the body of a post when the headline already asks the whole question.
- **ㅂㄷㅂㄷ** – **부들부들** (budeulbudeul), shaking with anger. Playful frustration, like "grr".

## Use with caution

- **ㅁㅊ** – **미친** (michin), crazy. Very common for "insane!" (good or bad), but it's a strong word. Keep it for close friends.

## Who can you send them to?

초성 shortcuts are strictly **반말** (banmal) territory: friends, classmates, siblings, gaming teammates. Never send ㄱㅅ to a teacher, a boss or someone older you're not close to. It reads like sending "thx" in a work email, only ruder. With them, type the whole thing: **감사합니다** (gamsahamnida). The same goes for ㅈㅅ: to an older person, write **죄송합니다** (joesonghamnida) in full.

You'll also see 초성 outside chats. **초성 퀴즈** (choseong kwijeu), where players guess a word from its consonants, is a favourite game on Korean variety shows. It's also good practice for you: try guessing what **ㅅㄹㅎ** might be (hint: 사랑해).

Useful pattern: **생일 축하해! ㅊㅋㅊㅋ** (Saengil chukahae!) – Happy birthday! Congrats! Write the full phrase first and add the 초성 as a cheerful echo; it's the friendliest way to use them.
