You don't need much Korean to get through a day in Seoul, but a few well-chosen phrases make taxis, cafés and convenience stores much smoother. Everything below uses the polite **-요** (-yo) ending or **주세요** (juseyo), "please give me / please do," which is right for talking to drivers and staff.

The pattern to remember: **[thing] + 주세요** for "[thing], please," and **[verb] + 아/어 주세요** for "please [do something]."

## In a taxi

Many drivers speak little English, so show the address on your phone, preferably in Korean, and use these:

1. **여기로 가 주세요.** (yeogiro ga juseyo.) – Please take me here. *(while showing the address)*
2. **명동역까지 가 주세요.** (myeongdong-yeok-kkaji ga juseyo.) – Please go to Myeongdong Station. *Swap in any place name before **까지** (kkaji), "to/as far as."*
3. **여기 세워 주세요.** (yeogi sewo juseyo.) – Please stop here.
4. **카드 돼요?** (kadeu dwaeyo?) – Can I pay by card? *Most taxis take cards and transit cards, but asking is polite.*
5. **트렁크 좀 열어 주세요.** (teureongkeu jom yeoreo juseyo.) – Could you open the trunk, please? *Adding **좀** (jom), "a little," softens a request.*

You may hear **어디로 가세요?** (eodiro gaseyo?) – "Where are you going?" – as you get in. Fasten your seatbelt; drivers and passengers are expected to wear one.

## At a café

6. **아이스 아메리카노 한 잔 주세요.** (aiseu amerikano han jan juseyo.) – One iced americano, please. ***잔** (jan) is the counter for cups and glasses: 한 잔 (han jan) one, 두 잔 (du jan) two.*
7. **따뜻한 걸로 주세요.** (ttatteutan geollo juseyo.) – A hot one, please. *Iced drinks are the default for many Koreans, even in winter, so staff may ask: **따뜻한 거요, 아이스요?** (ttatteutan geoyo, aiseuyo?) – "Hot or iced?"*
8. **먹고 갈게요. / 가져갈게요.** (meokgo galgeyo. / gajyeogalgeyo.) – For here. / To go. *You'll often be asked **드시고 가세요?** (deusigo gaseyo?) – "Are you eating here?"*
9. **얼음 적게 주세요.** (eoreum jeokge juseyo.) – Less ice, please.
10. **화장실 비밀번호가 뭐예요?** (hwajangsil bimilbeonhoga mwoyeyo?) – What's the restroom code? *Many café restrooms have a keypad lock; the code is often printed on your receipt.*

When your drink is ready, a buzzer (**진동벨**, jindongbel) may vibrate, or staff will call your order number.

## At the convenience store

Korean convenience stores (**편의점**, pyeonuijeom) sell everything from lunch boxes to phone chargers, and most are open 24 hours.

11. **봉투 하나 주세요.** (bongtu hana juseyo.) – One bag, please. *Bags aren't free, so the cashier will often ask **봉투 필요하세요?** (bongtu piryohaseyo?) – "Do you need a bag?" If not: **괜찮아요** (gwaenchanayo), "I'm fine, thanks."*
12. **이거 원 플러스 원이에요?** (igeo won peulleoseu wonieyo?) – Is this buy-one-get-one-free? *Look for "1+1" and "2+1" stickers on the shelves.*
13. **교통카드 충전해 주세요.** (gyotong kadeu chungjeonhae juseyo.) – Please top up my transit card. *Hand over the card with cash.*
14. **젓가락 주세요.** (jeotgarak juseyo.) – Chopsticks, please. *Useful when you buy instant noodles or a lunch box.*
15. **영수증은 괜찮아요.** (yeongsujeungeun gwaenchanayo.) – I don't need the receipt.

The cashier may also ask **포인트 카드 있으세요?** (pointeu kadeu isseuseyo?) – "Do you have a points card?" A simple **없어요** (eopseoyo), "I don't," is enough.

## Two phrases that help everywhere

If you get lost in a conversation, two short phrases go a long way:

- **다시 한번 말씀해 주세요.** (dasi hanbeon malsseumhae juseyo.) – Could you say that again, please?
- **감사합니다.** (gamsahamnida.) – Thank you. *Say it when you leave the taxi, take your coffee or walk out of the shop.*
