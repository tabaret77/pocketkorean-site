Adding **요** makes your Korean polite. But when you talk to or about someone older or senior, such as a grandparent, a teacher or a customer, Korean goes one step further. Most verbs simply add **-시-**: **가다** (gada) becomes **가시다** (gasida). A handful of very common words, though, are swapped for a completely different word. You'll hear them every time you visit a Korean family, and using them correctly instantly makes your Korean sound well brought up.

## Honorific verbs: raising the other person

These replace the normal verb when the subject is someone you respect.

| Normal | Honorific | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 먹다 (meokta [먹따]), 마시다 (masida) | **[드시다](/en/dictionary/?q=드시다)** (deusida) | to eat, to drink |
| 먹다 (meokta [먹따]) | **[잡수시다](/en/dictionary/?q=잡수시다)** (japssusida [잡쑤시다]) | to eat (very respectful) |
| 자다 (jada) | **[주무시다](/en/dictionary/?q=주무시다)** (jumusida) | to sleep |
| 있다 (itta [읻따]) | **[계시다](/en/dictionary/?q=계시다)** (gyesida) | to be (somewhere) |
| 말하다 (malhada) | **말씀하시다** (malsseumhasida) | to speak |
| 죽다 (jukta [죽따]) | **돌아가시다** (doragasida) | to pass away |

So you say **밥 먹었어?** (Bap meogeosseo?) to a friend, but **할머니, 진지 드셨어요?** (Halmeoni, jinji deusyeosseoyo?) to your grandmother. 잡수시다 is even more respectful and sounds slightly old-fashioned; you'll hear it mostly with grandparents: **진지 잡수셨어요?** (Jinji japssusyeosseoyo? [진지 잡쑤셔써요?])

At bedtime, **잘 자** (jal ja) – sleep well – becomes **안녕히 주무세요** (annyeonghi jumuseyo) for anyone older.

One detail about **있다**: use **계시다** when a person is somewhere, as in **사장님 계세요?** (Sajangnim gyeseyo?) – Is the boss in? But when someone *has* something, use **있으시다** (isseusida): **시간 있으세요?** (Sigan isseuseyo?) – Do you have time?

## Humble verbs: lowering yourself

The other half of the system works in reverse. When *you* do something for a respected person, you use a humble verb.

| Normal | Humble | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 주다 (juda) | **[드리다](/en/dictionary/?q=드리다)** (deurida) | to give |
| 묻다 (mutta [묻따]) | **[여쭙다](/en/dictionary/?q=여쭙다)** (yeojjupta [여쭙따]) | to ask |
| 보다 (boda) | **뵙다** (boepta [뵙따]) | to see, to meet |
| 데리다 (derida) | **모시다** (mosida) | to take, to accompany |

You already know some of these from set phrases: **처음 뵙겠습니다** (cheoeum boepgetseumnida) – "Nice to meet you" – and shop staff saying **뭐 드릴까요?** (Mwo deurilkkayo?) – "What can I get you?" To ask a teacher a question: **하나 여쭤봐도 될까요?** (Hana yeojjwobwado doelkkayo?) – May I ask you something?

## Honorific nouns

Some nouns change too:

- **집 → [댁](/en/dictionary/?q=댁)** (daek) – home
- **나이 → [연세](/en/dictionary/?q=연세)** (yeonse) – age
- **이름 → [성함](/en/dictionary/?q=성함)** (seongham) – name
- **생일 → [생신](/en/dictionary/?q=생신)** (saengsin) – birthday
- **밥 → 진지** (jinji) – meal
- **말 → 말씀** (malsseum) – words

**성함이 어떻게 되세요?** (Seonghami eotteoke doeseyo?) – "May I have your name?" – is what you'll hear at a hospital or hotel desk.

Even particles change: **이/가** becomes **께서** (kkeseo), and **에게** (to) becomes **께** (kke). **할아버지께서 주무세요** (Harabeojikkeseo jumuseyo) – Grandfather is sleeping.

## Two common mistakes

**Never use honorifics for yourself.** **제가 드실게요** is wrong; say **제가 먹을게요** (Jega meogeulgeyo) – I'll eat it. Honorifics raise *other* people, never the speaker.

**Don't honour things.** You may hear staff say **커피 나오셨습니다** – "Your coffee has (honourably) come out" – but this is considered a mistake, since coffee doesn't need respect. The correct form is **커피 나왔습니다** (Keopi nawatseumnida).

| 안녕히 주무세요 | annyeonghi jumuseyo | Good night (to an elder) |

| 여쭤봐도 될까요? | yeojjwobwado doelkkayo? | May I ask (you something)? |
| 성함이 어떻게 되세요? | seonghami eotteoke doeseyo? | May I have your name? |
| 연세가 어떻게 되세요? | yeonsega eotteoke doeseyo? | May I ask your age? |

Useful pattern: **할머니, 진지 드셨어요?** (Halmeoni, jinji deusyeosseoyo?) – Grandma, have you eaten? Swap in **할아버지** (harabeoji, grandfather) or **선생님** (seonsaengnim, teacher), and you have the warmest greeting in Korean family life.
