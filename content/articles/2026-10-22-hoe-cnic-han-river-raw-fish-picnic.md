For years, the classic Han River picnic in Seoul meant one thing: fried chicken and beer, or **치맥** (chimaek). This autumn a new menu is taking over the riverside mats. As reported by The Korea Times on October 21, 2026, young people are buying takeout sashimi and eating it in the parks along the river. The trend even has its own name: **회크닉** (hoekeunik), "hoe-cnic," from [회](/en/dictionary/?q=회) (hoe), raw fish, and **피크닉** (pikeunik), picnic.

## How a hoe-cnic works

The usual route starts at **노량진수산시장** (Noryangjin Susansijang), Seoul's big fish market. People choose their fish, have it sliced and packed to go, and then head to a riverside park such as Yeouido Hangang Park, Nodeulseom island or Yanghwa Hangang Park. Many add a cup of riverside instant noodles, and some rent mats, tables or even tents. Some market vendors now sell "hoe-cnic omakase" sets, and takeout raw shrimp is popular too.

The numbers behind it are striking. One seafood business told Asia Economy in late September that about 80% of its recent sales came from hoe-cnic customers, and that waits for on-site orders could reach an hour; it suggested ordering ahead online instead. LE DESK, which visited the market in early October, reported waits of more than an hour on Friday evenings and weekends, and vendors said they were stocking about 40% more flatfish, **광어** (gwangeo), and rockfish, **우럭** (ureok), than before. Customers get their fish in ice bags along with **초고추장** (chogochujang), a red chili dipping sauce.

## Who's going, and why

Noryangjin used to be a place where people in their 40s and 50s sat down to eat fish on the spot. The hoe-cnic boom is bringing in customers in their 20s and 30s. Social media is a big part of it: posts from riverside picnics are spreading fast, and the Noryangjin market hashtag alone had about 189,000 Instagram posts according to LE DESK.

There is also a health angle. Compared with fried chicken or pizza, raw fish is high in protein and low in fat, which fits the "healthy pleasure" mood many young Koreans like.

## Before you try it

Autumn is the season for it, but it does not last. Temperatures have dropped recently, and social media is full of picnics that went wrong: wind blowing slices away, noodles going cold, groups giving up in under an hour. Dress warmly and pick a sunny afternoon. The Korea Times also points out that soju or wine may feel warming but can actually lower your body temperature. And Seoul's Han River office has asked picnickers to separate their plastic containers and food waste before throwing them away, since all that packaging adds up.

## Why it matters for learners

회크닉 is a perfect example of how Korean builds new words by gluing a Korean syllable onto an English loanword, just like 치맥 (치킨 + 맥주). Once you know the pattern, you can guess the meaning of many new words in the news.
