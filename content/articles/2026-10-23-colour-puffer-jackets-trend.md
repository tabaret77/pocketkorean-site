For years, a Korean winter street looked like a sea of black and grey puffer jackets. This season, that is changing. As shoppers start buying winter coats, Korean fashion platforms and brands report a clear move towards bright colours – lemon, mint, purple and sky blue – in the **패딩** (paeding), the padded jacket that is a Korean winter staple. Segye Ilbo summed it up on October 23, 2026: the "winter = black puffer" formula has broken.

## What the numbers say

The clearest signs come from online shopping data for July to September 2026, compared with the same months a year earlier:

- On **Zigzag**, a fashion app run by Kakao Style, searches for "purple" rose 740%, searches for "lemon" grew more than eightfold, and "mint" more than fivefold.
- On **W Concept**, a women's fashion platform, the average transaction value of lemon, olive and purple items rose about 40%. The number of mint items on sale grew 130%, lemon 92% and olive 39%.

Brands are seeing it in sales too. New Balance, run in Korea by E-Land World, said sales of its puffers and down jackets rose 31% in the week of October 15–21 compared with the week before. Hankyung reported earlier this month that the sky-blue colour of a lightweight hooded down jacket from Musinsa Standard sold out in every size and outsold grey, and that Kolon's Henly Cotton added orange and beige to a parka that used to come only in black.

## Why now?

Korean media call it **컬러 코어** (keolleo koeo), "colour core": building your style around one favourite colour. Until recently, bold colour mostly showed up on accessories like bags, caps and shoes; now it has reached the biggest item in a winter wardrobe. Bright spring and summer shades are also moving into autumn and winter collections, so the old rule of "dark colours for winter" is fading.

An industry source told Hankyung that big "colour of the year" picks matter less than they used to, so no single colour dominates a season. Segye notes that black and grey puffers still sell well – shoppers simply have more choice, and more of them want a coat that shows their taste.

## Why it matters for learners

**패딩** is a classic piece of Konglish. It comes from the English word "padding," but in Korea it means the whole jacket. You will also hear **롱패딩** (rongpaeding), a long padded coat, and **경량 패딩** (gyeongnyang paeding), a light down jacket – note that 경량 is said [경냥].

Colours are easy to learn because many are borrowed: **민트** (minteu), **레몬** (remon), **퍼플** (peopeul). The native Korean versions are useful too: [보라색](/en/dictionary/?q=보라색) (borasaek), purple, and [노란색](/en/dictionary/?q=노란색) (noransaek), yellow. The opposite of all this colour is **무채색** (muchaesaek), "colourless" shades like black, white and grey.

A pattern for shopping: **다른 색깔도 있어요?** (Dareun saekkkaldo isseoyo?) – "Do you have it in other colours too?" Noun + **도** means "also, too"; you can name the colour instead: **보라색도 있어요?** (Borasaekdo isseoyo?) – "Do you have it in purple too?"
