This is a good week to put on a [한복](/en/dictionary/?q=한복) (hanbok). The 2026 Hanbok Culture Week started on Monday, October 19, and runs until Sunday, October 25, with exhibitions, workshops, fashion shows and discounts at more than 410 places in Korea and abroad. It is the ninth edition, organised by the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism with the Korea Craft and Design Foundation. This year's theme, as reported by Yonhap, is that hanbok is a cultural heritage that has lived alongside people's everyday lives.

## Hanbok Day at the National Museum

The week is built around **한복의 날** (hanbogui nal), Hanbok Day, which falls every year on October 21. This year's celebration took place on Wednesday at the open yard of the National Museum of Korea in Seoul's Yongsan District. Five hanbok brands showed their designs on the runway under the title **빛을 품은 한복** (bicheul pumeun hanbok), roughly "hanbok that holds light," and singer Kim Yoon-ah of the rock band Jaurim performed.

Awards went to people who keep hanbok alive. Professor emerita Kwon Young-sook of Pusan National University received the Hanbok Culture Merit Award, designer Lee Hye-mi of the brand Saimdang was named Hanbok Person of the Year, and Jung Hye-jin of Songhwa won the Young Hanbok Person award. The Human Resources Development Service of Korea was honoured for bringing in the most hanbok-style work uniforms.

## What you can do this week

- **Gyeongbokgung Palace**: a traditional hanbok exhibition and a campaign on how to wear hanbok properly – **한복 바르게 입기** (hanbok bareuge ipki [한복 바르게 입끼]).
- **Namsangol Hanok Village**: a "little scholar" entrance ceremony for children, based on the old ceremony for a crown prince entering school.
- **Jeonju and South Jeolla**: hanbok accessory making, a hanbok party and natural-dye workshops.
- **Abroad**: hanbok-wearing sessions and etiquette classes in Canada, Japan and Denmark.

The discounts are the part many visitors notice first. If you visit in hanbok, Lotte World gives 50% off admission and Seoul Sky 35% off, from October 19 through October 31. The National Gugak Center halves the price of its Saturday performance on October 24. Other museums, cultural venues and leisure spots around the country offer free entry or extras (as of October 2026; check the official Hanbok Culture Week site for each venue).

If you are in Seoul this weekend, the last two days of the week are a nice excuse to see the palaces in autumn colours in hanbok – and some of the deals last until the end of the month.

## Why it matters for learners

**한복** combines **한** (han), Korean, with **복** (bok), clothes – the same 복 you find in **교복** (gyobok), school uniform, and **근무복** (geunmubok), work uniform. Notice the sound change in **한복의 날**: 한복 + 의 is said [한보긔] or [한보게], so the ㄱ slides into the next syllable.

The verb for wearing clothes is [입다](/en/dictionary/?q=입다) (ipta [입따]). Korean is picky here: 입다 is for clothes on your body, **신다** (sintta [신따]) for shoes and socks, and **쓰다** (sseuda) for hats and glasses.

A pattern you can use at the ticket booth: **한복을 입으면 할인돼요?** (Hanbogeul ibeumyeon harindwaeyo?) – "Is there a discount if I wear hanbok?" Verb stem + **-(으)면** means "if"; **할인되다** (harindoeda) means "to be discounted."
