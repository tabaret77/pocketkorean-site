In English, you speak to your best friend and your boss with mostly the same grammar. In Korean, you don't. Every sentence carries a choice between **[존댓말](/en/dictionary/?q=존댓말)** (jondaenmal) – polite speech – and **[반말](/en/dictionary/?q=반말)** (banmal) – casual speech. Getting it right shows respect; getting it wrong can sound cold or rude. Here's how to choose, and how to switch when the time comes.

## What's the difference?

The clearest difference is at the end of the sentence. Compare three ways of saying "I'm going to eat":

- **먹을 거예요.** (Meogeul geoyeyo.) – polite (존댓말, -요 style)
- **먹을 겁니다.** (Meogeul geomnida.) – formal polite (-습니다 style)
- **먹을 거야.** (Meogeul geoya.) – casual (반말)

In most cases, you make 반말 simply by dropping the final **요**: **고마워요 → 고마워** (gomawo) – thanks, **괜찮아요 → 괜찮아** (gwaenchana) – it's okay. But a few words change completely:

| 존댓말 | 반말 | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 네 (ne) | 응 (eung) / 어 (eo) | yes |
| 아니요 (aniyo) | 아니 (ani) | no |
| 저 / 제가 (jeo / jega) | 나 / 내가 (na / naega) | I |
| 학생이에요 (haksaengieyo) | 학생이야 (haksaengiya) | (I) am a student |
| 친구예요 (chinguyeyo) | 친구야 (chinguya) | (it) is a friend |
| 안녕하세요 (annyeonghaseyo) | 안녕 (annyeong) | hello |

Notice that **-이에요/예요** (is/am) becomes **-이야/야** in 반말.

## When to use 존댓말

Use polite speech by default with:

- people you've just met, whatever their age
- anyone older than you, unless they've told you otherwise
- colleagues, teachers and customers
- shop and restaurant staff (and they'll use it with you)

For formal situations such as presentations, job interviews or speaking to a large group, the even more formal **-습니다** style is common: **감사합니다** (gamsahamnida), **반갑습니다** (bangapseumnida) – nice to meet you.

## When to use 반말

Casual speech is for people you're close to:

- friends of the same age
- younger siblings and children
- your partner
- younger people who have agreed to it

Koreans are very aware of age because it affects speech. That's why people sometimes ask **몇 년생이에요?** (Myeot nyeonsaengieyo?) – "What year were you born?" – soon after meeting. If you turn out to be the same age, **동갑** (donggap), switching to 반말 often comes quickly.

## How to switch

Here's the important part: **don't just start using 반말**. Switching is usually agreed out loud, and it's normally the older or more senior person who suggests it. Common ways to propose it:

- **말 놓을까요?** (Mal noeulkkayo?) – Shall we speak casually? (literally "Shall we put down our speech?")
- **말 편하게 하세요.** (Mal pyeonhage haseyo.) – Feel free to talk casually (to me). Said by an older person to a younger one.
- **우리 말 놓자.** (Uri mal nocha.) – Let's drop the formalities. Already casual, so used when you're nearly friends.

If you're younger and want to ask:

- **말 편하게 해도 돼요?** (Mal pyeonhage haedo dwaeyo?) – Is it okay if I speak casually?

Even after an older friend says **편하게 해** (pyeonhage hae) – "be comfortable" – some younger Koreans keep a mix, such as calling them **언니** (eonni) or **형** (hyeong) while speaking casually. That's normal.

## Common traps

**Avoid 너 with people you don't know well.** **너** (neo) means "you" in 반말, but Korean generally avoids "you" altogether. With acquaintances, use their name plus **씨** (ssi), like **민지 씨** (Minji ssi), or a title such as **선생님** (seonsaengnim) – teacher.

**Don't use 반말 with service staff.** Speaking casually to a waiter or taxi driver you don't know sounds rude, even if they're younger than you.

**Politeness isn't only 요.** To show extra respect to someone older, Koreans add **-시-** to verbs: **가세요** (gaseyo) – you go (respectful), **드세요** (deuseyo) – please eat. You can use these even after switching to 반말 with peers, when talking about elders.

**Switching back can hurt.** If a friend suddenly goes back to 존댓말 with you, it can signal distance or anger. In dramas, a cold **알겠어요** (algesseoyo) – "I understand" – from someone who usually speaks casually is a clear sign something is wrong.

## Korean you can use

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 말 놓을까요? | mal noeulkkayo? | Shall we speak casually? |
| 말 편하게 하세요 | mal pyeonhage haseyo | Feel free to speak casually |
| 말 편하게 해도 돼요? | mal pyeonhage haedo dwaeyo? | May I speak casually? |
| 몇 년생이에요? | myeot nyeonsaengieyo? | What year were you born? |
| 동갑 | donggap | the same age |
| 응 / 아니 | eung / ani | yes / no (casual) |

When in doubt, stay polite. Nobody minds being spoken to with 존댓말, and when someone is ready to get closer, they'll let you know.
