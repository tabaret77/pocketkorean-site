Late autumn in Korea brings dry air, chilly mornings and the first wave of colds. Getting help is easy: pharmacies, **[약국](/en/dictionary/?q=약국)** (yakkuk [약꾹]), are on almost every street, marked with a big green or red **약** (yak), "medicine," and small neighbourhood clinics see walk-in patients without an appointment. A few short phrases will get you most of the way. All of these use the polite **-요** (-yo) ending.

## The one pattern you need

**[body part] + 이/가 아파요.** – My [body part] hurts.

- **머리가 아파요.** (meoriga apayo.) – I have a headache.
- **목이 아파요.** (mogi apayo.) – I have a sore throat.
- **배가 아파요.** (baega apayo.) – I have a stomach ache.

Use **가** (ga) after a vowel and **이** (i) after a consonant. On its own, **[아파요](/en/dictionary/?q=아프다)** (apayo) means "it hurts" or "I'm sick."

## At the pharmacy

1. **감기약 있어요?** (gamgiyak isseoyo?) – Do you have cold medicine?
2. **열이 나요.** (yeori nayo.) – I have a fever.
3. **기침이 나요.** (gichimi nayo.) – I have a cough.
4. **콧물이 나요.** (konmuri nayo.) – I have a runny nose.
5. **소화가 안 돼요.** (sohwaga an dwaeyo.) – I have indigestion.
6. **어제부터요.** (eojebuteoyo.) – Since yesterday.

The pharmacist will probably ask **어디가 아프세요?** (eodiga apeuseyo?) – "Where does it hurt?" – and how to take it: **하루에 세 번, 식후에 드세요** (harue se beon, sikue deuseyo), "three times a day, after meals." If you have an allergy, say **[thing] 알레르기가 있어요** (allereugiga isseoyo), "I'm allergic to [thing]."

When pharmacies are closed, convenience stores sell a short list of basic medicines, such as painkillers, fever reducers and digestion aids. As of October 2026, 11 products are available this way, and the government is discussing adding more.

## At a clinic or hospital

For a cold or a minor injury, Koreans go to a small clinic, **[의원](/en/dictionary/?q=의원)** (uiwon), often named after its speciality: **내과** (naegwa) for internal medicine, **이비인후과** (ibiinhugwa) for ear, nose and throat. Larger hospitals, **[병원](/en/dictionary/?q=병원)** (byeongwon), are for serious cases.

7. **진료 받고 싶어요.** (jillyo batko sipeoyo. [질료 받꼬 시퍼요.]) – I'd like to see a doctor.
8. **처음 왔어요.** (cheoeum wasseoyo.) – It's my first visit. *Reception will ask for your passport or residence card.*
9. **영어 하는 의사 선생님 있어요?** (yeongeo haneun uisa seonsaengnim isseoyo?) – Is there a doctor who speaks English?
10. **여기가 아파요.** (yeogiga apayo.) – It hurts here. *Point to the spot.*
11. **보험이 없어요.** (boheomi eopsseoyo. [보허미 업써요.]) – I don't have (Korean) insurance.
12. **처방전 주세요.** (cheobangjeon juseyo.) – Could I have the prescription, please? *Clinics write a prescription, **[처방전](/en/dictionary/?q=처방전)** (cheobangjeon), and you take it to a pharmacy, often in the same building.*
13. **진단서 받을 수 있어요?** (jindanseo badeul su isseoyo?) – Can I get a medical certificate? *Useful for travel insurance claims.*

## In an emergency

- **119**: ambulance and fire.
- **112**: police.
- **1330**: the Korea Travel Hotline run by the Korea Tourism Organization, with help in English and other languages. It can also join a three-way call with emergency services.

The key phrase: **도와주세요!** (dowajuseyo!) – Help! Then **구급차 불러 주세요** (gugeupcha bulleo juseyo), "Please call an ambulance."
