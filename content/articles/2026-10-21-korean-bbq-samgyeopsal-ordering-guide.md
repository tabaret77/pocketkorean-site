A Korean barbecue restaurant is loud, smoky and friendly, and it can be confusing the first time. There's a grill in the middle of the table, a small menu on the wall, and staff moving fast. Here's how a typical **삼겹살** (samgyeopssal [삼겹쌀]) – pork belly – dinner works, and what to say at each step.

## Step 1: Sit down and count people

When you walk in, staff will often ask **몇 분이세요?** (myeot buniseyo?) – "How many people?" Answer with a number and **명** (myeong), the counter for people: **두 명이요** (du myeong-iyo) – "Two people." Hold up fingers too; it helps.

Many places seat you at a table with a built-in grill. Some ask you to put coats in a bag or a bench seat with storage, so your clothes don't smell of smoke.

## Step 2: Order by the portion

Meat is ordered in portions called **인분** (inbun), meaning "portion for one person." The usual way to order is meat + number of portions + **주세요** (juseyo), "please give me":

- **삼겹살 2인분 주세요.** (samgyeopsal iinbun juseyo.) – Two portions of pork belly, please.

Many barbecue restaurants have a **minimum of two portions** for the first order, even for one diner. A common pattern is to order one portion per person to start, then add more later.

Other popular cuts:

- **목살** (mokssal [목쌀]) – pork neck/shoulder, a bit leaner
- **항정살** (hangjeongsal) – pork jowl, rich and chewy
- **갈비** (galbi) – ribs, often marinated

To call staff, say **저기요!** (jeogiyo!) – "Excuse me!" – or press the call button on the table if there is one.

## Step 3: Banchan and the lettuce basket

Before the meat arrives, you get **반찬** (banchan), small side dishes, plus lettuce, garlic, green chilli, and dipping sauces such as **쌈장** (ssamjang), a thick soybean-chilli paste. Side dishes are usually refillable at no extra charge. Many restaurants now have a **셀프바** (selpeuba) – self-service bar – where you refill them yourself.

If you'd rather ask: **상추 좀 더 주세요.** (sangchu jom deo juseyo.) – "More lettuce, please."

## Step 4: Who grills?

It depends on the restaurant. At some places staff grill and cut the meat for you; at many casual places, the diners do it. If no one comes, it's your job. Use the **집게** (jipke [집께]), tongs, to turn the meat and the **가위** (gawi), scissors, to cut it into bite-sized pieces once it's browned on both sides.

Not sure if it's done? Ask **이거 다 익었어요?** (igeo da igeosseoyo?) – "Is this cooked?" When the grill plate gets black and sticky, ask **불판 좀 갈아 주세요.** (bulpan jom gara juseyo.) – "Could you change the grill plate?"

## Step 5: Make a ssam

**쌈** (ssam) means "wrap." Take a lettuce leaf in your palm, add a piece of meat, a dab of ssamjang, maybe garlic or kimchi, fold it, and eat it **in one bite**. Biting a wrap in half is messy and not really the done thing.

## Drinking manners

Barbecue often comes with **소주** (soju) or **맥주** (maekjju [맥쭈]), beer. A few customs:

- Don't pour your own glass; pour for others, and they'll pour for you.
- When an older person pours for you, hold your glass with **two hands**.
- When drinking with someone much older, people often turn their head slightly away as they drink.
- The toast is **건배!** (geonbae!) – "Cheers!"

Nobody expects visitors to get all of this right, but trying is always appreciated.

## Step 6: Finish like a local

Koreans often finish a barbecue meal with something starchy. Try **된장찌개** (doenjang-jjigae), soybean paste stew, with **공깃밥** (gonggitpap [공긷빱]), a bowl of rice, or **볶음밥** (bokkeumbap), fried rice made on the grill with the leftovers. Cold noodles, **냉면** (naengmyeon), are another favourite.

## Step 7: Pay at the counter

In most Korean restaurants, you pay at the counter by the door, not at the table. Get up and say **계산할게요.** (gyesanhalgeyo.) – "I'd like to pay." Tipping is not expected. On the way out, **잘 먹었습니다** (jal meogeotseumnida) – "I ate well" – is a lovely thank-you. (Before eating, say **잘 먹겠습니다**, jal meokgetseumnida.)

With these lines and a pair of tongs, you're ready for your first samgyeopsal night.
