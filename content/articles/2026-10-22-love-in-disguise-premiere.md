tvN has a new Monday–Tuesday drama. **Love in Disguise**, starring Yim Si-wan and Seol In-ah, premiered on Monday, October 19, 2026, with an average nationwide rating of 5.6%, according to Nielsen Korea. The second episode on Tuesday, October 20, dipped to 4.7%, but it still ranked first in its time slot across all cable channels.

## A solid start on a busy weeknight

The drama took over the 8:50 p.m. KST slot from *The Ordinary Jackpot*, which signed off with its own best result of 6.2%. Opening above 5% on a weeknight is a good sign for a cable drama, and the small drop on day two is common: many viewers sample a premiere and then decide whether to stay. What matters now is whether the numbers climb again over the next weeks.

The same evening, ENA's *Between Steps* (Choo Young-woo, Kim So-hyun) reached its highest rating so far, about 2.4%, as it headed into the second half of its run. Outside Korea, *Love in Disguise* streams weekly on Amazon Prime Video (as of October 2026).

## A detective in disguise

The Korean title is **나의 유죄인간** (Naui yujoe ingan), roughly "my guilty one." Yim Si-wan plays Yoon Yi-jun, an arrogant [재벌](/en/dictionary/?q=재벌) (jaebeol) heir – the son of a huge family-run business – who becomes a murder **용의자** (yonguija), a suspect. Seol In-ah plays Kang Jae-hee, a former special forces officer turned [형사](/en/dictionary/?q=형사) (hyeongsa), a police detective. To investigate him, she goes undercover at his side as his personal **비서** (biseo), secretary, dressed as a man. The press in Korea describes her as a **남장** (namjang) detective – someone disguised as a man.

The series is based on a web novel, and it mixes romance with action, mystery and comedy. Seol In-ah cut her hair short for the role. Kim Jung-hyun and Yeonwoo play a second couple. The director is Park Dan-hee, and the script is by Moon Soo-jung.

## Why it matters for learners

The title is a little word game. [유죄](/en/dictionary/?q=유죄) (yujoe) means "guilty" in a court verdict; its opposite is **무죄** (mujoe), "not guilty." Put **인간** (ingan) – human, person – after it and you get "a guilty person," but in casual Korean **유죄** is also a playful compliment for someone so attractive it should be a crime. So the title hints at both the murder case and the romance.

Crime dramas like this are also great for police and courtroom vocabulary. Listen for **수사** (susa), investigation, and **잠입 수사** (jamip susa), undercover investigation. Note the sound change: 잠입 is written jam-ip but said [자밉], so the m slides into the next syllable.

A pattern you will hear in any disguise story: **남자인 척해요** (namja-in cheokaeyo) – "(She) pretends to be a man." Noun + **인 척하다** means "to pretend to be …"; with a verb it becomes **-는 척하다**, as in **모르는 척해요** (moreuneun cheokaeyo), "pretends not to know."
