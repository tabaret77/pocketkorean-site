Korea's high-speed rail has had a big autumn. Until this summer there were two separate brands: **KTX**, run by Korail mostly from Seoul Station, and **SRT**, run by a separate company from Suseo Station in southern Seoul. Each had its own app, its own fares and its own trains. On 1 September 2026 they were merged, and now all high-speed trains run as KTX. Then, on 1 October, the booking window doubled. Here's what that means for your trip, as of October 2026.

## What changed in September

- **One brand.** The SRT name has gone. Trains from both **서울역** (Seoul-yeok), Seoul Station, and **수서역** (Suseo-yeok), Suseo Station, are now sold as KTX.
- **Lower fares.** KTX fares were cut by an average of about 10% to match the old SRT level. A standard Seoul–Busan ticket fell from 59,800 won to 54,400 won, according to Korail's announcement.
- **One app.** Korail launched a new app, **코레일+** (koreil peulleoseu), Korail Plus, which lists trains from Seoul, Yongsan and Suseo stations together. The old SRT app still works for now, but only for non-member bookings.
- **More seats.** Korail says the merger adds around 16,000 seats a day, with the biggest increase at Suseo.
- **Mileage.** The 5% mileage reward on Korail memberships now applies to trains to and from Suseo too.

If you're staying in Gangnam or southern Seoul, Suseo is often the easier station. If you're near Myeong-dong, Hongdae or the airport rail line, Seoul Station is usually closer.

## Book up to two months ahead

From 1 October 2026, train tickets – **승차권** (seungchagwon) – can be booked two months before departure instead of one, according to the transport ministry and Korail. The change was made partly for overseas visitors, who usually book flights months ahead: foreign passengers took 6.69 million rail trips between January and August 2026. The ministry also said it would add French, German and Mongolian to the foreign-visitor booking site and develop payment by WeChat Pay and Alipay.

Weekend trains on popular routes, especially Friday evening and Sunday afternoon, still sell out. If you know your dates, book early.

## How to book, step by step

1. **Choose where to book.** Visitors can use Korail's English booking site for foreign passengers (Let's Korail) or the Korail+ app.
2. **Search** your departure station, **출발** (chulbal), and arrival station, **도착** (dochak), and the date.
3. **Pick a seat class.** **일반실** (ilbansil) is standard class; **특실** (teuksil) is first class, with wider seats. When seats sell out, **입석** (ipseok), standing tickets, may be offered.
4. **Pay and save the ticket** on your phone. No printing is needed; staff check tickets on board.
5. **Find your car.** Your ticket shows a car number, **호차** (hocha), and a seat number. Platforms are marked with car numbers, so stand at the right spot before the train arrives.

Need to change plans? Under the new rules reported in August, you can switch to a train within seven days of your original date, up to 30 minutes before departure, without a fee. Check the conditions when you book.

## At the station

Departure boards show the train number, destination and platform, **타는 곳** (taneun got). Trains leave exactly on time, and doors close quickly. If you're unsure, show your ticket to staff and ask **몇 번 타는 곳이에요?** (myeot beon taneun gosieyo?) – "Which platform is it?"
