Today, October 24, is a brand-new date on the Korean calendar: the first **한식의 날** (hansigui nal), or Hansik Day. [한식](/en/dictionary/?q=한식) (hansik) means Korean food, and from this year the country celebrates it with an official commemorative day, a **법정기념일** (beopjeong ginyeomil [법쩡기념일]). The first ceremony was held on Friday, October 23, at the National Museum of Korea in Seoul, and a three-day food festival is running there until Sunday, October 25.

## Why October 24?

The day was created by an amendment to the Hansik Promotion Act, which the National Assembly passed on June 18. The same change gives national and local governments a legal basis for events, education and promotion around Korean food, and the week that starts on October 24 is now **한식 주간** (hansik jugan), Hansik Week.

The date has a meaning. According to the government, the 10 of October stands for completion and harmony, and the 24 stands for the **24절기** (isipsa jeolgi), the 24 solar terms of the traditional calendar that have long guided farming in Korea. The day is meant to tie Korean food to the seasons and the land it comes from.

## The first ceremony

The Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs and the Ministry of Foreign Affairs hosted the ceremony together. About 300 people came, including diplomats from 15 countries, people from the food industry, academics and **식품명인** (sikpum myeongin), certified master food artisans who keep traditional techniques alive.

The government presented four directions for Korean food:

- keeping fermented foods such as kimchi and **장** (jang), Korea's soybean-based sauces and pastes, alive and showing their scientific value;
- using AI and food technology to bring new ideas into Korean cooking;
- food tourism that links regional food culture with travel;
- stronger Korean restaurants abroad, and training people to work in them.

Six people and companies received ministerial citations. As reported by The Korea Times, they included the Venerable Seonjae, a master of Buddhist temple food, chef Lee Jun of the Michelin-starred Seoul restaurant Soigné, cartoonist Huh Young-man, creator of the food comic *Sikgaek*, and the sauce maker Sempio Foods. The foreign ministry also called Korean food a diplomatic asset and said it is expanding "K-food diplomacy" through more than 170 missions abroad.

## Hansik Festa this weekend

If you are in Seoul, the **2026 한식대축제** (hansik dae-chukje [한식대축쩨]), or Hansik Festa, runs from October 23 to 25 around **거울못** (geoulmot), the Mirror Pond at the National Museum of Korea in Yongsan. The programme includes cooking demonstrations by master artisans and star chefs, cooking classes, a Korean food quiz called **한식 골든벨** (hansik goldeunbel), pop-up stores from Korean restaurants and food companies, and a flea market for ingredients and traditional crafts. A conference on Korean food and dining trends, with chefs and researchers, is also part of the festival. Check the organisers' announcements for times before you go (as of October 2026).

## Why it matters for learners

Food is one of the easiest ways into Korean. **한식** is built like **한복** (hanbok): **한** (han) means Korean, and **식** (sik) means food or eating, as in **식당** (sikttang [식땅]), restaurant, and **식사** (sikssa [식싸]), a meal. Notice the sound changes: 한식 + 의 is said [한시긔] or [한시게], with the ㄱ moving into the next syllable, and after a ㄱ final sound the next ㄷ or ㅅ becomes tense.

Other words with 식 you will see on menus: **양식** (yangsik), Western food, **중식** (jungsik), Chinese food, and **일식** (ilssik [일씩]), Japanese food.

A pattern for talking about food: **제일 좋아하는 한식은 비빔밥이에요** (jeil joahaneun hansigeun bibimbabieyo [제일 조아하는 한시근 비빔바비에요]) – "My favourite Korean food is bibimbap." Verb stem + **-는** turns 좋아하다 (joahada), to like, into "that I like," and you can swap in any dish you love.
