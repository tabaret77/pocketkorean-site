The last question of the TOPIK II writing section, question 54, scares a lot of learners. It is a 600–700 character opinion essay on a social topic, and it is worth more than any other single question on the test. The good news: it follows the same pattern every time, so you can plan ahead. Here is how it works, as of October 2026.

## What question 54 asks for

The writing section (**[쓰기](/en/dictionary/?q=쓰기)**, sseugi) has four questions and 100 points, and you get 50 minutes for all of them. Questions 51 and 52 ask you to fill in sentences (10 points each), question 53 is a short 200–300 character text describing a chart (30 points), and question 54 is the long essay, worth 50 points – half of your writing score.

Question 54 gives you a topic, for example the role of technology in daily life or how to deal with a social problem, plus two or three guiding questions. Your essay must answer all of them. The length is 600–700 characters, spaces included, written on a grid answer sheet called **원고지** (wongoji), where each square holds one character.

Examiners look at three things:

- **Content and task** – did you answer every guiding question with relevant, specific ideas?
- **Structure** – is the essay clearly organised, with a logical flow and good linking words?
- **Language** – is the grammar and vocabulary accurate, varied and at an advanced level, in a style that fits a formal essay?

## The three-part plan

The easiest way to hit all the criteria is to turn the guiding questions into paragraphs:

1. **서론** (seoron) – introduction: two or three sentences that introduce the topic and say what you will discuss. Answer the first guiding question here or in the next paragraph.
2. **본론** (bollon) – body: one paragraph per guiding question. State a point, then give a reason (**[근거](/en/dictionary/?q=근거)**, geungeo) or an example.
3. **결론** (gyeollon) – conclusion: restate your **[주장](/en/dictionary/?q=주장)** (jujang) – your position – in fresh words and end with a suggestion.

A balanced split is roughly 120 characters for the introduction, 400 for the body and 120 for the conclusion. Before you start writing, spend two or three minutes jotting down keywords for each paragraph in the margin. A plan stops you from running out of ideas at 400 characters.

## Write in the essay style, not 해요

This is the most common mistake. Question 54 should be written in the plain written style ending in **-ㄴ다/는다** (-nda/-neunda), not in the polite **-아요/어요** you use in conversation. Compare:

- Conversation: 기술이 중요해요. (Gisuri jungyohaeyo.)
- Essay: 기술은 중요하다. (Gisureun jungyohada.) – Technology is important.

Also avoid casual words like **그래서** (geuraeseo) and **진짜** (jinjja); use **따라서** (ttaraseo) – therefore – and **매우** (maeu) – very – instead.
## Useful building blocks

Learn a handful of phrases you can drop into any topic:

- **최근 ~이/가 사회적 문제가 되고 있다.** – Recently, ~ has become a social issue.
- **첫째, … 둘째, …** – First, … Second, …
- **왜냐하면 ~기 때문이다.** – This is because ~.
- **반면에** – on the other hand
- **이를 위해서는 ~(으)ㄹ 필요가 있다.** – To do this, it is necessary to ~.

## Timing and practice

With 50 minutes for the whole section, many test-takers finish 51 and 52 in about 10 minutes, give 53 about 15 minutes, and keep at least 25 minutes for 54. Some students write 54 before 53 so the bigger question never gets squeezed. Whichever order you choose, always attempt both essays: a missing answer scores zero.

For practice, take a past question 54 from the official site, set a 25-minute timer and write by hand on squared paper. Then count your characters and check three things: every guiding question answered, every sentence in -ㄴ다 style, and at least two linking words per paragraph. ## Korean for the essay

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 쓰기 | sseugi | writing (section) |
| 원고지 | wongoji | grid answer sheet |
| 서론 / 본론 / 결론 | seoron / bollon / gyeollon | introduction / body / conclusion |
| 주장 | jujang | claim, position |
| 근거 | geungeo | reason, evidence |
| 따라서 | ttaraseo | therefore |
| 반면에 | banmyeone | on the other hand |
| 필요하다 | piryohada | to be necessary |

**이를 위해서는 ~(으)ㄹ 필요가 있다.** (Ireul wihaeseoneun ~(eu)l piryoga itda.) – To achieve this, it is necessary to ~. Learn it once and you have a ready-made sentence for almost every conclusion.
