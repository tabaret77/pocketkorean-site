Sooner or later, a Korean will tell you **한국어 잘하시네요!** (Hangugeo jalhasineyo!) – "Your Korean is so good!" – often after you've said nothing more than 안녕하세요. Knowing how to receive that compliment gracefully is as useful as knowing how to give one. Here are the everyday phrases for both.

These are in polite speech (**해요체**, haeyoche), the safe choice with classmates, colleagues and new friends. Drop the **요** for close friends.

## Giving compliments

**잘했어요** (Jalhaesseoyo [잘해써요]) – "Well done." For a finished task, a good presentation, a game won. With **-네요** it becomes **잘하네요** (Jalhaneyo) – "You're good at this", said as you watch someone in action.

**멋있어요** (Meosisseoyo [머시써요]) – "That's cool / You look great." From **[멋있다](/en/dictionary/?q=멋있다)** (meositta [머싣따]). It works for people of any gender, clothes, views and even attitudes: **그 생각 멋있어요** (Geu saenggak meosisseoyo) – "That's a great way of thinking."

**예뻐요** (Yeppeoyo) – "It's pretty / You look pretty." From **[예쁘다](/en/dictionary/?q=예쁘다)** (yeppeuda). Commenting on someone's looks can feel personal, so a safer habit is to praise a thing: **가방 예뻐요** (Gabang yeppeoyo) – "Your bag is lovely."

**잘 어울려요** (Jal eoullyeoyo) – "It suits you." The most useful clothes compliment of all: **그 색 진짜 잘 어울려요** (Geu saek jinjja jal eoullyeoyo) – "That colour really suits you."

**대단해요** (Daedanhaeyo) – "That's amazing / Impressive." For effort and achievements: running a marathon, passing an exam, cooking for twelve people.

**부러워요** (Bureowoyo) – "I'm jealous / Lucky you." From **[부럽다](/en/dictionary/?q=부럽다)** (bureoptta [부럽따]). In Korean, saying you envy someone is a friendly compliment, not a complaint: **제주도 가요? 부러워요!** (Jejudo gayo? Bureowoyo!) – "You're going to Jeju? So jealous!"

To praise someone's skill, the pattern is noun + **잘하시네요**: **요리 잘하시네요** (Yori jalhasineyo) – "You're a great cook."

## Answering compliments

Korean manners prefer a little modesty, so a flat "Yes, I know" is rare. Common answers, from humble to happy:

- **아니에요** (Anieyo) – "Oh no, not at all." The reflex answer, often with a small wave of the hand.
- **아직 멀었어요** (Ajik meoreosseoyo [아직 머러써요]) – "I've still got a long way to go." The classic reply to praise for your Korean.
- **과찬이세요** (Gwachanieseyo) – "You're too kind." **과찬** (gwachan) means too much praise. It sounds polished and slightly formal: good with older people and at work.
- **감사합니다** (Gamsahamnida) – "Thank you." Perfectly fine and increasingly common, especially among younger people.
- **덕분이에요** (Deokbunieyo [덕뿌니에요]) – "It's thanks to you." Shares the credit, which makes it very warm. Fuller: **선생님 덕분이에요** (Seonsaengnim deokbunieyo) – "It's all thanks to you, teacher."

A natural combination is modesty plus thanks: **아니에요, 그래도 감사해요** (Anieyo, geuraedo gamsahaeyo) – "Oh, not really – but thank you."

## Two short scenes

**At a language exchange**

**A:** 한국어 진짜 잘하시네요! (Hangugeo jinjja jalhasineyo!) – Your Korean is really good!
**B:** 아니에요, 아직 멀었어요. (Anieyo, ajik meoreosseoyo.) – Oh no, I've still got a long way to go.

**At the office**

**A:** 오늘 발표 정말 대단했어요. (Oneul balpyo jeongmal daedanhaesseoyo. [대단해써요]) – Your presentation today was really impressive.
**B:** 과찬이세요. 팀장님 덕분이에요. (Gwachanieseyo. Timjangnim deokbunieyo.) – You're too kind. It's thanks to you.

Useful pattern: **노래 잘하시네요!** (Norae jalhasineyo!) – You sing so well! Put any skill before **잘하시네요**: **운전** (unjeon, driving), **춤** (chum, dancing), **영어** (yeongeo, English).
