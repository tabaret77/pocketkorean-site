A quiet hillside neighbourhood in northern Seoul has become one big open-air gallery. The first **성북아트위크** (Seongbuk Ateuwikeu), Seongbuk Art Week, opened on October 20, 2026 and runs until November 1. It is organised by the Seongbuk District Office, the Seongbuk Culture Foundation and the Seongbuk Museum of Art, and its theme is a single, very Korean word: [집](/en/dictionary/?q=집) (jip) – house, home.

## Art inside historic homes

Seongbuk-dong has long been where Korean writers and artists lived, and the festival uses those old houses as its stage. Contemporary works have been placed in 11 historic sites, alongside seven special exhibitions and linked shows at 22 museums, galleries and alternative spaces across the district. The full theme is **집: 이어진 숨결** (jip: ieojin sumgyeol), roughly "Home: a breath that carries on."

A few examples reported this week:

- At **심우장** (Simujang), the former home of Han Yong-un, Lee Woo-sung shows new takes on traditional Buddhist ox-herding pictures.
- At **성북동 별서** (Seongbuk-dong Byeolseo), a late-Joseon garden villa, photographer Han Sung-pil has wrapped a fire-damaged pavilion in a screen showing how it once looked. The garden opens only during the festival, and reservations are limited to 15 visitors a day.
- Beside the old city wall, **성곽** (seonggwak), Jung Hyun's "Standing Man" figures, made from used railway ties, look out over the city.
- At Samcheonggak, a historic residence, Yoo Na-ul shows 25 works, 10 of them new.

A loop bus connecting the main venues runs until October 24, and the festival also has a small art fair from October 22 to 25.

## A father, a son and one house

The centrepiece is **"Suh Se-ok, Do Ho Suh: Caress"** at the Seongbuk Museum of Art, and admission is free. It brings together the ink painter Suh Se-ok (1929–2020) and his son, the installation artist Do Ho Suh. The link is a real **한옥** (hanok), a traditional house, in Seongbuk-dong that the father built and the son grew up in. Do Ho Suh's rubbings recreate his father's "Longevity Painting" (**장생도**, jangsaengdo), and his thread installations echo his father's ink figures. This show stays open until December 20, long after the festival ends.

Nearby museums are joining in too: the Kansong Art Museum has a collection show until December 6, and the Korea University Museum holds a centenary exhibition for the colonial-era painter Byun Shi-ji.

## Why it matters for learners

집 is one of the first Korean words most people learn, and this festival shows how much it can hold: a building, a family, memories. You will also see it inside longer words: **한옥집** (hanokjip), a hanok house; **우리 집** (uri jip), literally "our house," which Koreans use for "my home"; and **집순이 / 집돌이** (jipsuni / jipdori), a homebody. If you visit, look for **무료** (muryo), free, and **예약** (yeyak), reservation, on the signs – some of the historic houses can only be entered with a booking.
