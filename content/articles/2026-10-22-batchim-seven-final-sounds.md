Say **옷** (ot) – clothes – and **꽃** (kkot) – flower – out loud, and you'll notice they end in the same "t" sound, even though one is written with ㅅ and the other with ㅊ. That's not a typo. Korean has many letters that can sit at the bottom of a syllable block, but when a word ends there, they collapse into just **seven sounds**. Learn those seven and a whole layer of Korean spelling suddenly makes sense.

## What batchim is

A **받침** (batchim) is the consonant at the bottom of a syllable block, as in 한 (han) or 국 (guk). At the start of a syllable, a consonant is "released" – you let the air out. At the end, Korean doesn't release it: your tongue or lips close and stop. A final ㄱ sounds like the "k" in "book" said without the little puff of air at the end. Because there's no release, the differences between, say, ㅅ, ㅈ and ㅊ disappear.

## The seven final sounds

Every batchim is pronounced as one of these seven: **ㄱ, ㄴ, ㄷ, ㄹ, ㅁ, ㅂ, ㅇ**.

| Final sound | Letters that make it | Example | Said as |
|---|---|---|---|
| ㄱ [k] | ㄱ, ㅋ, ㄲ | [부엌](/en/dictionary/?q=부엌) (bueok) – kitchen | [부억] |
| ㄴ [n] | ㄴ | 산 (san) – mountain | [산] |
| ㄷ [t] | ㄷ, ㅌ, ㅅ, ㅆ, ㅈ, ㅊ, ㅎ | [꽃](/en/dictionary/?q=꽃) (kkot) – flower | [꼳] |
| ㄹ [l] | ㄹ | 물 (mul) – water | [물] |
| ㅁ [m] | ㅁ | 밤 (bam) – night, chestnut | [밤] |
| ㅂ [p] | ㅂ, ㅍ | [앞](/en/dictionary/?q=앞) (ap) – front | [압] |
| ㅇ [ng] | ㅇ | 강 (gang) – river | [강] |

The big group is the ㄷ row: seven different letters all end in the same short "t". That's why **[옷](/en/dictionary/?q=옷)** (ot), **[낮](/en/dictionary/?q=낮)** (nat) – daytime – and **꽃** (kkot) rhyme, and why **낮** (daytime) and **낫** (sickle) sound the same on their own. Similarly, **[밖](/en/dictionary/?q=밖)** (bak) – outside – sounds just like **박** (bak).

Official romanization follows the sound, not the letter, so final ㄱ/ㄷ/ㅂ groups are written k, t and p: 부엌 is "bueok", 꽃 "kkot", 앞 "ap".

## Double batchim

Some syllables have two consonants at the bottom, like **닭** or **값**. On their own, you pronounce only one of them – usually the first:

- **[값](/en/dictionary/?q=값)** (gap) – price → [갑]
- **앉다** (antta [안따]) – to sit → [안따]
- **없다** (eopta [업따]) – not to have → [업따]

A few common ones use the second letter instead, especially ㄺ and ㄻ:

- **[닭](/en/dictionary/?q=닭)** (dak) – chicken → [닥]
- **읽다** (ikta [익따]) – to read → [익따]
- **삶** (sam) – life → [삼]

Don't try to memorise every combination at once; learn the pronunciation together with each word.

## When a vowel follows: the letter comes back

Here's the twist. When the next syllable starts with a vowel (that is, with silent ㅇ), the batchim isn't stuck at the end any more. It moves over and is pronounced with its **original** sound. This linking is called **연음** (yeoneum).

- 옷 [옫] but **옷이** (osi) → [오시] – the ㅅ comes back
- 꽃 [꼳] but **꽃을** (kkocheul) → [꼬츨] – the ㅊ comes back
- 부엌 [부억] but **부엌에** (bueoke) → [부어케]
- 닭 [닥] but **닭이** (dalgi) → [달기] – both letters are heard
- 값 [갑] but **값이** (gapssi) → [갑씨]

This is why spelling keeps the "real" letter: it shows up as soon as a particle or ending is added. (ㅎ is the exception: it usually goes silent, as in **좋아요** [조아요].)

## Korean you can use

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 받침 | batchim | final consonant |
| [발음](/en/dictionary/?q=발음) | bareum | pronunciation |
| 연음 | yeoneum | linking a final consonant to the next vowel |
| 옷 / 옷이 | ot / osi | clothes / clothes (subject) |
| 꽃 / 꽃을 | kkot / kkocheul | flower / flower (object) |
| 닭 / 닭이 | dak / dalgi | chicken / chicken (subject) |
| 값 | gap | price |
| 발음이 어려워요 | bareumi eoryeowoyo | The pronunciation is difficult |

**꽃은 [꼳]으로 발음해요.** (Kkocheun [kkot]-euro bareumhaeyo.) – 꽃 is pronounced "kkot". Use the pattern "___은/는 [___](으)로 발음해요" to ask a teacher or friend to check your batchim.
