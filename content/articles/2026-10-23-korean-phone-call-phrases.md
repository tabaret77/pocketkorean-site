Many learners can chat in a café but freeze when the phone rings. A delivery driver who can't find your door, a clinic confirming an appointment, a restaurant you want to book for a year-end dinner: sooner or later you'll have to talk on the phone in Korean. The good news is that phone Korean is very formulaic. Learn a dozen lines and you can handle most calls.

These phrases are polite (**해요체**, haeyoche) because most calls you'll make are to strangers or businesses. Casual versions for friends are at the end.

## Answering and saying who you are

- **[여보세요](/en/dictionary/?q=여보세요)?** (Yeoboseyo?) – Hello? This is only for the phone (and for calling out to someone), never for saying hello in person.
- **네, 여보세요.** (Ne, yeoboseyo.) – Yes, hello? A natural way to pick up.
- **저 ○○인데요.** (Jeo ○○-indeyo.) – It's ○○. The ending **-인데요** sets up what comes next, like "This is ○○, and…"
- **실례지만 누구세요?** (Sillyejiman nuguseyo?) – Excuse me, who's calling? 실례 is pronounced **[실례]**, sillye.

## Asking for someone, checking it's a good time

- **○○ 씨 계세요?** (○○ ssi gyeseyo?) – Is ○○ there? **계시다** (gyesida) is the honorific of 있다.
- **지금 통화 괜찮으세요?** (Jigeum tonghwa gwaenchaneuseyo?) – Is now a good time to talk? **[통화](/en/dictionary/?q=통화)** (tonghwa) means a phone call or conversation. A thoughtful line to start with when you call someone you know.
- **지금 자리에 안 계세요.** (Jigeum jarie an gyeseyo.) – They're not at their desk right now. You'll hear this when calling an office.

## When you didn't catch it

This is the most important section. Don't pretend you understood.

- **잘 안 들려요.** (Jal an deullyeoyo.) – I can't hear you well. **들리다** (deullida) means "to be heard".
- **다시 한번 말씀해 주시겠어요?** (Dasi hanbeon malsseumhae jusigesseoyo?) – Could you say that again, please?
- **조금 천천히 말씀해 주세요.** (Jogeum cheoncheonhi malsseumhae juseyo.) – Please speak a little more slowly.
- **문자로 보내 주실 수 있어요?** (Munjaro bonae jusil su isseoyo?) – Could you send that by text? A lifesaver for addresses, times and numbers.

**말씀** (malsseum) is the respectful word for someone's words, so **말씀해 주세요** is a polite "please tell me".

## Waiting, messages and calling back

- **잠시만 기다려 주세요.** (Jamsiman gidaryeo juseyo.) – Please hold on a moment.
- **메모 남겨 드릴까요?** (Memo namgyeo deurilkkayo?) – Shall I take a message?
- **나중에 다시 전화드릴게요.** (Najunge dasi jeonhwadeurilgeyo.) – I'll call you back later. **전화드리다** (jeonhwadeurida) is the humble form of 전화하다, used when you call someone you respect.
- **전화 잘못 거셨어요.** (Jeonhwa jalmot geosyeosseoyo.) – You've got the wrong number.

## With delivery drivers

A typical call when a package arrives:

**Driver:** 택배인데요, 집에 계세요? (Taekbaeindeyo, jibe gyeseyo?) – It's a delivery. Are you home?
**You:** 아니요, 문 앞에 놔 주세요. (Aniyo, mun ape nwa juseyo.) – No, please leave it at the door.
**Driver:** 네, 알겠습니다. (Ne, algetseumnida.) – Okay, got it.

## Hanging up

Korean phone goodbyes are a little different from face-to-face ones:

- **네, 들어가세요.** (Ne, deureogaseyo.) – Literally "go on in", it's a very common polite goodbye on the phone, like "take care".
- **감사합니다. 수고하세요.** (Gamsahamnida. Sugohaseyo.) – Thank you, have a good day. Good for calls to shops and offices.
- **끊을게요.** (Kkeuneulgeyo.) – I'll hang up now. Said **[끄늘께요]**, from **끊다** (kkeunta), to cut or hang up.

## With friends

Between friends it's much shorter: **어, 왜?** (Eo, wae?) – Yeah, what's up? / **지금 통화 돼?** (Jigeum tonghwa dwae?) – Can you talk now? / **이따 전화할게** (Itta jeonhwahalge) – I'll call you later. / **응, 끊어** (Eung, kkeuneo) – Okay, bye.

Useful pattern: **천천히 말씀해 주세요.** (Cheoncheonhi malsseumhae juseyo.) – Please speak slowly. Swap the adverb for **크게** (keuge, loudly) or **다시** (dasi, again) to fix almost any phone problem.
