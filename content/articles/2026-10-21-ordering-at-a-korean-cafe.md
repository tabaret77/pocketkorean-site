Korea runs on coffee. There seems to be a café on every corner, from big chains to tiny independent spots in old hanok houses. Ordering is a perfect first real-life Korean conversation: it's short, predictable and the staff are used to it. Here's what you'll hear and what to say back.

## The magic formula: [item] + [number] + 주세요

Almost every order follows the same pattern: what you want, how many, and **[주세요](/en/dictionary/?q=주다)** (juseyo) – please give me.

- **아메리카노 한 잔 주세요.** (Amerikano han jan juseyo.) – One Americano, please.
- **라떼 두 잔 주세요.** (Ratte du jan juseyo.) – Two lattes, please.

**잔** (jan) is the counter for cups and glasses. Use native Korean numbers with it: **한 잔** (han jan) – one cup, **두 잔** (du jan) – two, **세 잔** (se jan) – three.

## Hot or iced?

Staff will often ask:

- **따뜻한 거로 드릴까요, 아이스로 드릴까요?** (Ttatteutan georo deurilkkayo, aiseuro deurilkkayo?) – Would you like it hot or iced?

Answer with **따뜻한 거요** (ttatteutan geoyo) – a hot one, please, or **아이스요** (aiseuyo) – iced, please. Or say it up front: **아이스 아메리카노 한 잔 주세요.**

Iced Americano is so popular that it has its own nickname: **아아** (a-a), short for 아이스 아메리카노. The hot version is **뜨아** (tteu-a), from 뜨거운 아메리카노. There's even a word for people who drink it iced all winter: **얼죽아** (eoljuga), from "얼어 죽어도 아이스 아메리카노" – "iced Americano even if I freeze to death."

## Size

Sizes vary by chain, and many smaller cafés only have one or two. If you're asked **사이즈는 어떻게 해 드릴까요?** (Saijeuneun eotteoke hae deurilkkayo?) – What size would you like? – point at the menu or say **작은 거요** (jageun geoyo) – the small one, or **큰 거요** (keun geoyo) – the large one.

## For here or to go?

This is the question that catches people out:

- **드시고 가세요?** (Deusigo gaseyo?) – Are you eating/drinking here? (literally "Will you have it and then go?")
- **매장에서 드세요, 가져가세요?** (Maejangeseo deuseyo, gajyeogaseyo?) – For here, or to take away?

Your answers:

- **먹고 갈게요.** (Meokgo galgeyo.) – I'll have it here.
- **가져갈게요.** (Gajyeogalgeyo.) – I'll take it away.
- **포장해 주세요.** (Pojanghae juseyo.) – Please wrap it to go. Common for food and drinks alike.

You'll also hear the English loanword **테이크아웃** (teikeuaut) – takeout.

## Customising your drink

- **샷 추가해 주세요.** (Syat chugahae juseyo.) – An extra shot, please.
- **연하게 해 주세요.** (Yeonhage hae juseyo.) – Make it weaker, please.
- **시럽 빼 주세요.** (Sireop ppae juseyo.) – No syrup, please.
- **얼음 조금만 넣어 주세요.** (Eoreum jogeumman neoeo juseyo.) – Just a little ice, please.
- **디카페인 있어요?** (Dikapein isseoyo?) – Do you have decaf?
- **우유를 두유로 바꿀 수 있어요?** (Uyureul duyuro bakkul su isseoyo?) – Can I switch the milk to soy milk?

**빼 주세요** (ppae juseyo) – please take it out – is extremely useful anywhere you order food.

## Paying

- **결제는 어떻게 하시겠어요?** (Gyeoljeneun eotteoke hasigesseoyo?) – How would you like to pay?
- **카드로 할게요.** (Kadeuro halgeyo.) – By card.
- **영수증 드릴까요?** (Yeongsujeung deurilkkayo?) – Would you like the receipt?
- **아니요, 괜찮아요.** (Aniyo, gwaenchanayo.) – No, thanks.

Many cafés now take orders through a **키오스크** (kioseukeu) – a touchscreen ordering machine – instead of at the counter. Some offer other languages, but the Korean buttons are worth learning: **매장** (maejang) – for here, **포장** (pojang) – to go, **결제** (gyeolje) – pay.

## Picking up your drink

Often you'll be handed a **진동벨** (jindongbel) – a vibrating buzzer – that goes off when your drink is ready. Other places call out your order number: **37번 고객님!** (Samsipchil-beon gogaengnim!) – Customer number 37! Before leaving, it's polite to say **감사합니다** (gamsahamnida) – thank you – or **잘 마실게요** (jal masilgeyo) – "I'll enjoy it."

## A full order, start to finish

- **Staff:** 주문하시겠어요? (Jumunhasigesseoyo?) – Ready to order?
- **You:** 아이스 아메리카노 한 잔 주세요. – One iced Americano, please.
- **Staff:** 드시고 가세요? – For here?
- **You:** 아니요, 가져갈게요. – No, to go.
- **Staff:** 결제 도와드릴게요. (Gyeolje dowadeurilgeyo.) – I'll take your payment.
- **You:** 카드로 할게요. – By card.

Start with the formula, add one customisation, and you'll be ordering like a local by your second visit.
