Every K-drama has its goodbyes. Some are tiny – two friends splitting up at a subway exit after dinner. Others are huge – a couple at the airport, or a final meeting in the rain before one of them leaves for good. The lines are short, but the choice of words tells you a lot: is this "see you tomorrow" or "see you never"?

As in most drama dialogue between close characters, these lines are casual (**반말**, banmal). Polite versions are in the table at the end.

## 1. 잘 가 / 잘 있어 – Bye (to the one leaving / staying)

Korean goodbyes depend on who is moving. The person staying says **잘 가** (jal ga) – "go well" – from **[가다](/en/dictionary/?q=가다)** (gada). The person leaving says **잘 있어** (jal isseo) – "stay well". In polite speech, these become **안녕히 가세요** (annyeonghi gaseyo) and **안녕히 계세요** (annyeonghi gyeseyo).

## 2. 조심히 가 – Get home safe

**조심히 가** (josimhi ga) is the warm version of 잘 가, said at night or in bad weather. **조심히** means "carefully". In a drama, it's often the first sign that someone cares more than they admit.

## 3. 도착하면 연락해 – Text me when you get there

**도착하면 연락해** (Dochakamyeon yeollakae [도차카면 열라캐]) – "Contact me when you arrive." **-(으)면** means "when/if", and **[연락하다](/en/dictionary/?q=연락하다)** (yeollakada) covers calling, texting and messaging. Real Korean friends say this all the time after a late night out.

## 4. 또 보자 / 다시 만나자 – See you again

**또 보자** (Tto boja) – "Let's see each other again" – is light and friendly. **다시 만나자** (Dasi mannaja) – "Let's meet again" – sounds heavier, as if the next meeting isn't certain. Dramas save it for long separations: 다시 만나자, 꼭 (kkok) – "We'll meet again, I promise."

## 5. 보고 싶을 거야 – I'll miss you

**보고 싶을 거야** (Bogo sipeul kkeoya [보고 시플 꺼야]) is the future version of "I miss you". The ending **-(으)ㄹ 거야** says what will happen, and in speech the ㄱ of 거 becomes tense: [꺼]. Politely: **보고 싶을 거예요** (bogo sipeul kkeoyeyo [보고 시플 꺼예요]).

## 6. 잘 지내 – Take care

**잘 지내** (Jal jinae) comes from **[지내다](/en/dictionary/?q=지내다)** (jinaeda) – to get along, to live. As a question, **잘 지내?** (Jal jinae?) means "How are you doing?" But as a goodbye, said slowly and without a smile, it often means "This is the end." Its stronger cousin: **잘 지내야 돼** (Jal jinaeya dwae) – "You have to be okay."

## 7. 행복해야 돼 – Be happy

**행복해야 돼** (Haengbokaeya dwae [행보캐야 돼]) is the classic line of someone who is letting go. **-아/어야 되다** means "must", so it's literally "You must be happy." When you hear it, expect tears.

## 8. 우리 헤어지자 – Let's break up

**우리 헤어지자** (Uri heeojija) is the breakup line. **헤어지다** (heeojida) means both "to part" after a meeting and "to break up". Context does the work: **여기서 헤어지자** (Yeogiseo heeojija) after dinner just means "Let's split up here."

## 9. 이게 마지막이야 – This is the last time

**이게 마지막이야** (Ige majimagiya [이게 마지마기야]) – "This is the last time" – closes the door. **[마지막](/en/dictionary/?q=마지막)** (majimak) means last. In happier scenes it's simply practical: **이게 마지막 버스야** (Ige majimak beoseuya [이게 마지막 뻐스야]) – "This is the last bus."

## A typical scene

**A:** 나 이제 가야 돼. (Na ije gaya dwae.) – I have to go now.
**B:** 조심히 가. 도착하면 연락해. (Josimhi ga. Dochakamyeon yeollakae.) – Get home safe. Text me when you're there.
**A:** 응, 잘 있어. 또 보자! (Eung, jal isseo. Tto boja!) – Okay, bye. See you again!

## Real-life note

Lines 1 to 4 are everyday Korean; use them freely with friends. Lines 6 to 9 are drama-heavy. Saying 행복해야 돼 to a classmate going home for the weekend would get a laugh.

## Korean you can use

| Korean | Romanization | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 잘 가 / 안녕히 가세요 | jal ga / annyeonghi gaseyo | Bye (to someone leaving) |
| 잘 있어 / 안녕히 계세요 | jal isseo / annyeonghi gyeseyo | Bye (to someone staying) |
| 조심히 가세요 | josimhi gaseyo | Get home safe (polite) |
| 도착하면 연락해요 | dochakamyeon yeollakaeyo [도차카면 열라캐요] | Let me know when you arrive (polite) |
| 또 봐요 | tto bwayo | See you again (polite) |
| 보고 싶을 거예요 | bogo sipeul kkeoyeyo [보고 시플 꺼예요] | I'll miss you (polite) |
| 잘 지내요 | jal jinaeyo | Take care (polite) |
| 행복해야 돼 | haengbokaeya dwae [행보캐야 돼] | You have to be happy |
| 우리 헤어지자 | uri heeojija | Let's break up |

Useful pattern: **도착하면 연락해요.** (Dochakamyeon yeollakaeyo.) – Let me know when you get there. Swap the first verb: **집에 가면 연락해요** (jibe gamyeon yeollakaeyo) – let me know when you're home; **끝나면 연락해요** (kkeunnamyeon yeollakaeyo [끈나면 열라캐요]) – let me know when you're done.
