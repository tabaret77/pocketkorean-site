## 1. This Sunday, the clocks go back one hour

![News 1 summary card](/german/media/life/2026-10-22/news-en-01.jpg)

In the night from Saturday to Sunday, **3:00 am on 25 October becomes 2:00 am**. Summer time (Sommerzeit) ends and winter time (Winterzeit, CET) begins, so you get an extra hour of sleep. The UK and Ireland change on the same night.

Phones, laptops and radio-controlled clocks switch by themselves. Oven and microwave clocks, older car clocks and mechanical timers need a manual change. If you call family abroad, check the new time difference.

The European Parliament voted in 2019 to end the clock change, but member states never agreed on whether to keep summer or winter time, so nothing has changed.

| When | What happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Sun 25 Oct, 03:00 | Back to 02:00 | Reset analogue and kitchen clocks |
| The week after | Darker evenings | Check your bike lights |

## 2. First autumn storm, windy all weekend

![News 2 summary card](/german/media/life/2026-10-22/news-en-02.jpg)

The German Weather Service (DWD) expects the first autumn storms of the season. Low **"Luise"**, which formed over the Atlantic, brings the strongest gusts to the **west on Friday**; the west and north, reaching into the northeast, stay stormy **until Monday**. The UK Met Office named the same low "Austen".

On exposed high ground, even hurricane-force gusts are possible. Around Cologne and Frankfurt, take care on Friday; in Hamburg and along the North Sea coast, through Sunday. Bring balcony plants and chairs inside, and check the DB app before you travel, as storms can delay or stop trains.

## 3. States: cut the electricity tax for everyone

![News 3 summary card](/german/media/life/2026-10-22/news-en-03.jpg)

At today's meeting of state premiers (MPK) in Berlin, the states asked the federal government to **cut the electricity tax (Stromsteuer) for all households and businesses** as soon as possible. The coalition agreement promises a cut to the EU minimum, but so far only industry, agriculture and forestry got it. Households still pay 2.05 cents per kWh.

The new official tax estimate, also out today, says federal, state and local governments will collect **about €11 billion a year more** than expected over the next four years. Chancellor Merz wants to use that room for the cut; Finance Minister Klingbeil is cautious and says whoever proposes it should also say how to pay for it.

**What it means for you:** a modest saving on your bill — if it happens. Nothing is decided yet.

| Household | Use per year | Estimated saving per year |
|---|---|---|
| Single | 1,500 kWh | about €35 |
| 2 people | 2,800 kWh | about €65 |
| 3 people | 4,000 kWh | about €93 |

## 4. Tobacco tax law took effect a year early — by mistake

![News 4 summary card](/german/media/life/2026-10-22/news-en-04.jpg)

The new tobacco tax law was meant to apply from January 2027, but a technical error in the published text put it **into force on Wednesday, 21 October**. The finance ministry says it has started a correction.

Manufacturers are stuck: on paper they now owe much more tax, but the new tax stamps don't exist yet. Customs will tolerate the old prices for now. **For smokers, nothing changes at the till yet.** The planned step-by-step increases still start in January 2027.

| Year | Expected price of a 20-pack (ministry estimate) |
|---|---|
| Now | about €8 on average |
| 2027 | €8.77 |
| 2030 | €11.36 |

## 5. Regional trains: fewer or pricier?

![News 5 summary card](/german/media/life/2026-10-22/news-en-05.jpg)

At the transport ministers' conference in Hohenschwangau, Bavaria (21–22 October), the states warned that much higher **track access fees (Trassenpreise)** for regional trains could mean **thinner timetables or higher fares** unless the federal government pays the difference. Federal Transport Minister Bilger says his ministry doesn't have the money.

Separately, it's already decided that the **Deutschlandticket goes up from €63 to €66.80 a month on 1 January 2027**. If you're thinking about switching or cancelling, do the maths before the end of the year.
