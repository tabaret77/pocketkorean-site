## Luther next to the pumpkins

By late October, German supermarkets are full of pumpkins and bags of sweets. But look at a German calendar and 31 October has a second name: **Reformationstag**, Reformation Day. The very next day, 1 November, is **Allerheiligen**, All Saints' Day. Three moods in two days.

![Three days at the turn of October](/german/media/life/2026-10-22/life-en-01.jpg)

## Reformation Day: Wittenberg, 1517

According to tradition, on 31 October 1517 Martin Luther nailed his **95 theses** to the door of the Castle Church in Wittenberg. That moment is seen as the start of the Reformation, and today it is a public holiday in **nine states**, mostly in the Protestant north and east. For the 500th anniversary in 2017, the whole country had the day off — just that once.

## A Saturday this year — check before you shop

German public holidays that fall on a weekend are not moved to a weekday. So on **Saturday, 31 October 2026, most shops in those nine states will be closed**. Berlin is not one of them, but Brandenburg, which surrounds it, is — worth knowing if you planned a trip to an outlet or a big supermarket outside the city. All Saints' Day falls on a Sunday this year, so it changes little.

![Who has a day off?](/german/media/life/2026-10-22/life-en-02.jpg)

| Date | Name | Public holiday in | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|
| 31 Oct | Reformationstag | Brandenburg, Bremen, Hamburg, Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, Lower Saxony, Saxony, Saxony-Anhalt, Schleswig-Holstein, Thuringia | Church services, events |
| 31 Oct | Halloween | nowhere (not a holiday) | Costumes, sweets, pumpkins |
| 1 Nov | Allerheiligen | Baden-Württemberg, Bavaria, North Rhine-Westphalia, Rhineland-Palatinate, Saarland | Grave visits, a quiet day |

## Halloween: "Süßes oder Saures!"

Halloween is a fairly recent import in Germany; it spread mainly from the 1990s on. Children in costumes ring the neighbours' doorbells and call **"Süßes oder Saures!"** — literally "sweet or sour", the German take on "trick or treat". In apartment buildings, some people leave a small bowl of sweets by the door. If you'd rather not join in, it's fine not to open.

## All Saints' Day: candles in the cemetery

In the Catholic south and west, 1 November is a day to remember the dead. Families tidy the graves and light **Grablichter**, grave candles in red or white holders, so at dusk the cemeteries glow. In several states it is a legally "quiet day" (stiller Tag) with limits on loud events such as dances; the exact rules depend on the state.
