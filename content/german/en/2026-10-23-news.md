## 1. [Politics] CSU conference opens — and pushes Ilse Aigner for president

- **CSU party conference, 23–24 Oct, Munich trade fair grounds** — Chancellor Merz speaks on Saturday (24 Oct)
- **The CSU wants Ilse Aigner** — The president of the Bavarian state parliament would be the first woman and the first CSU politician to become Federal President, succeeding Frank-Walter Steinmeier
- **The CDU is holding back** — Merz hasn't openly backed her, and no formal nomination is expected at the conference
- **The vote is on 30 January 2027** — Steinmeier's term ends on 18 March 2027. The president isn't elected by the people but by the Federal Convention, made up of Bundestag members and delegates from the states

## 2. [Economy] VW and unions sit down for a second "promise check"

- **Second review meeting on 23 Oct** — Workers want to know if VW still keeps its 2024 promises (no plant closures, no forced layoffs, investment). These aren't wage talks yet
- **Row over a "pay freeze until 2030"** — According to IG Metall, VW wants to freeze pay for about 660,000 staff worldwide until 2030. VW declined to comment
- **50,000 more jobs to go worldwide** — On top of existing cuts; about half could be in Germany
- **IG Metall wants 5% more pay** — The no-strike obligation ends on 31 December, so real talks must start by late November or early December
- **What it means** — If talks go badly, warning strikes at VW plants are possible early next year

## 3. [Society] German trains: no real improvement until 2029

- **Rail infrastructure chief's outlook** — Philipp Nagl, head of DB InfraGo, told *Die Zeit* that 2029, when the biggest line renovations end, will be the turning point
- **Long-distance punctuality fell from 64% to about 60%** — between 2023 and 2025, and 2026 is expected to be worse
- **Old equipment is the main problem** — Switches, overhead lines and signal boxes break faster than they can be fixed; infrastructure-related disruptions rose 40% in 2026
- **Renovation timeline** — Hanover–Berlin until the end of 2027, Hamburg–Hanover in 2029
- **What to do** — Plan extra time for long trips. A delay of 60+ minutes gets you 25% of the fare back, 120+ minutes 50% (strikes included; exceptions for major natural disasters or people on the tracks)

## 4. [Culture] Prize for exile literature goes to Belarusian poet Julia Cimafiejeva

- **Presented on 20 Oct at Heidelberg town hall, €15,000** — The Hilde Domin Prize goes every three years to writers living in exile in Germany who publish in German
- **Poet and translator, born in Belarus in 1982** — She left Belarus in 2020 for political reasons, together with her husband, the writer Alhierd Bacharevič
- **Named after Hilde Domin (1909–2006)** — a poet who fled the Nazis and later returned to Germany
- **Worth a read?** — Her poems about exile and memory are a good challenge for intermediate learners: short, clear and personal

## 5. [Arts] Berlin: first big Tanja Ostojić show opens in Kreuzberg

- **Opens today (23 Oct) at 5 pm, runs until 14 February 2027** — at Kunstraum Kreuzberg/Bethanien on Mariannenplatz
- **Free entry** — the artist's first comprehensive exhibition in Germany
- **25 years of work** — performance, video, installation, photography, drawing, textiles and new projects
