Today's words · 🎻 Classical music

A night at the concert hall in 10 German words

B1B2

You're in the land of Bach, Beethoven and Brahms — a concert should be on your list.

The first time I went to a German concert hall, I opened the programme and found Sinfonie, Dirigent, Werk… The words were strange, but the music was beautiful. In the interval the person next to me started chatting, and I couldn't answer.

Today's ten words come up whenever people talk about classical music. With them you can read a concert programme and chat about your favourite composers and pieces in German. 🎻

📋 The ten words at a glance

  1. die Klassik — classical music; Classicism, classical period
  2. klassisch — classical; classic, typical
  3. der Komponist — composer
  4. komponieren — to compose
  5. das Orchester — orchestra
  6. die Sinfonie — symphony
  7. der Dirigent — conductor (of an orchestra)
  8. dirigieren — to conduct (music); to direct (traffic)
  9. das Werk — work (of art); works, plant
  10. der Takt — beat, time, rhythm; bar, measure; tact

🧠 Words, examples and memory tricks

01

die Klassik

[KLAS-sik]classical music; Classicism, classical period

(no pl.)

📌 Ich höre beim Lernen am liebsten Klassik.

→ When I study I like listening to classical music best.

💡 From Latin classicus, first-class 🎼 First-class music = Klassik!

Bonus Ich höre gern Klassik. = I like listening to classical music.

02

klassisch

[KLAS-ish]classical; classic, typical

📌 Sie hört am liebsten klassische Musik.

→ She likes classical music best.

💡 The adjective of Klassik 🎹 It means both ‘classical’ and ‘classic, typical’.

Bonus Das ist ein klassischer Fehler! = That's a classic mistake!

03

der Komponist

[kom-po-NIST]composer

(Pl. die Komponisten (f: die Komponistin))

📌 Beethoven ist wohl der bekannteste Komponist aus Bonn.

→ Beethoven is probably the most famous composer from Bonn.

💡 Latin componere, to put together ✍️ A composer puts notes together.

⚠ Weak noun: den/dem/des Komponisten.

04

komponieren

[kom-po-NEER-en]to compose

(komponiert – hat komponiert)

📌 Beethoven komponierte neun Sinfonien.

→ Beethoven composed nine symphonies.

💡 Same root as compose 🎶 -ieren verbs take no ge- in the past participle.

Bonus Perfect: Er hat neun Sinfonien komponiert.

05

das Orchester

[or-KES-ter]orchestra

(Pl. die Orchester)

📌 Meine Tochter spielt Geige im Orchester.

→ My daughter plays the violin in the orchestra.

💡 Say the ch as k 🎺 In Greek theatres it was the dancing area in front of the stage.

Bonus Singular and plural are the same: das/die Orchester.

06

die Sinfonie

[zin-fo-NEE]symphony

(Pl. die Sinfonien)

📌 Das Orchester spielt eine Sinfonie von Beethoven.

→ The orchestra is playing a Beethoven symphony.

💡 Greek symphonia, ‘sounding together’ 🎻 Lots of instruments sounding as one.

Bonus The spelling Symphonie is also correct.

07

der Dirigent

[dee-ree-GENT]conductor (of an orchestra)

(Pl. die Dirigenten (f: die Dirigentin))

📌 Der Dirigent hob den Taktstock, und das Konzert begann.

→ The conductor raised his baton and the concert began.

💡 Latin dirigere, to lead 🪄 A cousin of English direct.

⚠ Weak noun: den/dem/des Dirigenten.

08

dirigieren

[dee-ree-GEER-en]to conduct (music); to direct (traffic)

(dirigiert – hat dirigiert)

📌 Wer dirigiert heute das Orchester?

→ Who is conducting the orchestra today?

💡 What a Dirigent does 🙌 A police officer directing traffic also dirigiert!

Bonus Perfect: Wer hat gestern dirigiert?

09

das Werk

[VAIRK]work (of art); works, plant

(Pl. die Werke)

📌 Das ist das bekannteste Werk von Goethe.

→ That's Goethe's best-known work.

💡 Same root as English work 🖼️ A work of art and a factory are both Werk.

Bonus Beethoven's complete works = Beethovens Gesamtwerk

10

der Takt

[TAKT]beat, time, rhythm; bar, measure; tact

(Pl. die Takte)

📌 Klatscht im Takt mit!

→ Clap along to the beat!

💡 Tap, tap — the baton keeps the beat 🥁 Same root as English tact.

Bonus im Takt = in time · Taktgefühl = tact, sensitivity

🇩🇪 A spoonful of German culture

Germany has a remarkable number of orchestras and opera houses. Even smaller towns often have their own theatre, and many venues offer reduced tickets for students and young people.

At a concert, people usually don't clap between movements (Sätze) — they wait until the whole piece is over. If you're unsure, just follow the people around you.

The dress code is more relaxed than you might think. Some people dress up, but plenty come in smart everyday clothes. At the end the applause often goes on for a long time, and you may hear a few “Bravo!”s. 👏

✅ Quick quiz

Q. During the concert, your friend whispers, “Wer ist der Mann mit dem Taktstock?” What do you answer?

  1. ① Das ist der Dirigent.
  2. ② Das ist der Komponist.
  3. ③ Das ist eine Sinfonie.
Show the answer

① Das ist der Dirigent.
The person holding the baton (Taktstock) is the conductor, der Dirigent.

Put on a favourite classical piece and say out loud: Der Komponist ist … Das Werk heißt … — in time with the Takt, of course! 🎶
