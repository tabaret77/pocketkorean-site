Today's words · 💰 Money

Money talk: 10 German words for cash, coins and saving

A1A2B1

Can't get a trolley at a German supermarket? That's exactly when you'll need today's words.

You try to pull a trolley out at a German supermarket and it won't budge. It needs a coin — and your wallet is full of cards. Too shy to ask ‘Haben Sie eine Münze?’, you grab a basket instead.

Today's ten words are all about money: cash and coins, saving and spending, lending and borrowing. With them you'll be fine at the till and when money comes up with friends. 💰

📋 The ten words at a glance

  1. das Geld — money
  2. das Bargeld — cash
  3. sparen — to save (money); to economize
  4. ausgeben — to spend (money)
  5. das Taschengeld — pocket money, allowance
  6. die Münze — coin
  7. das Kleingeld — small change, coins
  8. leihen — to lend; (sich + dat.) to borrow
  9. die Schulden — debts
  10. der Geldbeutel — wallet, purse

🧠 Words, examples and memory tricks

01

das Geld

[GELT]money

(no pl.)

📌 Ich habe leider kein Geld mehr.

→ Unfortunately I have no money left.

💡 Related to gelten (to be worth) 💶 Geld is what's worth something.

⚠ Uncountable, no plural: viel Geld, kein Geld

02

das Bargeld

[BAR-gelt]cash

((no pl.))

📌 Hast du Bargeld dabei?

→ Do you have any cash on you?

💡 bar (bare) + Geld = money in your bare hand 💵

Bonus to pay in cash = bar bezahlen

03

sparen

[SHPAH-ren]to save (money); to economize

(spart – hat gespart)

📌 Ich spare für eine Reise nach Italien.

→ I'm saving for a trip to Italy.

💡 Same root as English spare 🐷 A piggy bank is das Sparschwein.

Bonus Saving for something uses für: für eine Reise sparen

04

ausgeben

[OWS-gay-ben]to spend (money)

(gibt aus – hat ausgegeben)

📌 Ich gebe zu viel Geld für Kleidung aus.

→ I spend too much money on clothes.

💡 aus (out) + geben (give) = money going out 💸

⚠ Separable: Ich gebe viel Geld aus. – hat ausgegeben

05

das Taschengeld

[TASH-en-gelt]pocket money, allowance

(sg.)

📌 Ich bekomme 20 Euro Taschengeld im Monat.

→ I get 20 euros pocket money a month.

💡 Tasche (pocket) + Geld = pocket money, word for word 👖

06

die Münze

[MUEN-tsuh]coin

(Pl. die Münzen)

📌 Hast du eine Münze für den Einkaufswagen?

→ Have you got a coin for the shopping trolley?

💡 Same root as English money and mint 🪙 Ends in -e, so die.

Bonus A banknote is der Schein (Geldschein).

07

das Kleingeld

[KLINE-gelt]small change, coins

(sg.)

📌 Hast du Kleingeld für den Automaten?

→ Do you have change for the machine?

💡 klein (small) + Geld = small change 🪙

Bonus At the till you'll hear: Haben Sie es passend? (Do you have the exact amount?)

08

leihen

[LY-en]to lend; (sich + dat.) to borrow

(leiht – hat geliehen)

📌 Kannst du mir dein Fahrrad leihen?

→ Can you lend me your bike?

💡 Same root as English loan 🤝 One verb for lending and borrowing.

⚠ Lend: jemandem etwas leihen · Borrow: sich etwas leihen

09

die Schulden

[SHOOL-den]debts

(Pl. (sg. die Schuld = fault, guilt))

📌 Er hat hohe Schulden bei der Bank.

→ He has large debts with the bank.

💡 Lots of Schuld (what you owe) piles up into Schulden 😰

⚠ The singular die Schuld means fault or guilt: Das ist nicht meine Schuld.

10

der Geldbeutel

[GELT-boy-tel]wallet, purse

(Pl. die Geldbeutel)

📌 Ich habe meinen Geldbeutel zu Hause vergessen.

→ I left my wallet at home.

💡 Geld (money) + Beutel (pouch) = money pouch 👛 Beutel makes it der.

Bonus In the north you'll also hear das Portemonnaie.

🇩🇪 A spoonful of German culture

Germany has long had a reputation for loving cash. Card payments have become much more common, but some small bakeries and local restaurants still take cash only, so it's wise to carry a little.

Supermarket trolleys usually need a coin or a trolley token to unlock them, and you get it back when you return the trolley. Lots of people keep a token on their key ring.

Saving is also valued. Many children get Taschengeld and learn to manage it by putting some aside in a piggy bank. 🐷

✅ Quick quiz

Q. You've left your wallet at home and want to borrow ten euros from a friend. What do you ask?

  1. ① Kannst du mir zehn Euro leihen?
  2. ② Kannst du zehn Euro sparen?
  3. ③ Kannst du zehn Euro ausgeben?

① Kannst du mir zehn Euro leihen?
jemandem etwas leihen means to lend someone something, so you ask: can you lend me (mir) ten euros?
