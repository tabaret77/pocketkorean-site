Today's words · 🚄 Train journeys

All aboard! 10 words for German train journeys

A1A2B1B2

A ticket isn't enough on German trains. You'll want to understand the announcements too.

You're waiting on the platform for the ICE to Munich when an announcement comes: “Der ICE nach München fährt heute in umgekehrter Wagenreihung ab…” Everyone walks off to the other end of the platform, and you're left standing there.

Today's ten words are the ones you hear and use on every train trip. With them you can check departure and arrival times, find your reserved seat, visit the dining car and talk to the train staff. 🚄

📋 The ten words at a glance

  1. der Zug — train
  2. der ICE — ICE (German high-speed train)
  3. abfahren — to depart, leave
  4. ankommen — to arrive
  5. die Reservierung — reservation, booking
  6. der Sitzplatz — seat
  7. der Wagen — carriage, coach (train); car
  8. der Speisewagen — dining car
  9. der Zugbegleiter — train conductor, train attendant
  10. die Bahncard — BahnCard (German rail discount card)

🧠 Words, examples and memory tricks

01

der Zug

[TSOOK]train

(Pl. die Züge)

📌 Der Zug nach Hamburg fährt um 9 Uhr ab.

→ The train to Hamburg leaves at 9 o'clock.

💡 From ziehen (to pull), like English tug 🚆 The engine pulls the carriages.

⚠ Plural with umlaut: die Züge.

02

der ICE

[ee-tsay-AY]ICE (German high-speed train)

(Pl. die ICEs)

📌 Mit dem ICE sind wir in vier Stunden in München.

→ On the ICE we can be in Munich in four hours.

💡 InterCityExpress ⚡ Spell it out the German way — never ‘ice’!

Bonus Slower long-distance trains are der IC; regional ones are der RE or die RB.

03

abfahren

[AP-fah-ren]to depart, leave

(fährt ab – ist abgefahren)

📌 Der Bus fährt in fünf Minuten ab.

→ The bus leaves in five minutes.

💡 ab (off) + fahren (go) = to depart 🚉

⚠ Separable, perfect with sein: Der Zug fährt um 9 Uhr ab. / Der Zug ist schon abgefahren.

04

ankommen

[AN-kom-en]to arrive

(kommt an – ist angekommen)

📌 Wann kommt der Zug in Berlin an?

→ When does the train arrive in Berlin?

💡 an (at) + kommen (come) = to arrive 🏁 The partner of abfahren.

⚠ Arrive in = in + dative: in Berlin ankommen (not nach). Perfect with sein: Wir sind angekommen.

05

die Reservierung

[reh-zer-VEE-roong]reservation, booking

(Pl. die Reservierungen)

📌 Ich habe eine Reservierung für zwei Personen.

→ I have a reservation for two.

💡 reservieren + -ung = reservation 📌 -ung is always die.

Bonus Ich möchte einen Platz reservieren. = I'd like to reserve a seat.

06

der Sitzplatz

[ZITS-plats]seat

(Pl. die Sitzplätze)

📌 Ich möchte einen Sitzplatz reservieren.

→ I'd like to reserve a seat.

💡 sitzen (sit) + Platz (place) = seat 💺

Bonus Ist der Platz noch frei? = Is this seat free? · Answers: Ja, frei! or Nein, besetzt.

07

der Wagen

[VAH-gen]carriage, coach (train); car

(Pl. die Wagen)

📌 Ihr Platz ist in Wagen 12.

→ Your seat is in carriage 12.

💡 Same root as English wagon 🚃 Each carriage is a Wagen.

Bonus Same in the plural: die Wagen. Wagen 12 on your ticket means coach 12.

08

der Speisewagen

[SHPY-zuh-vah-gen]dining car

(Pl. die Speisewagen)

📌 Im Speisewagen gibt es warmes Essen.

→ Hot food is available in the dining car.

💡 Speise (food) + Wagen (carriage) = dining car 🍝 Same Speise as in Speisekarte (menu).

Bonus The smaller snack car is das Bordbistro.

09

der Zugbegleiter

[TSOOK-buh-gly-ter]train conductor, train attendant

(Pl. die Zugbegleiter (f: die Zugbegleiterin))

📌 Der Zugbegleiter hat die Fahrkarten kontrolliert.

→ The conductor checked the tickets.

💡 Zug + Begleiter (companion) = the person who travels with the train 🧑‍✈️

Bonus The older word is der Schaffner.

10

die Bahncard

[BAHN-kart]BahnCard (German rail discount card)

(Pl. die Bahncards)

📌 Mit der Bahncard 50 zahle ich nur die Hälfte des Fahrpreises.

→ With the BahnCard 50 I only pay half the fare.

💡 Bahn (railway) + English card = rail discount card 🎫 A German-English team-up!

Bonus BahnCard 25, 50 and 100 — the number tells you the discount level.

🇩🇪 A spoonful of German culture

German stations have no ticket barriers. Instead, staff check tickets on the train (Fahrkartenkontrolle). Riding without a ticket means a fine, so always buy one before you board.

On the ICE, reservations are optional, so you can sit in any free seat. But if the display above a seat shows it's reserved for part of the route, you'll need to move when that person gets on. For peace and quiet, look for the Ruhebereich (quiet zone).

German trains are also fairly often late. If your connection is tight, leave extra time, and check the coach order (Wagenreihung) on the platform display before the train comes in. 🧳
