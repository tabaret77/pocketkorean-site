Today's words · 📜 German history

German history in 10 words

A2B1B2

Walk through any German town and history speaks to you from walls and pavements.

You visit a museum at the weekend and every sign is in German. "Mittelalter", "Jahrhundert", "Nachkriegszeit"… It feels a shame to just look at the pictures and leave.

Today's ten words come up whenever people talk about German history. With them you'll understand far more of museum signs and documentaries, and you can even chat about history with German friends. 📜

📋 The ten words at a glance

  1. die Geschichte — story; history
  2. das Jahrhundert — century
  3. der Krieg — war
  4. der Kaiser — emperor
  5. das Mittelalter — Middle Ages
  6. der Weltkrieg — world war
  7. die Epoche — epoch, era, period
  8. die Revolution — revolution
  9. die Diktatur — dictatorship
  10. die Nachkriegszeit — postwar period

🧠 Words, examples and memory tricks

01

die Geschichte

[guh-SHIKH-tuh]story; history

(Pl. die Geschichten)

📌 Erzähl mir eine Geschichte!

→ Tell me a story!

💡 From geschehen (to happen) 📖 What happened = a story, and history.

Bonus Both meanings: Erzähl mir eine Geschichte! / Ich studiere Geschichte.

02

das Jahrhundert

[yar-HOON-dert]century

(Pl. die Jahrhunderte)

📌 Diese Kirche wurde im 15. Jahrhundert gebaut.

→ This church was built in the 15th century.

💡 Jahr (year) + hundert (hundred) = century 💯 Looks just like English.

Bonus ‘in the 15th century’ = im 15. Jahrhundert (read: fünfzehnten)

03

der Krieg

[kreek]war

(Pl. die Kriege)

📌 Mein Großvater hat viel vom Krieg erzählt.

→ My grandfather told a lot of stories about the war.

💡 It first meant ‘stubbornness, strife’ ⚔️ Strife grows into war.

Bonus The opposite: der Frieden = peace

04

der Kaiser

[KY-zer]emperor

(Pl. die Kaiser)

📌 Karl der Große wurde im Jahr 800 zum Kaiser gekrönt.

→ Charlemagne was crowned emperor in the year 800.

💡 From the Roman name Caesar 👑 Russian tsar has the same root!

Bonus empress = die Kaiserin

05

das Mittelalter

[MIT-el-al-ter]Middle Ages

(Sg.)

📌 Viele Burgen in Deutschland stammen aus dem Mittelalter.

→ Many castles in Germany date from the Middle Ages.

💡 mittel (middle) + Alter (age) = the age in the middle 🏰

⚠ It's singular in German: das Mittelalter (unlike English ‘the Middle Ages’).

06

der Weltkrieg

[VELT-kreek]world war

(Pl. die Weltkriege)

📌 Nach dem Zweiten Weltkrieg lag Deutschland in Trümmern.

→ After the Second World War, Germany lay in ruins.

💡 Welt (world) + Krieg (war) = world war 🌍 Lego-style again!

Bonus First World War = der Erste Weltkrieg · Second = der Zweite Weltkrieg

07

die Epoche

[eh-POKH-uh]epoch, era, period

(Pl. die Epochen)

📌 Die Romantik ist eine wichtige Epoche der deutschen Literatur.

→ Romanticism is an important period in German literature.

💡 The same word as English epoch ⏳ Ends in -e, so die.

Bonus Similar: die Zeit, das Zeitalter

08

die Revolution

[reh-vo-loo-TSYOHN]revolution

(Pl. die Revolutionen)

📌 Die Französische Revolution veränderte ganz Europa.

→ The French Revolution changed all of Europe.

💡 Nouns ending in -tion are always die 🔥 Spelt almost like English.

Bonus Adjective: revolutionär = revolutionary

09

die Diktatur

[dik-ta-TOOR]dictatorship

(Pl. die Diktaturen)

📌 Das Land war über dreißig Jahre lang eine Diktatur.

→ The country was a dictatorship for over thirty years.

💡 From dictate ✋ Borrowed nouns in -ur are mostly die.

Bonus dictator = der Diktator

10

die Nachkriegszeit

[NAHKH-kreeks-tsite]postwar period

(Sg.)

📌 In der Nachkriegszeit fehlte es an Wohnungen und Lebensmitteln.

→ In the postwar period there was a shortage of housing and food.

💡 nach (after) + Krieg (war) + s + Zeit (time) = the time after the war 🧱

⚠ Zeit comes last, so it's die. The last part of a compound sets the gender.

🇩🇪 A spoonful of German culture

Until 1871, Germany wasn't one country but many kingdoms and duchies. That's why dialects, food and architecture still vary so much from region to region.

Germans put a lot of weight on remembering the dark chapters of their own history. Schools teach the Nazi era in detail, and there are memorials and museums in towns all over the country.

On the pavement you may spot small brass plaques called Stolpersteine. Each one marks the home of a person persecuted by the Nazis and bears their name. Stopping for a moment to read it means something. 🕯️

✅ Quick quiz

Q. A museum sign says, "Diese Burg stammt aus dem 12. Jahrhundert." Which period is the castle from?

  1. ① aus dem Mittelalter
  2. ② aus der Nachkriegszeit
  3. ③ aus dem Zweiten Weltkrieg
Show the answer

① aus dem Mittelalter
The 12th century belongs to the Middle Ages — das Mittelalter.

Pick a building or monument near you and say out loud, "Das stammt aus dem … Jahrhundert." History will feel much closer! 🏛️
