Today's words · 🌸 Plants & flowers

10 German words that bloom on your windowsill

A1A2B1B2

If you're invited to a German home, what's in your hand will probably be a bunch of flowers.

When spring comes, flowerpots appear on windowsills and balconies all over Germany. I bought a little pot of herbs at the supermarket — then wondered who'd water it while I was away. I wanted to ask my neighbour, but the words wouldn't come.

Today's ten words are all about flowers and plants. With them you can buy a bouquet at the florist's, look after your pots and ask a neighbour to water them. 🌸

📋 The ten words at a glance

  1. die Blume — flower
  2. die Pflanze — plant
  3. gießen — to pour; to water (plants); to pour (with rain)
  4. blühen — to bloom, to blossom, to flower
  5. die Rose — rose
  6. pflanzen — to plant
  7. wachsen — to grow
  8. der Blumentopf — flowerpot
  9. der Blumenstrauß — bunch of flowers, bouquet
  10. die Zimmerpflanze — houseplant

🧠 Words, examples and memory tricks

01

die Blume

[BLOO-muh]flower

(Pl. die Blumen)

📌 Ich schenke dir Blumen.

→ I'll give you flowers.

💡 Same root as English bloom 🌼 Even the bl- sound matches.

Bonus Ich schenke dir Blumen. = I'm giving you flowers.

02

die Pflanze

[PFLAN-tsuh]plant

(Pl. die Pflanzen)

📌 Ich gieße jeden Tag meine Pflanzen.

→ I water my plants every day.

💡 The same word as English plant 🌱 Swap p for pf and you're speaking German.

Bonus The verb pflanzen (to plant) is in the same family.

03

gießen

[GEE-sen]to pour; to water (plants); to pour (with rain)

(gießt – goss – hat gegossen)

📌 Kannst du im Urlaub meine Blumen gießen?

→ Can you water my plants while I'm on holiday?

💡 A cousin of English gush 💦 Picture water gushing onto your plants.

⚠ Irregular: gießt – goss – hat gegossen.

04

blühen

[BLÜ-en]to bloom, to blossom, to flower

(blüht – hat geblüht)

📌 Im April blühen die Kirschbäume.

→ The cherry trees blossom in April.

💡 Family of Blume and bloom 🌸 Flowers open up and blühen.

Bonus A blossom is die Blüte.

05

die Rose

[ROH-zuh]rose

(Pl. die Rosen)

📌 Zum Geburtstag bekommt sie rote Rosen.

→ For her birthday she gets red roses.

💡 From Latin rosa 🌹 Remember to pronounce the final e!

Bonus red roses = rote Rosen · Keine Rose ohne Dornen. = No rose without thorns.

06

pflanzen

[PFLAN-tsen]to plant

(pflanzt – hat gepflanzt)

📌 Im Frühling pflanzen wir Tomaten.

→ We plant tomatoes in spring.

💡 Add -n to Pflanze and you have the verb 🪴

Bonus Im Frühling pflanzen wir Tomaten. = We plant tomatoes in spring.

07

wachsen

[VAK-sen]to grow

(wächst – wuchs – ist gewachsen)

📌 Mein Sohn ist dieses Jahr zehn Zentimeter gewachsen.

→ My son grew ten centimeters this year.

💡 Like the old English wax, as in the moon waxing 🌙 Growing bigger and bigger.

⚠ Perfect with sein: ist gewachsen. Don't mix it up with waschen (to wash)!

08

der Blumentopf

[BLOO-men-topf]flowerpot

(Pl. die Blumentöpfe)

📌 Auf der Fensterbank stehen drei Blumentöpfe mit Kräutern.

→ There are three pots of herbs on the windowsill.

💡 Blume + Topf (pot) = flowerpot 🏺 Topf is der, so the whole word is der.

⚠ Plural with umlaut: die Blumentöpfe.

09

der Blumenstrauß

[BLOO-men-shtrows]bunch of flowers, bouquet

(Pl. die Blumensträuße)

📌 Zum Geburtstag hat sie einen großen Blumenstrauß bekommen.

→ She got a big bunch of flowers for her birthday.

💡 Blume + Strauß (bunch) = bouquet 💐 And der Strauß also means ostrich!

⚠ Plural with umlaut: die Blumensträuße.

10

die Zimmerpflanze

[TSIM-er-pflan-tsuh]houseplant

(Pl. die Zimmerpflanzen)

📌 Zimmerpflanzen verbessern die Luft in der Wohnung.

→ Houseplants improve the air in the flat.

💡 Zimmer (room) + Pflanze (plant) = a room plant 🪴 The name explains itself.

Bonus A balcony plant is die Balkonpflanze.

🇩🇪 A spoonful of German culture

When you're invited to someone's home in Germany, a small bunch of flowers is a common gift. Red roses feel strongly romantic, though, so for friends it's safer to choose something else.

Cities are full of allotment gardens called Kleingarten (or Schrebergarten), small rented plots where people grow flowers and vegetables. People without a garden often fill their balconies with flowers and herbs instead.

During the holidays, neighbours often water each other's plants and empty each other's letterboxes. A small thank-you gift afterwards is a nice touch. 🌷
