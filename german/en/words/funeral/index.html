Today's words · 🕯️ Funerals & condolences

Saying the right thing: 10 German words for funerals

A1A2B1B2

In sad moments, a few simple words said properly mean a lot.

You hear that a German colleague's father has died. You want to say something, but it's hard enough in your own language — how do you say it in German?

Today's ten words are about funerals and condolences. With them you can understand the news, write a sympathy card and quietly show you care. It's a heavy topic, but these are words everyone needs one day. 🕯️

📋 The ten words at a glance

  1. sterben — to die
  2. der Tod — death
  3. tot — dead
  4. die Beerdigung — funeral; burial
  5. der Friedhof — cemetery, graveyard
  6. das Grab — grave
  7. trauern — to mourn, to grieve (for)
  8. die Trauer — grief, sorrow; mourning
  9. das Beileid — condolence(s), sympathy
  10. der Sarg — coffin

🧠 Words, examples and memory tricks

01

sterben

[SHTAIR-ben]to die

(stirbt – ist gestorben)

📌 Mein Großvater ist letztes Jahr gestorben.

→ My grandfather died last year.

💡 Same root as English starve 🥀 It once simply meant ‘to die’.

⚠ Perfect with sein: Er ist gestorben. · Irregular: stirbt

02

der Tod

[toht]death

(Pl. die Tode (rare))

📌 Der Tod seiner Mutter war schwer für ihn.

→ His mother's death was hard for him.

💡 Same root as English death ⚰️ The noun ends in d.

⚠ Don't mix it up with tot (dead), which sounds the same.

03

tot

[toht]dead

📌 Die Pflanze ist leider tot.

→ Unfortunately, the plant is dead.

💡 Same root as English dead 🍂 The adjective ends in t.

Bonus Noun der Tod with d, adjective tot with t — same sound.

04

die Beerdigung

[buh-AIR-di-goong]funeral; burial

(Pl. die Beerdigungen)

📌 Zur Beerdigung kamen viele Freunde der Familie.

→ Many friends of the family came to the funeral.

💡 be- + Erde (earth) = putting into the earth 🌍 English earth is hiding inside.

Bonus Also useful: die Trauerfeier = memorial service

05

der Friedhof

[FREET-hohf]cemetery, graveyard

(Pl. die Friedhöfe)

📌 Meine Großeltern liegen auf dem Friedhof in unserem Dorf.

→ My grandparents are buried in the cemetery in our village.

💡 Sounds like Frieden (peace) + Hof (yard) 🕊️ A quiet, peaceful yard.

⚠ Plural with umlaut: die Friedhöfe

06

das Grab

[grahp]grave

(Pl. die Gräber)

📌 Am Sonntag bringen wir Blumen zum Grab unserer Oma.

→ On Sunday we're taking flowers to our grandma's grave.

💡 From graben (to dig) ⛏️ Almost the same as English grave.

⚠ Plural with umlaut and -er: die Gräber

07

trauern

[TROW-ern]to mourn, to grieve (for)

(trauert – hat getrauert (+ um + acc.))

📌 Das ganze Land trauert um die Opfer.

→ The whole country is mourning the victims.

💡 Picture someone lowering their head 😔 to mourn, to grieve.

⚠ The preposition is um + accusative: um jemanden trauern (not über).

08

die Trauer

[TROW-er]grief, sorrow; mourning

(Sg.)

📌 Die Trauer um seine Mutter war groß.

→ His grief over his mother was great.

💡 The noun of trauern 🖤 Black funeral clothes are Trauerkleidung.

Bonus in Trauer sein = to be in mourning

09

das Beileid

[BY-lite]condolence(s), sympathy

(no pl.)

📌 Mein herzliches Beileid.

→ My sincere condolences.

💡 bei (with) + Leid (sorrow) = being with someone in their pain 🤍

Bonus The standard phrase: Mein herzliches Beileid.

10

der Sarg

[zark]coffin

(Pl. die Särge)

📌 Der Sarg wurde langsam ins Grab gelassen.

→ The coffin was slowly lowered into the grave.

💡 Shortened from Greek sarkophagos 🪦 Think of English sarcophagus.

⚠ Plural with umlaut: die Särge

🇩🇪 A spoonful of German culture

To offer condolences in German, people usually keep it short: Mein herzliches Beileid. or Es tut mir sehr leid. You don't need many words for them to feel sincere.

Mourners often wear black or dark clothes to a funeral. If you can't attend, sending a Trauerkarte (sympathy card) is common. After the funeral, family and friends sometimes gather for coffee and cake, called Trauerkaffee.

German cemeteries are often green and quiet, and some people walk there like in a park. Looking after the grave is an important task for many families. 🕯️
