Sound like a local: 10 German proverbs

C2

Imagine a German friend drops a proverb into the conversation — and you know exactly what it means.

A colleague's daughter has become a lawyer, just like her mum. Someone smiles and says, "Na ja, der Apfel fällt nicht weit vom Stamm." The apple doesn't fall far from the trunk? Oh — like mother, like daughter!

Today's ten proverbs are ones Germans really use in everyday life. Many have close English twins, so they're easier to learn than you'd think. Slip one into a conversation and your German instantly sounds more fluent! 📜

📋 The ten words at a glance

  1. Morgenstund hat Gold im Mund. — The early bird catches the worm.
  2. Lügen haben kurze Beine. — Lies have short legs; the truth will out.
  3. Der Apfel fällt nicht weit vom Stamm. — The apple doesn't fall far from the tree.
  4. Reden ist Silber, Schweigen ist Gold. — Speech is silver, silence is golden.
  5. Wer zuletzt lacht, lacht am besten. — He who laughs last laughs best.
  6. Viele Köche verderben den Brei. — Too many cooks spoil the broth.
  7. Es ist nicht alles Gold, was glänzt. — all that glitters is not gold; appearances are deceptive
  8. Ohne Fleiß kein Preis. — no pain, no gain
  9. Aller guten Dinge sind drei. — third time lucky; all good things come in threes
  10. Wer A sagt, muss auch B sagen. — In for a penny, in for a pound; if you start sth., you have to see it through.

🧠 Words, examples and memory tricks

01

Morgenstund hat Gold im Mund.

[MOR-gen-shtoont hat GOLT im MOONT]The early bird catches the worm.

(proverb)

📌 Sie schreibt ihre Romane vor Sonnenaufgang – Morgenstund hat Gold im Mund, sagt sie.

→ She writes her novels before sunrise – the early bird catches the worm, she says.

💡 Stund rhymes with Mund: the morning hour has gold in its mouth 🌅

Bonus English: The early bird catches the worm.

⚠ Morgenstund is an old short form of Morgenstunde, used only in this saying.

02

Lügen haben kurze Beine.

[LUE-gen HAH-ben KOOR-tsuh BY-nuh]Lies have short legs; the truth will out.

(proverb)

📌 Er dachte, niemand würde die gefälschten Zahlen bemerken, aber Lügen haben kurze Beine.

→ He thought nobody would notice the falsified figures, but the truth always comes out.

💡 Lies have short legs, so they can't run far 🦵 You'll get caught!

Bonus a lie = die Lüge · to lie = lügen

03

Der Apfel fällt nicht weit vom Stamm.

[dair AP-fel FELT nikht VITE fom SHTAM]The apple doesn't fall far from the tree.

(proverb)

📌 Auch die Tochter ist Anwältin geworden – der Apfel fällt eben nicht weit vom Stamm.

→ The daughter has become a lawyer too – the apple doesn't fall far from the tree.

💡 Apples land right by the trunk (Stamm) 🍎 Same picture as in English.

Bonus Also used as ‘like father, like son’.

04

Reden ist Silber, Schweigen ist Gold.

[RAY-den ist ZIL-ber, SHVY-gen ist GOLT]Speech is silver, silence is golden.

(proverb)

📌 Bei der Pressekonferenz hielt sie sich zurück – Reden ist Silber, Schweigen ist Gold.

→ At the press conference she held back – speech is silver, silence is golden.

💡 Talking (Reden) gets silver, silence (Schweigen) gets gold 🥇

Bonus Verbs used as nouns are das: das Reden, das Schweigen

05

Wer zuletzt lacht, lacht am besten.

[vair tsoo-LETST lakht, lakht am BES-ten]He who laughs last laughs best.

(proverb)

📌 Alle haben über seinen Plan gespottet, doch wer zuletzt lacht, lacht am besten.

→ Everyone mocked his plan, but he who laughs last laughs best.

💡 Whoever laughs last (zuletzt) laughs best (am besten) 😏

⚠ In a wer clause the verb goes to the end: Wer zuletzt lacht, …

06

Viele Köche verderben den Brei.

[FEE-luh KUR-khuh fer-DAIR-ben dain BRY]Too many cooks spoil the broth.

(proverb)

📌 Bei sieben Abteilungsleitern im Projekt gilt: Viele Köche verderben den Brei.

→ With seven heads of department on the project, it's a case of too many cooks spoiling the broth.

💡 Too many cooks spoil the porridge (Brei) 🍲 The English has broth instead.

⚠ der Koch has an umlaut in the plural: die Köche.

07

Es ist nicht alles Gold, was glänzt.

[es ist nikht AL-es GOLT, vas GLENTST]all that glitters is not gold; appearances are deceptive

📌 Die Start-up-Szene wirkt glamourös, doch es ist nicht alles Gold, was glänzt.

→ The start-up scene looks glamorous, but all that glitters is not gold.

💡 glänzen (to shine) is a cousin of English glint and glance ✨

Bonus Often shortened: Nicht alles Gold, was glänzt.

08

Ohne Fleiß kein Preis.

[OH-nuh FLICE kine PRICE]no pain, no gain

📌 Ohne Fleiß kein Preis – also ran an die Grammatikübungen!

→ No pain, no gain – so let's get on with the grammar exercises!

💡 Fleiß (hard work) rhymes with Preis (prize) 🏆 No pain, no gain!

Bonus fleißig = hard-working · Du bist so fleißig!

09

Aller guten Dinge sind drei.

[AL-er GOO-ten DING-uh zint DRY]third time lucky; all good things come in threes

📌 Zweimal bin ich durchgefallen, aber aller guten Dinge sind drei.

→ I failed twice, but third time lucky.

💡 Good things come in threes 3️⃣ Third time lucky!

⚠ aller isn't alle — it's an old genitive plural. Just learn it as it is.

10

Wer A sagt, muss auch B sagen.

[vair AH zahkt, mooss owkh BAY ZAH-gen]In for a penny, in for a pound; if you start sth., you have to see it through.

(proverb)

📌 Du hast das Projekt angestoßen; wer A sagt, muss auch B sagen.

→ You started this project; in for a penny, in for a pound.

💡 If you say A, you have to say B 🔤 In for a penny, in for a pound!

Bonus German letters: A sounds like ‘ah’, B like ‘bay’.

🇩🇪 A spoonful of German culture

Lots of German proverbs rhyme — Stund and Mund, Fleiß and Preis — which makes them easy to remember. Many people grow up hearing them again and again from grandparents and teachers.

In conversation, people often say only the first half of a proverb. Stop at "Na ja, wer zuletzt lacht …" and the listener finishes it in their head.

One theory says the Ding in Aller guten Dinge sind drei refers to old Germanic court assemblies. Even a small word can carry a bit of history. 📜

✅ Quick quiz

Q. Your friend has failed the driving test twice and is off to try a third time. What do you say to cheer them on?

  1. ① Lügen haben kurze Beine.
  2. ② Viele Köche verderben den Brei.
  3. ③ Aller guten Dinge sind drei!
Show the answer

③ Aller guten Dinge sind drei!
Aller guten Dinge sind drei = third time lucky, so it wishes them success this time.

Pick your favourite proverb from today and look for a chance to use it. Say it out loud three times — aller guten Dinge sind drei! 😉
