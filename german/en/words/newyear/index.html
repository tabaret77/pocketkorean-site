Today's words · 🎆 New Year's Eve

Guten Rutsch! 10 words for a German New Year

A2B1B2C1

In Germany, people wish you a ‘good slide’ into the New Year. Really!

It's the night of 31 December in Berlin. Bangers have been going off since the afternoon, and at your friend's flat the Sekt glasses are ready. Even the supermarket cashier wished you "Guten Rutsch!" — but why would anyone want you to slide?

Today's ten words are the ones you'll hear all over Germany at New Year. With them you can wish people a happy New Year, count down to midnight and share your resolutions in German. Let's see the year out in style! 🎆

📋 The ten words at a glance

  1. Silvester — New Year's Eve
  2. die Mitternacht — midnight
  3. das Feuerwerk — fireworks
  4. Neujahr — New Year's Day
  5. der Sekt — sparkling wine
  6. der Rutsch — slide, slip; short trip (colloquial)
  7. der Vorsatz — resolution, intention; (law) intent
  8. wünschen — to wish
  9. der Glücksbringer — lucky charm, mascot
  10. der Jahreswechsel — turn of the year, New Year

🧠 Words, examples and memory tricks

01

Silvester

[zil-VES-ter]New Year's Eve

(sg.)

📌 An Silvester gibt es ein Feuerwerk.

→ There are fireworks on New Year's Eve.

💡 31 December is Saint Sylvester's day, hence the name 🎇

⚠ No article: an Silvester, zu Silvester.

02

die Mitternacht

[MIT-ter-nakht]midnight

(sg. · = um Mitternacht)

📌 Um Mitternacht gehe ich schlafen.

→ I go to bed at midnight.

💡 Mitte (middle) + Nacht (night) = midnight 🕛 Exactly the same build as English!

Bonus at midnight = um Mitternacht

03

das Feuerwerk

[FOY-er-vairk]fireworks

(Pl. die Feuerwerke)

📌 An Silvester gibt es überall Feuerwerk.

→ On New Year's Eve there are fireworks everywhere.

💡 Feuer (fire) + Werk (work) = firework 🎆 A word-for-word match.

Bonus A banger or firecracker is der Böller.

04

Neujahr

[NOY-yar]New Year's Day

(sg.)

📌 An Neujahr schlafen alle lange.

→ On New Year's Day everyone sleeps in.

💡 neu (new) + Jahr (year) = New Year's Day 🌅 Sounds a bit like ‘new year’ too.

⚠ Like Silvester, no article: an Neujahr.

05

der Sekt

[zekt]sparkling wine

(Pl. die Sekte)

📌 Zu Silvester stoßen wir mit Sekt an.

→ We toast with sparkling wine on New Year's Eve.

💡 From Italian secco (dry) 🥂 Germany's own sparkling wine.

⚠ It's der Sekt. die Sekte means ‘sect, cult’ — careful!

06

der Rutsch

[rootch]slide, slip; short trip (colloquial)

(Pl. die Rutsche)

📌 Guten Rutsch ins neue Jahr!

→ Happy New Year!

💡 From rutschen (to slide) 🛷 Slide smoothly into the New Year!

⚠ Say Guten Rutsch! only before New Year. From 1 January it's Frohes neues Jahr!

07

der Vorsatz

[FOR-zats]resolution, intention; (law) intent

(Pl. die Vorsätze)

📌 Meine guten Vorsätze fürs neue Jahr halten nie lange.

→ My good New Year's resolutions never last long.

💡 vor (before) + Satz (something set) = something you decide in advance 📝

Bonus New Year's resolutions = gute Vorsätze fürs neue Jahr

08

wünschen

[VUEN-shen]to wish

(wünscht – hat gewünscht)

📌 Ich wünsche dir alles Gute!

→ I wish you all the best!

💡 Same root as English wish 🌠 Wish upon a star: wünschen!

Bonus Ich wünsche dir ein frohes neues Jahr! = Happy New Year to you!

09

der Glücksbringer

[GLUEKS-bring-er]lucky charm, mascot

(Pl. die Glücksbringer)

📌 Ein vierblättriges Kleeblatt gilt als Glücksbringer.

→ A four-leaf clover is considered a lucky charm.

💡 Glück (luck) + Bringer (bringer) = luck-bringer 🍀

Bonus Classic Glücksbringer: das Glücksschwein (pig), der Schornsteinfeger (chimney sweep), das Kleeblatt (clover)

10

der Jahreswechsel

[YAH-res-vek-sel]turn of the year, New Year

(Pl. die Jahreswechsel)

📌 Zum Jahreswechsel gibt es überall Feuerwerk.

→ At New Year there are fireworks everywhere.

💡 Jahr (year) + Wechsel (change) = the changing of the year 🔄 A bureau de change is a Wechselstube!

Bonus around New Year = zum Jahreswechsel

🇩🇪 A spoonful of German culture

On Silvester many Germans get together with friends at home, often over raclette or fondue, and wait for midnight. A long-standing tradition in lots of homes is watching the old British comedy sketch "Dinner for One" on TV.

At midnight people toast with Sekt and fireworks go off everywhere. There's also a fortune-telling game: you drop melted wax into cold water and read the shape — these days it's usually wax rather than the old lead.

Around this time people like to give little Glücksbringer, such as marzipan pigs or chimney-sweep figures. Yes, in Germany a pig means good luck! 🐷
