-았어요/었어요: talking about the past in Korean

2026-10-23

Phrase from this story: 했어요 = (I) didSearch a word

"What did you do at the weekend?" is probably the question you'll hear most on a Monday in Korea. To answer it, you need the past tense, and the good news is that it's built on something you may already know: the present-tense -아요/어요 ending. Swap 요 for ㅆ어요 and you are in the past. 가요 (gayo) – I go – becomes 갔어요 (gasseoyo) – I went.

The basic rule

Take the verb stem (dictionary form minus -다) and look at its last vowel:

  • Last vowel ㅏ or ㅗ → add -았어요 (-asseoyo)
  • Any other vowel → add -었어요 (-eosseoyo)
  • Anything ending in 하다 → 했어요 (haesseoyo)

It's exactly the same vowel choice as in the present tense, so if you can say 먹어요, you can say 먹었어요.

Dictionary form Stem Past (polite) Meaning
살다 (salda) 살- 살았어요 (sarasseoyo) I lived
좋다 (jota) 좋- 좋았어요 (joasseoyo) it was good
먹다 (meokta [먹따]) 먹- 먹었어요 (meogeosseoyo) I ate
읽다 (ikta [익따]) 읽- 읽었어요 (ilgeosseoyo) I read
맛있다 (masitta [마싣따]) 맛있- 맛있었어요 (masisseosseoyo) it was delicious
공부하다 (gongbuhada) 공부하- 공부했어요 (gongbuhaesseoyo) I studied
일하다 (ilhada) 일하- 일했어요 (ilhaesseoyo) I worked

Vowels that merge

When the stem ends in a vowel, the two vowels usually squeeze together, just as in the present tense:

Dictionary form Past (polite) What happens
가다 (gada) 갔어요 (gasseoyo) 가 + 았 → 갔
오다 (oda) 왔어요 (wasseoyo) 오 + 았 → 왔
보다 (boda) 봤어요 (bwasseoyo) 보 + 았 → 봤
마시다 (masida) 마셨어요 (masyeosseoyo) 시 + 었 → 셨
배우다 (baeuda) 배웠어요 (baewosseoyo) 우 + 었 → 웠
서다 (seoda) 섰어요 (seosseoyo) 서 + 었 → 섰
보내다 (bonaeda) 보냈어요 (bonaesseoyo) 내 + 었 → 냈

A few stems, like 쉬다 (swida) – to rest – don't merge: 쉬었어요 (swieosseoyo).

Nouns: 이었어요 / 였어요

To say "was" with a noun, use 이었어요 after a consonant and 였어요 after a vowel:

  • 어제는 제 생일이었어요. (Eojeneun je saengirieosseoyo.) – Yesterday was my birthday.
  • 그 사람은 제 친구였어요. (Geu sarameun je chinguyeosseoyo.) – That person was my friend.

Irregular verbs keep their quirks

Irregular verbs change in the past exactly as they do in the present, so learn them in pairs:

  • 듣다 (deutta [듣따]) – to listen → 들었어요 (deureosseoyo)
  • 춥다 (chupta [춥따]) – to be cold → 추웠어요 (chuwosseoyo)
  • 바쁘다 (bappeuda) – to be busy → 바빴어요 (bappasseoyo)
  • 쓰다 (sseuda) – to write, use → 썼어요 (sseosseoyo)
  • 모르다 (moreuda) – not to know → 몰랐어요 (mollasseoyo)

Negative past

Put 안 (not) or 못 (couldn't) in front of the past verb:

  • 아침을 안 먹었어요. (Achimeul an meogeosseoyo.) – I didn't eat breakfast.
  • 어제 못 잤어요. (Eoje mot jasseoyo.) – I couldn't sleep last night.

With 하다 verbs the negative goes right before 했어요: 공부 안 했어요 (gongbu an haesseoyo) – I didn't study.

Time words that go with it

어제 (eoje) – yesterday, 지난주 (jinanju) – last week, 주말에 (jumare) – at the weekend, 작년 (jangnyeon) – last year. Late October is the perfect time to practise: 주말에 단풍 구경 갔어요. (Jumare danpung gugyeong gasseoyo.) – At the weekend I went to see the autumn leaves.

In casual speech with friends, just drop 요: 갔어, 먹었어, 뭐 했어?

Mini practice

Put these into the polite past. Answers below.

  1. 만나다 (to meet)
  2. 마시다 (to drink)
  3. 청소하다 (to clean)
  4. 덥다 (to be hot)
  5. 학생 (student) + was

Answers: 1. 만났어요 (mannasseoyo) 2. 마셨어요 (masyeosseoyo) 3. 청소했어요 (cheongsohaesseoyo) 4. 더웠어요 (deowosseoyo) 5. 학생이었어요 (hakssaengieosseoyo [학쌩이어써요])
