Kimjang words: Korean for the kimchi-making season

2026-10-22

Phrase from this story: 김장 = making kimchi for the winterSearch a word

When the air turns cold in Korea, markets fill with stacks of cabbages and sacks of red chilli flakes. It's 김장 (gimjang) season – the time when families and neighbours get together to make a big batch of kimchi to last through the winter. The tradition is so central to Korean life that UNESCO added "Kimjang, making and sharing kimchi in the Republic of Korea" to its intangible cultural heritage list in 2013. Here's the vocabulary you need to join in.

How kimjang works

A typical kimjang day follows the same steps, and each one has its own verb:

  1. 절이다 (jeorida) – to salt. Halved cabbages are soaked or sprinkled with salt so they soften. Many families now buy ready-salted cabbage, 절임배추 (jeorimbaechu), to save a day of work.
  2. 씻다 (ssitda) – to wash. The salted cabbage is rinsed and drained.
  3. 양념을 만들다 (yangnyeomeul mandeulda) – to make the seasoning: chilli flakes, garlic, ginger, fish sauce, shredded radish and spring onion.
  4. 버무리다 (beomurida) – to mix and coat. Everyone sits around a big tub, wearing rubber gloves, and rubs the red filling between every leaf.
  5. 담그다 (damgeuda) – to make (kimchi, pickles). The finished kimchi goes into containers, often into a dedicated kimchi fridge.

Then comes the best part: eating boiled pork wrapped in fresh, just-made kimchi, and sending boxes to relatives and neighbours.

Watch the pronunciation

A few of these don't sound the way they're spelled:

  • 고춧가루 is said [고추까루] – the ㅅ in the middle makes the ㄱ tense: "go-chu-kka-ru".
  • 젓갈 is said [젇깔]: the final ㅅ sounds like a t, and the ㄱ after it becomes tense: "jeot-kkal".
  • 익다 is said [익따] (ikda), and 익었어요 (igeosseoyo) – it's ripe – links the ㄱ to the next syllable.

Taste words

Kimjang is also when everyone has an opinion about flavour. You'll hear:

  • 맵다 (maepda) – to be spicy → 좀 매워요 (jom maewoyo) – it's a bit spicy
  • 짜다 (jjada) – to be salty → 너무 짜요 (neomu jjayo) – it's too salty
  • 싱겁다 (singgeopda) – to be bland, not salty enough → 좀 싱거워요 (jom singgeowoyo)
  • 맛있다 (masitda) – to be delicious → 진짜 맛있어요! (jinjja masisseoyo!)

맵다 and 싱겁다 are ㅂ-irregular verbs, so the ㅂ turns into 우: 매워요, 싱거워요.

Phrases for the kimjang table

  • 저도 도와드릴게요. (Jeodo dowadeurilgeyo.) – I'll help too.
  • 간 좀 봐 주세요. (Gan jom bwa juseyo.) – Please taste it for seasoning.
  • 김치 좀 나눠 드릴게요. (Gimchi jom nanwo deurilgeyo.) – I'll share some kimchi with you.

If a Korean friend or neighbour invites you to their kimjang, say yes. It's hard work, but it's one of the warmest experiences of the Korean year.

올해 김장 언제 해요? (Olhae gimjang eonje haeyo?) – When are you doing kimjang this year? It's a natural small-talk question from November onwards, and a nice way to show you know the season.
