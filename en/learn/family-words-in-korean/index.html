Family words in Korean: 엄마, 할머니, 이모 and who says what

2026-10-24

Phrase from this story: 가족 = familySearch a word

가족 (gajok) – family – is one of the first topics in any Korean class, and also one of the trickiest. English has one word for "aunt"; Korean has several, depending on whether she is your mother's or your father's sister. English "brother" works for everyone; in Korean, the word depends on whether he's older, and on whether you are male or female. Here's a map.

Parents and grandparents

Casual Polite Meaning
엄마 (eomma) 어머니 (eomeoni) mum, mother
아빠 (appa) 아버지 (abeoji) dad, father
– 부모님 (bumonim) parents
할머니 (halmeoni) 할머님 (halmeonim) grandmother
할아버지 (harabeoji) 할아버님 (harabeonim) grandfather

Adults still say 엄마 and 아빠 at home, but when talking to a teacher, a boss or a stranger, 어머니 and 아버지 sound more grown-up. The forms with -님 (nim) are extra respectful and are mostly used for someone else's parents or your in-laws: 어머님 (eomeonim).

Brothers and sisters

Older siblings get different words depending on the speaker's gender. Younger siblings are simply 동생 (dongsaeng):

Speaker Older brother Older sister Younger sibling
female 오빠 (oppa) 언니 (eonni) 동생 (dongsaeng)
male 형 (hyeong) 누나 (nuna) 동생 (dongsaeng)

To be specific about a younger sibling, say 남동생 (namdongsaeng) – younger brother – or 여동생 (yeodongsaeng) – younger sister. 형제 (hyeongje) means brothers or siblings in general, and 자매 (jamae) means sisters. These same words are also used for older friends; we cover that in how to address people in Korean.

친가 and 외가: two sides of the family

Korean separates the father's side, 친가 (chinga), from the mother's side, 외가 (oega). Relatives on the mother's side often get 외- (oe-) in front:

  • 외할머니 (oehalmeoni) – grandmother on your mother's side
  • 외할아버지 (oeharabeoji) – grandfather on your mother's side
  • 외삼촌 (oesamchon) – your mother's brother

Aunts and uncles each have their own word:

Father's side Mother's side
고모 (gomo) – father's sister 이모 (imo) – mother's sister
삼촌 (samchon) / 큰아버지 (keunabeoji) / 작은아버지 (jageunabeoji) – father's brother 외삼촌 (oesamchon) – mother's brother

큰아버지 is your father's older brother and 작은아버지 his younger brother; 삼촌 is used for an unmarried one and in casual talk. A cousin on either side is 사촌 (sachon). You'll also hear 이모 in restaurants: it's a friendly way to call an older woman who serves you.

Talking about your own family

Ask: 가족이 몇 명이에요? (Gajogi myeot myeongieyo? [가조기 면 명이에요?]) – How many people are in your family? Answer with native Korean numbers:

  • 저희 가족은 네 명이에요. (Jeohui gajogeun ne myeongieyo. [저히 가조근 네 명이에요]) – There are four of us in my family.
  • 언니가 한 명 있어요. (Eonniga han myeong isseoyo.) – I have one older sister.
  • 형제가 없어요. (Hyeongjega eopsseoyo. [형제가 업써요]) – I don't have any siblings.

Two habits sound natural. First, Koreans say 우리 (uri) or the humble 저희 (jeohui) – "our" – instead of "my": 우리 엄마 (uri eomma) – my mum. Second, older relatives get honorific verbs: 할머니가 서울에 계세요 (Halmeoniga Seoure gyeseyo) – My grandmother lives in Seoul – uses 계시다 instead of 있다.

Korean you can use

Korean Romanization Meaning
가족 gajok family
부모님 bumonim parents
동생 dongsaeng younger sibling
외할머니 oehalmeoni maternal grandmother
이모 / 고모 imo / gomo aunt (mother's / father's sister)
사촌 sachon cousin
가족이 몇 명이에요? gajogi myeot myeongieyo? [가조기 면 명이에요?] How many people are in your family?
외동이에요 oedongieyo I'm an only child

저희 가족은 ___ 명이에요. ___하고 ___ 있어요. (Jeohui gajogeun ___ myeongieyo. hago ___ isseoyo.) – There are ___ of us in my family. I have ___ and . Fill it in for your own family, using 우리 or 저희 instead of "my".
