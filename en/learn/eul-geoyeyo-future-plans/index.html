-(으)ㄹ 거예요: talking about plans and guesses in Korean

2026-10-24

Phrase from this story: 갈 거예요 = (I) will go, (I'm) going to goSearch a word

Yesterday's lesson took you into the past with 갔어요 (gasseoyo) – I went. Today we go the other way. On a Friday in Korea you'll hear 주말에 뭐 할 거예요? (Jumare mwo hal kkeoyeyo? [주마레 뭐 할 꺼예요?]) – What are you going to do at the weekend? The ending -(으)ㄹ 거예요 is the everyday way to talk about plans, and it also works for guesses: "it'll probably rain".

The basic rule

Take the verb stem (dictionary form minus -다) and look at its last letter:

  • Stem ends in a vowel → add -ㄹ 거예요
  • Stem ends in ㄹ → just add 거예요 (the ㄹ is already there)
  • Stem ends in another consonant → add -을 거예요
Dictionary form Future (polite) Meaning
가다 (gada) 갈 거예요 (gal kkeoyeyo [갈 꺼예요]) I'm going to go
보다 (boda) 볼 거예요 (bol kkeoyeyo [볼 꺼예요]) I'm going to watch
공부하다 (gongbuhada) 공부할 거예요 (gongbuhal kkeoyeyo [공부할 꺼예요]) I'm going to study
놀다 (nolda) 놀 거예요 (nol kkeoyeyo [놀 꺼예요]) I'm going to hang out
먹다 (meokta [먹따]) 먹을 거예요 (meogeul kkeoyeyo [머글 꺼예요]) I'm going to eat
읽다 (ikta [익따]) 읽을 거예요 (ilgeul kkeoyeyo [일글 꺼예요]) I'm going to read

Watch the hidden ㄲ

The spelling says 거, but after the ㄹ of this ending, 거 is always said with a tense ㄲ: 갈 거예요 sounds like [갈 꺼예요], never "gal geoyeyo". Revised Romanization doesn't show this, so we write gal kkeoyeyo to remind you. In fast speech 거예요 also often sounds like [거에요]; you'll even see people misspell it 거에요, but the correct spelling is 거예요.

Casually, with friends, drop 예요 and use 거야 (kkeoya): 뭐 할 거야? (Mwo hal kkeoya?) – What are you going to do?

Irregular verbs

The same verbs that change in the past change here too, because -을 starts with a vowel:

  • 듣다 (deutta [듣따]) – to listen → 들을 거예요 (deureul kkeoyeyo)
  • 걷다 (geotta [걷따]) – to walk → 걸을 거예요 (georeul kkeoyeyo)
  • 춥다 (chupta [춥따]) – to be cold → 추울 거예요 (chuul kkeoyeyo)
  • 덥다 (deopta [덥따]) – to be hot → 더울 거예요 (deoul kkeoyeyo)
  • 만들다 (mandeulda) – to make → 만들 거예요 (mandeul kkeoyeyo), not 만들을
  • 짓다 (jitta [짇따]) – to build → 지을 거예요 (jieul kkeoyeyo)

Plans and guesses

With "I" or "we", the ending usually means a plan: 내일 친구를 만날 거예요. (Naeil chingureul mannal kkeoyeyo.) – I'm going to meet a friend tomorrow.

With weather, other people or things you can't control, it's a guess:

  • 내일은 추울 거예요. (Naeireun chuul kkeoyeyo.) – It'll probably be cold tomorrow.
  • 친구는 벌써 도착했을 거예요. (Chinguneun beolsseo dochakaesseul kkeoyeyo. [친구는 벌써 도차캐쓸 꺼예요]) – My friend has probably already arrived.

That last sentence mixes both lessons: past -었 + -을 거예요 = "probably did".

Past, present, future side by side

Past Present Future
갔어요 (gasseoyo) – I went 가요 (gayo) – I go 갈 거예요 (gal kkeoyeyo) – I'll go
먹었어요 (meogeosseoyo) – I ate 먹어요 (meogeoyo) – I eat 먹을 거예요 (meogeul kkeoyeyo) – I'll eat
했어요 (haesseoyo) – I did 해요 (haeyo) – I do 할 거예요 (hal kkeoyeyo) – I'll do

Not the same as -(으)ㄹ게요

-(으)ㄹ게요 (-(eu)lkkeyo [ㄹ께요]) also looks like "will", but it's a promise or offer made to the listener, in reaction to them: 제가 할게요. (Jega halkkeyo. [제가 할께요]) – I'll do it (for you). Use 할 거예요 to state a plan, 할게요 to volunteer or promise. -(으)ㄹ게요 is only for "I", so a weather guess can never use it.

이번 주말에 ___(으)ㄹ 거예요. (Ibeon jumare (eu)l kkeoyeyo.) – This weekend I'm going to . Write three plans for your weekend, then on Monday turn them into the past tense.
