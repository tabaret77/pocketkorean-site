TOPIK II listening: how to handle 50 questions you can't rewind

2026-10-24

Phrase from this story: 듣기 = listening (test section)Search a word

In reading you can go back. In listening you can't. The TOPIK II listening section (듣기, deutkki [듣끼]) has 50 multiple-choice questions in 60 minutes and is worth 100 of the 300 points. It opens the 110-minute first period, followed straight away by writing. The good news: the paper follows the same pattern every time, so you can learn to stay one step ahead of the audio.

Once, then twice

The most useful fact is how often you hear each recording:

  • Questions 1–20 are short dialogues, and each one is played only once.
  • Questions 21–50 come in pairs: one longer recording, two questions, and the recording is played twice.

The first part tests quick reactions; the second tests whether you can follow a whole talk.

Questions What you hear Typical task
1–3 short dialogue or report pick the right picture or graph
4–8 short dialogue choose the next line
9–12 short dialogue what will the woman do next?
13–16 dialogue, notice, news which statement matches?
17–20 dialogue the man's main idea
21–30 work talk, phone calls, interviews details, reasons, guessing a job
31–40 debate, lecture, speech attitude, main point, what came before
41–50 lectures, documentaries main idea, method, speaker's attitude

Use every pause to read ahead

Between items there is a short pause while the next number is announced. That pause is your preview time, and it's more valuable than trying to "check" the last answer.

  1. During the opening instructions, look at the pictures for questions 1–3. Notice what differs between them: who is speaking, a number, a place.
  2. For questions 21–50, read both questions and all eight options before the recording starts. Underline the key word of each question: 이유 (iyu) – reason, 태도 (taedo) – attitude, 중심 생각 (jungsim saenggak) – main idea.
  3. On the first play of a pair, answer the "main idea" question. On the second play, check the detail question, because "which matches the content" options need exact facts.

Short notes, not sentences

You don't have time to write Korean sentences. Use the margin of the booklet for tiny notes: numbers, days, a tick or a cross next to options as you hear them, and arrows for "before/after". A note like 남: 반대 (nam: bandae) – man: against – is enough for a debate question. Mark the answer sheet as you go, so you start writing on time.

Common traps

  • The word you heard isn't the answer. Wrong options often reuse a word from the audio in a different meaning. Pick the option that matches the idea.
  • Changed plans. A speaker says "I was going to … but". The answer is what they'll do now: listen for 그런데 (geureonde) – but – and 그럼 (geureom) – then.
  • Polite refusals. 글쎄요 (geulsseyo) – well… – usually means no.

A three-week plan for the next test

The 109th paper-based TOPIK is on November 15, 2026, and the 16th computer test on November 28, so if you're registered you have three to five weeks (see our TOPIK levels, scores and test dates guide; always confirm your session on topik.go.kr).

  1. Week 1: do questions 1–20 from two past papers. Mark every item you missed because of speed.
  2. Week 2: do questions 21–50 with the transcript. Listen once, answer, then read the script and listen again without it.
  3. Week 3: two full listening sections under test conditions, each followed by 50 minutes of writing. Shadow one recording a day: pause and repeat each sentence aloud.

For the rest of the paper, see our plans for TOPIK II reading and writing question 54.

Korean you can use

Korean Romanization Meaning
듣기 deutkki [듣끼] listening (section)
듣다 deutta [듣따] to listen, to hear
대화 daehwa dialogue, conversation
강연 gangyeon lecture, talk
이유 iyu reason
태도 taedo attitude
중심 생각 jungsim saenggak main idea
다시 한번 들어 주세요 dasi hanbeon deureo juseyo Please listen once more
잘 못 들었어요 jal mot tteureosseoyo [잘 몯 뜨러써요] I didn't catch that

한 번만 듣고 답을 골라야 해요. (Han beonman deutkko dabeul gollaya haeyo. [한 번만 듣꼬 다블 골라야 해요]) – You have to choose the answer after hearing it only once. For questions 1–20, that's exactly the skill to train.
