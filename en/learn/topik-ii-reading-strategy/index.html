TOPIK II reading: how to finish 50 questions in 70 minutes

2026-10-23

Phrase from this story: 지문 = reading passage (in a test)Search a word

Ask anyone who has taken TOPIK II what went wrong in reading, and the answer is rarely "I didn't understand". It's usually "I ran out of time". The reading section (읽기, ilgi) has 50 multiple-choice questions, a 70-minute limit and is worth 100 of the test's 300 points. It comes in the second period, after you have already spent 110 minutes on listening and writing, so you start it tired. Here's how to plan those 70 minutes so the last ten questions don't become guesses.

The paper in three zones

The questions get longer as you go. A useful way to think about the paper is in three zones:

  • Questions 1–20: the fast start. Grammar blanks, "closest meaning" items, short ads, signs and notices, a graph or notice to match, putting sentences in order and short paragraphs with a blank.
  • Questions 21–34: the steady middle. Passages with an idiom, short stories where you judge a character's feeling, newspaper headlines to explain, longer blanks and factual "which matches the text" items.
  • Questions 35–50: the careful finish. Main idea, inserting a missing sentence, memoir-style texts, the author's attitude and the purpose of a text. These passages are dense and academic.

With 100 points spread over 50 questions, each one is worth about two points on average. A quick grammar blank near the start helps your score just as much as a hard academic passage at the end, which is why speed in the first zone matters so much.

A time plan that works

Divide the 70 minutes roughly like this, and practise with a timer until it feels natural:

Zone Questions Target time
Fast start 1–20 about 15 minutes
Steady middle 21–34 about 20 minutes
Careful finish 35–50 about 30 minutes
Checking and marking – about 5 minutes

If a question takes more than two minutes, pick your best answer, mark it in the booklet and move on. Coming back later with fresh eyes is faster than staring at it now.

Tactics for each question type

Read the question before the passage. The question tells you what to hunt for: 주제 (juje) – main idea, 제목 (jemok) – headline, 순서 (sunseo) – order, or 빈칸 (binkan) – blank. Only then read the 지문 (jimun) – passage.

Sentence order. Look at the connecting words. A sentence that starts with 그래서 (geuraeseo) – so – or 하지만 (hajiman) – but – can't come first. Words like 이것 (igeot) – this – also point back to something said earlier.

Newspaper headlines. Headlines drop particles and endings and use compressed vocabulary, like 급증 (geupjjeung [급쯩]) – sharp rise. Learn a set of common headline words; they come back test after test.

Blanks. Read the sentence before and after the blank. The answer usually repeats or contrasts an idea that is right next to it.

Main idea. The writer's point often comes in the last sentence, and it's often signalled by 따라서 (ttaraseo) – therefore – or -아야/어야 한다 (must, should).

"Matches the content" items. Wrong options usually change one detail: a number, a time word, or "always" instead of "sometimes". Check each option against the passage instead of trusting your memory.

How to practise

  1. Take a past paper and do only questions 1–20 in 15 minutes. Repeat until you finish on time.
  2. Keep a notebook of headline words and connecting words (그런데, 게다가, 반면에).
  3. Read one short Korean news article a day and summarise it in one Korean sentence. That trains the main-idea skill.
  4. Do at least two full reading sections under real time, after a long listening session, to feel the tiredness of test day.

If you are sitting the test this autumn, see our guide to TOPIK levels, scores and test dates for the remaining 2026 sessions, and always confirm your own session on topik.go.kr.

Korean you can use

Korean Romanization Meaning
읽기 ilkki [일끼] reading (section)
지문 jimun reading passage
빈칸 binkan blank
주제 juje main idea, topic
제목 jemok title, headline
순서 sunseo order
정답 jeongdap correct answer
시간이 부족해요 sigani bujokaeyo I don't have enough time
훑어보다 hulteoboda to skim

시간이 부족해서 마지막 문제를 못 풀었어요. (Sigani bujokaeseo majimak munjereul mot pureosseoyo.) – I didn't have enough time, so I couldn't solve the last question. With a good time plan, you won't need this sentence on test day.
