ㅐ, ㅘ, ㅢ: the 11 compound vowels of hangul

2026-10-24

Phrase from this story: 모음 = vowelSearch a word

Once you know the 10 basic vowels, the rest of hangul's vowels are a puzzle you can solve yourself. Korean has 11 more, called compound or double vowels (이중 모음, ijung moeum, is the usual name). Each one is two basic vowels pushed together, and the spelling tells you how to say it: put the two sounds side by side and say them quickly. 모음 (moeum) – vowel – is the word to know today.

The "plus ㅣ" group

Add a vertical ㅣ to a tall vowel and you get the four vowels of this group:

Built from Vowel Sound Example
ㅏ + ㅣ ㅐ e (as in "bed") 개 (gae) – dog
ㅓ + ㅣ ㅔ e (as in "bed") 게 (ge) – crab
ㅑ + ㅣ ㅒ ye 얘기 (yaegi) – story, chat
ㅕ + ㅣ ㅖ ye 예 (ye) – yes

ㅒ is rare: 얘 (yae, "this kid") and 얘기 are the words you'll meet most.

The "w" group

Start with ㅗ or ㅜ (lips rounded) and slide into a second vowel. The result begins with a "w" sound. In a block the round vowel sits under the consonant and the second vowel stands on the right: ㄱ + ㅘ = 과.

Built from Vowel Sound Example
ㅗ + ㅏ ㅘ wa 사과 (sagwa) – apple
ㅗ + ㅐ ㅙ we 왜 (wae) – why
ㅗ + ㅣ ㅚ we (or ö) 외국 (oeguk) – foreign country
ㅜ + ㅓ ㅝ wo 뭐 (mwo) – what
ㅜ + ㅔ ㅞ we 웨이터 (weiteo) – waiter
ㅜ + ㅣ ㅟ wi 귀 (gwi) – ear

The pairing follows a pattern: ㅗ combines with the "bright" vowels ㅏ and ㅐ, and ㅜ with the "dark" vowels ㅓ and ㅔ. You will never see ㅗ + ㅓ.

ㅐ and ㅔ: same sound, different spelling

In textbooks ㅐ is a bit more open than ㅔ, but most younger and middle-aged speakers in Seoul no longer make that difference; the National Institute of Korean Language notes they use one in-between sound for both. So 개 (dog) and 게 (crab) sound the same in everyday speech, and so do 내 (nae, my) and 네 (ne, your / yes). Context does the work, and when Koreans need to be clear when spelling a word aloud, they say "아이 애" or "어이 에".

The same thing happens to ㅙ, ㅚ and ㅞ: in normal speech 왜, 외 and 웨 all sound like "we". That's why even native speakers mix up the spellings 돼 and 되. For you, it means one sound to learn and three spellings to memorise word by word.

Another small shortcut: after a consonant, ㅖ may be said as plain ㅔ. 시계 (sigye) – clock – can be [시게], and 혜택 (hyetaek) – benefit – can be [헤택].

The three readings of ㅢ

ㅢ (ㅡ + ㅣ) is the one compound vowel with rules of its own:

  1. At the start of a word, with no consonant: say both parts, "eu-i" quickly. 의사 (uisa) – doctor, 의자 (uija) – chair.
  2. After a consonant: it becomes plain [ㅣ]. 희망 (huimang) – hope – is said [히망], and 띄어쓰기 (ttuieosseugi) – word spacing – is [띠어쓰기].
  3. The particle 의 ("of", "'s"): it is usually said [에]. 우리의 꿈 (uriui kkum) – our dream – sounds like [우리에 꿈].

Inside a word, after the first syllable, 의 may also be said [이]: 주의 (juui) – caution – can be [주이].
