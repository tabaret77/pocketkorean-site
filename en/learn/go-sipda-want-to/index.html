-고 싶다: how to say "I want to" in Korean

2026-10-22

Phrase from this story: 보고 싶어요 = I miss you / I want to see (it)Search a word

Once you can say what you do, the next thing you'll want to say is what you want to do. In Korean that job belongs to -고 싶다 (-go sipda). Attach it to any action verb and you have "want to": 가고 싶어요 (gago sipeoyo) – I want to go. It's one of the easiest patterns in Korean.

How to make it

Take the verb stem (the dictionary form minus -다) and add -고 싶다. There are no vowel rules and no difference between stems that end in a vowel or a consonant. Then conjugate 싶다 like any other verb – in polite speech it becomes 싶어요 (sipeoyo).

Dictionary form Stem + -고 싶어요 Meaning
가다 (gada) 가- 가고 싶어요 (gago sipeoyo) I want to go
먹다 (meokda) 먹- 먹고 싶어요 (meokgo sipeoyo) I want to eat
보다 (boda) 보- 보고 싶어요 (bogo sipeoyo) I want to see / I miss you
마시다 (masida) 마시- 마시고 싶어요 (masigo sipeoyo) I want to drink
쉬다 (swida) 쉬- 쉬고 싶어요 (swigo sipeoyo) I want to rest
살다 (salda) 살- 살고 싶어요 (salgo sipeoyo) I want to live
듣다 (deutda) 듣- 듣고 싶어요 (deutgo sipeoyo) I want to listen
공부하다 (gongbuhada) 공부하- 공부하고 싶어요 (gongbuhago sipeoyo) I want to study

Notice that even irregular verbs like 듣다 stay regular here: because -고 starts with a consonant, the stem doesn't change.

Asking and answering

The question form is the same sentence with a rising tone:

  • 뭐 하고 싶어요? (Mwo hago sipeoyo?) – What do you want to do?
  • 한국에 가고 싶어요. (Hanguge gago sipeoyo.) – I want to go to Korea.
  • 오늘은 집에서 쉬고 싶어요. (Oneureun jibeseo swigo sipeoyo.) – Today I want to rest at home.

With a direct object you can use either 을/를 or 이/가. Both are correct; 이/가 puts a little more weight on the thing you crave:

  • 떡볶이를 먹고 싶어요. / 떡볶이가 먹고 싶어요. (Tteokbokkireul / Tteokbokkiga meokgo sipeoyo.) – I want to eat tteokbokki.

Negative and past

To say you don't want to, you have two options:

  • 안 가고 싶어요. (An gago sipeoyo.) – I don't want to go. (short and casual-sounding)
  • 가고 싶지 않아요. (Gago sipji anayo.) – I don't want to go. (a little softer)

For the past, change only 싶다: 가고 싶었어요 (gago sipeosseoyo) – I wanted to go. 정말 보고 싶었어요! (Jeongmal bogo sipeosseoyo!) – I really missed you!

"I miss you" and other people's wishes

보고 싶어요 literally means "I want to see (you)", and it's the everyday way to say "I miss you". Between close friends or couples you'll hear the casual 보고 싶어 (bogo sipeo).

In Korean, you can't directly state what's in someone else's head, so -고 싶다 is mainly for I (in statements) and you (in questions). For a third person, use -고 싶어하다 (-go sipeohada) – "shows that they want to":

  • 동생이 한국어를 배우고 싶어해요. (Dongsaengi hangugeoreul baeugo sipeohaeyo.) – My younger sibling wants to learn Korean.

A common mistake

-고 싶다 attaches to verbs, not nouns. "I want coffee" can't be 커피 싶어요 – that's wrong. Add a verb: 커피 마시고 싶어요 (keopi masigo sipeoyo) – I want to drink coffee. To want an object, use 갖고 싶어요 (gatgo sipeoyo) – I want to have it: 새 가방을 갖고 싶어요. – I want a new bag.

Mini practice

Make "I want to …" sentences. Answers below.

  1. 자다 (to sleep)
  2. 만나다 (to meet)
  3. 여행하다 (to travel)
  4. 읽다 (to read) – negative
  5. 배우다 (to learn) – past

Answers: 1. 자고 싶어요 (jago sipeoyo) 2. 만나고 싶어요 (mannago sipeoyo) 3. 여행하고 싶어요 (yeohaenghago sipeoyo) 4. 읽고 싶지 않아요 (ilkko sipji anayo) 5. 배우고 싶었어요 (baeugo sipeosseoyo)
