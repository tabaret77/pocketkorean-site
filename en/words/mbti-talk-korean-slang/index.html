"너 T야?": how Koreans talk in MBTI

2026-10-23

Phrase from this story: 너 T야? = Are you a T? (i.e. Why so unsympathetic?)Search a word

Meet a few young Koreans and, sooner or later, someone will ask: MBTI가 어떻게 되세요? (MBTI-ga eotteoke doeseyo?) – "What's your MBTI?" The four-letter personality types have become everyday small talk in Korea, and the letters themselves have turned into slang. A Yonhap report from 20 October 2026 notes that the question is now a common way to break the ice with someone new. Here's the vocabulary you need to follow along.

Reading the letters

Koreans say the letters in English: 엠비티아이 (embitiai). Each letter becomes a quick label:

  • I (아이, ai) – introvert; the Korean word is 내향형 (naehyanghyeong)
  • E (이, i) – extrovert; 외향형 (oehyanghyeong)
  • T (티, ti) – thinking type
  • F (에프, epeu) – feeling type

So you'll hear simple sentences like 저 I예요 (Jeo aiyeyo) – "I'm an introvert" – or 완전 E네! (Wanjeon ine!) – "You're such an extrovert!"

너 T야? – Are you a T?

The most famous MBTI meme is 너 T야? (Neo tiya?). It's said when someone answers an emotional story with cold logic. Your friend says they're sad because their plant died, and you reply, "Did you water it too much?" – that's when you get 너 T야?

The Hankyoreh 21 traced the meme to a sketch series by the YouTube comedy channel 밈고리즘 (Mimgorithm), in which feeling-type characters snap "T야?" at an unsentimental friend. People often make a T shape with their fingers as they say it. The line spread so far that, as The Korea Herald reported in 2024, Baskin Robbins Korea named an Earl Grey ice cream flavour "Are You Tea?"

The underlying idea is 공감 (gonggam) – empathy. A T is joked to lack it; an F is said to be full of it. Note that "T" here has drifted from its original meaning (thinking style) to simply "unsympathetic", which is why some T types find the meme a bit unfair.

대문자 E and 극I – the extremes

To say someone is very much one type, Koreans use two tricks:

  • 대문자 (daemunja) – capital letter. 대문자 E (daemunja i) is a super-extrovert, as if their E were written in capitals. The Hankyoreh 21 article lists it among popular MBTI terms.
  • 극- (geuk-) – extreme. 극I (geugai) is a strong introvert who loves time alone and recharges at home. You'll hear 저 극I라서… (Jeo geugairaseo…) – "I'm a hardcore introvert, so…" – as an excuse to skip a party.

Using it in conversation

  • MBTI가 뭐예요? (MBTI-ga mwoyeyo?) – What's your MBTI? (simpler)
  • 혹시 F세요? (Hoksi epeuseyo?) – Are you an F, by any chance? Often said when someone tears up at a film.
  • 저 I인데 오늘은 E 모드예요. (Jeo ainde oneureun i modeuyeyo.) – I'm an introvert, but today I'm in E mode.

A word of caution

MBTI talk is fun, but not everyone joins in. In the same Yonhap report, a Hankook Research survey found only 18% of people aged 60 and over had taken an MBTI test, so older Koreans may not follow the jokes. The head of a Korean culture institute quoted there also warned that boxing people into types can feed stereotypes. The types are not evenly spread, either: a Korean data set cited by The Korea Herald found far more F types than T types among Korean test-takers.

Keep 너 T야? for friends; with a colleague or someone older, it can come across as rude.

Korean you can use

Korean Romanization Meaning
MBTI가 어떻게 되세요? MBTI-ga eotteoke doeseyo? What's your MBTI? (polite)
너 T야? neo tiya? Are you a T? / Why so cold?
공감 gonggam empathy
내향형 naehyanghyeong introvert
외향형 oehyanghyeong extrovert
대문자 E daemunja i a total extrovert
극I geugai an extreme introvert
저 I예요 jeo aiyeyo I'm an introvert

Useful pattern: 저 극I라서 주말엔 집에 있어요. (Jeo geugairaseo jumaren jibe isseoyo.) – I'm a hardcore introvert, so I stay home at weekends. -(이)라서 gives the reason: swap in any noun, like 학생이라서 (hakssaengiraseo [학쌩이라서]) – because I'm a student.
