K-drama apology lines: from 미안해 to 우리 화해하자

2026-10-23

Phrase from this story: 화 풀어 = Don't be mad anymore / Let it goSearch a word

If the confession is the high point of a K-drama romance, the fight is the low point that always follows around episode ten. Someone keeps a secret, someone sees the wrong text, and suddenly two people who were holding hands are standing in the rain not speaking. Then comes the apology. The lines are short, emotional and very reusable – with friends, siblings and partners in real life too.

Like most drama dialogue between close characters, these lines are in casual speech (반말, banmal). We add the polite versions where you might need them.

1. 미안해 – I'm sorry

미안해 (mianhae) is the basic casual apology, from 미안하다 (mianhada). Politely it's 미안해요 (mianhaeyo), and to someone older or in a formal situation, 죄송합니다 (joesonghamnida). In dramas, you'll often hear it stretched out and repeated: 미안해, 진짜 미안해 (Mianhae, jinjja mianhae) – I'm sorry, I'm really sorry.

2. 내가 잘못했어 – It was my fault

내가 잘못했어 (Naega jalmotaesseo) literally means "I did wrong". 잘못하다 (jalmotada) is to make a mistake or do something wrong. Saying it out loud is a bigger step than 미안해, because it admits responsibility. Note the pronunciation: 잘못했어 is said [잘모태써], as ㅅ (sounding ㄷ) and ㅎ merge into ㅌ.

3. 내 말 좀 들어 봐 – Just hear me out

내 말 좀 들어 봐 (Nae mal jom deureo bwa) is what the character with the misunderstood explanation says, usually while the other person walks away. 들어 보다 (deureo boda) means "to try listening", and 좀 (jom) softens the request. Politely: 제 말 좀 들어 보세요 (Je mal jom deureo boseyo).

4. 아직도 화났어? – Are you still mad?

아직도 화났어? (Ajikto hwanasseo? [아직또 화나써?]) checks whether the storm has passed. 화나다 (hwanada) means to get angry, and the past tense 화났어 describes the state of being angry now. The sulky answer is often 됐어 (dwaesseo) – "forget it" – which means the apology needs more work.

5. 화 풀어 – Don't be mad anymore

화 풀어 (Hwa pureo) literally means "untie your anger". 화 (hwa) is anger and 풀다 (pulda) is to untie or loosen, so it asks the other person to let it go. It's often said with a little pleading tone or a small gift in hand. Politely: 화 푸세요 (Hwa puseyo). When the anger is gone, Koreans say 화가 풀렸어 (Hwaga pullyeosseo) – "I'm not mad anymore."

6. 다시는 안 그럴게 – I won't do it again

다시는 안 그럴게 (Dasineun an geureolge) is the promise part of the apology. 그러다 (geureoda) means "to do that", and the ending -(으)ㄹ게 makes a promise to the listener. The -는 on 다시 adds emphasis: "never again".

7. 한 번만 봐줘 – Let it go just this once

한 번만 봐줘 (Han beonman bwajwo) asks for a second chance. 봐주다 (bwajuda) here means to overlook or go easy on someone. It's playful between friends, desperate in a drama. You'll also hear it from a younger sibling caught doing something.

8. 사과 받아 줄게 – I'll accept your apology

사과 받아 줄게 (Sagwa bada julge) is the reply that means the fight is nearly over. 사과 (sagwa) is an apology (and also an apple), and 받아 주다 (bada juda) is "to accept, as a favour". The 줄게 adds a slightly superior "I'll do you this favour" tone, which is why it's often followed by a smile. The more sincere version: 나도 미안해 (Nado mianhae) – "I'm sorry too."

9. 우리 화해하자 – Let's make up

우리 화해하자 (Uri hwahaehaja) ends the episode. 화해하다 (hwahaehada) means to make peace or reconcile, and -자 is "let's". In real life, Koreans often seal it with food: 맛있는 거 먹으러 가자 (Masinneun geo meogeureo gaja) – "Let's go eat something good."

Real-life note

A real Korean apology often depends on hierarchy more than drama does. With friends, 미안해 plus 내가 잘못했어 is plenty. With an older person or at work, use 죄송합니다 and keep it simple; lines like 화 풀어 sound too close and casual there.

Useful pattern: 늦어서 미안해. 다음부터는 안 그럴게. (Neujeoseo mianhae. Daeumbuteoneun an geureolge.) – Sorry I'm late. I won't do it again. Put the reason before -아/어서 미안해 to say what you're sorry for: 연락 못 해서 미안해 (Yeollak mot haeseo mianhae) – sorry I didn't get in touch.
