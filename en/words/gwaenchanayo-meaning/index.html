괜찮아요: I'm fine, it's okay, no thanks and not bad

2026-10-21

Phrase from this story: 괜찮아요 = it's okay / I'm fineSearch a word

If you watch any Korean drama, you'll hear it within minutes: 괜찮아요 (gwaenchanayo). It comes from the adjective 괜찮다 (gwaenchanta) – to be okay, to be fine. But depending on the situation, it can mean "I'm fine", "no problem", "no thanks" or even "pretty good". Here is how to tell which one is meant, and how to use it yourself.

How to say it

The ㅎ in 괜찮아요 is silent, so it's pronounced [괜차나요], roughly "gwen-cha-na-yo". Don't try to say the ㅎ; it softens away completely between vowels.

1. "I'm fine" / "Are you okay?"

The most basic meaning. With a rising tone, it's a question; with a falling tone, it's an answer.

  • A: 괜찮아요? (Gwaenchanayo?) – Are you okay?
  • B: 네, 괜찮아요. (Ne, gwaenchanayo.) – Yes, I'm fine.

When asking about someone older or someone you want to show respect to, add the honorific -으시-: 괜찮으세요? (Gwaenchaneuseyo?) – Are you all right? This is what you'd say if a stranger trips on the street.

2. "It's okay" / "No problem"

When someone apologises, 괜찮아요 is the natural reply.

  • A: 늦어서 죄송해요. (Neujeoseo joesonghaeyo.) – Sorry I'm late.
  • B: 괜찮아요. (Gwaenchanayo.) – That's okay.

You can make it warmer with 아니에요, 괜찮아요 (Anieyo, gwaenchanayo) – "No, no, it's fine."

3. "No thanks"

This is the one that trips up learners. In Korean, 괜찮아요 is a soft, polite way to decline an offer.

  • A: 봉투 필요하세요? (Bongtu piryohaseyo?) – Do you need a bag?

  • B: 아니요, 괜찮아요. (Aniyo, gwaenchanayo.) – No, I'm fine (no thanks).

  • A: 더 드세요. (Deo deuseyo.) – Have some more.

  • B: 괜찮아요, 배불러요. (Gwaenchanayo, baebulleoyo.) – I'm fine, I'm full.

Here it works much like English "I'm good" or "I'm fine, thanks". If there's any doubt, adding 아니요 (aniyo) – no – in front makes your meaning clear.

4. "Not bad" / "Pretty good"

As a description, 괜찮다 means something is decent, acceptable or even quite good. It's understated praise.

  • 이 식당 괜찮아요. (I sikdang gwaenchanayo.) – This restaurant is pretty good.
  • 그 사람 괜찮아요. (Geu saram gwaenchanayo.) – He/She is a decent person (or: quite attractive, depending on context).

Before a noun, it becomes 괜찮은 (gwaenchaneun): 괜찮은 카페 (gwaenchaneun kape) – a nice café.

5. Asking for permission

Combine it with -아도/어도 (even if) to ask whether something is okay.

  • 여기 앉아도 괜찮아요? (Yeogi anjado gwaenchanayo?) – Is it okay if I sit here?
  • 사진 찍어도 괜찮아요? (Sajin jjigeodo gwaenchanayo?) – Is it okay to take a photo?

The answer is simply 네, 괜찮아요 (Ne, gwaenchanayo) – yes, that's fine.

Polite, casual, formal

Like most Korean words, 괜찮다 changes with the level of politeness:

  • 괜찮아 (gwaenchana) – casual, with friends and younger people
  • 괜찮아요 (gwaenchanayo) – polite, the everyday default
  • 괜찮습니다 (gwaenchanseumnida) – formal, for work, customers and announcements

A caring friend might say 괜찮아? (Gwaenchana?) – "You okay?" – and you can answer 응, 괜찮아 (Eung, gwaenchana) – "Yeah, I'm fine."

When "I'm fine" isn't fine

As in English, tone matters. A short, flat 괜찮아요 with no eye contact can mean the opposite. In dramas, a friend often answers with 안 괜찮아 보이는데? (An gwaenchana boineunde?) – "You don't look okay, though."

Listen for 괜찮아요 the next time you watch a Korean show, and try to guess which of the four meanings it has. Context almost always gives it away.
