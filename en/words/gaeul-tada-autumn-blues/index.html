가을 타다: the Korean way to say you've got the autumn blues

2026-10-23

Phrase from this story: 가을 타다 = to feel low or wistful in autumnSearch a word

Late October in Korea is beautiful: maple leaves on every mountain, clear blue skies, crisp mornings. And yet, right around now, you'll hear friends sigh and say 나 가을 타나 봐 (Na gaeul tana bwa) – "I think I've got the autumn blues." The expression is 가을 타다 (gaeul tada), and it's one of the most seasonal phrases in Korean.

What it means

가을 (gaeul) is autumn, and 타다 (tada) usually means "to ride" (a bus, a bike) or "to burn". But 타다 has another meaning: to be easily affected by a season or the weather. So 가을 타다 is to be touched by autumn – to feel a little lonely, wistful, restless or down when the days get shorter.

It's a light, everyday phrase, not a medical term. People say it when they feel like staring out of a café window, listening to sad ballads or texting an old friend for no reason. Some say it half as a joke, to explain a sudden mood.

There may be something real behind it, though. A Weekly Kyunghyang article from 10 October 2026 explains that less daylight in autumn may affect serotonin, a brain chemical linked to mood, and melatonin, which controls the sleep cycle. That can leave people sluggish in the day and gloomy in the evening. Its advice: get outside for morning sunlight, exercise regularly and keep regular mealtimes – and talk to a professional if low moods last a long time or come back every year.

How to use it

The most common forms are:

  • 가을 타나 봐 (gaeul tana bwa) – I think I'm feeling the autumn blues. The ending -나 보다 means "it seems that".
  • 가을 타요? (Gaeul tayo?) – Are you feeling a bit down with the season? (polite)
  • 저는 가을을 많이 타요 (Jeoneun gaeureul mani tayo) – I always get moody in autumn. With the object marker it becomes 가을을 타다 (gaeureul tada).
  • 요즘 가을 타서 그래 (Yojeum gaeul taseo geurae) – It's just the season getting to me lately.

A typical exchange between friends:

A: 왜 이렇게 조용해? (Wae ireoke joyonghae?) – Why are you so quiet? B: 몰라, 가을 타나 봐. (Molla, gaeul tana bwa.) – Dunno, I think it's the autumn thing. A: 그럼 산책이라도 하자! (Geureom sanchaegirado haja!) – Then let's at least go for a walk!

Its cousins: 추위를 타다 and 더위를 타다

The same 타다 works with weather words, and these are very practical as the temperature drops:

  • 추위를 타다 (chuwireul tada) – to feel the cold easily. 저는 추위를 많이 타요 (Jeoneun chuwireul mani tayo) – I really feel the cold.
  • 더위를 타다 (deowireul tada) – to suffer in the heat.
  • 봄을 타다 (bomeul tada) – to feel restless or low in spring.

So if your Korean friend keeps the window open while you're shivering, you can explain: 제가 추위를 좀 타요 (Jega chuwireul jom tayo) – "I get cold easily."

Don't mix it up with 썸 타다 (sseom tada), a different, newer use of 타다: being in a "something" phase with someone, close but not yet dating.

Words for the mood

If 가을 타다 isn't specific enough, these help describe the feeling:

  • 쓸쓸하다 (sseulsseulhada) – lonely, desolate (a perfect autumn word)
  • 외롭다 (oeropta [외롭따]) – lonely
  • 우울하다 (uulhada) – depressed, gloomy
  • 기분이 가라앉다 (gibuni garaanda) – to feel down (literally "the mood sinks")
