From 해 줘 to 해 주실 수 있으세요?: five levels of asking in Korean

2026-10-24

Phrase from this story: 해 주시겠어요? = Would you do it (for me)?Search a word

Asking for something is where politeness matters most. "Close the window" and "I'm so sorry, but would you mind closing the window?" ask for the same thing, but they land very differently. Korean has the same range, built on one helpful verb: 주다 (juda) – to give. Add it after another verb as -아/어 주다 and it means "do something for me". From there, Korean climbs a ladder of five steps.

We'll use one example all the way up: 창문 좀 닫다 (changmun jom datta [닫따]) – to close the window.

The ladder

1. 닫아 줘 (Dada jwo) – Close it (for me). A plain casual request. Fine with close friends, siblings and a partner, but a bit blunt even with them if you're asking a real favour.

2. 닫아 줄래? (Dada jullae?) – Will you close it? Still casual, but a question, so it sounds friendlier. The ending -(으)ㄹ래? asks what the listener wants to do, so the other person gets a choice. This is the nicest way to ask a friend.

3. 닫아 주세요 (Dada juseyo) – Please close it. Polite and very common: with shop staff, taxi drivers, classmates and anyone you're not close to. Note that it's still a command, just a polite one.

4. 닫아 주시겠어요? (Dada jusigesseoyo? [다다 주시게써요]) – Would you close it? A polite question with -시- (respect) and -겠- (here, a gentle "would"). It's the go-to form for strangers, customers and anyone older, and sounds courteous rather than stiff.

5. 닫아 주실 수 있으세요? (Dada jusil su isseuseyo? [다다 주실 쑤 이쓰세요]) – Could you possibly close it? It asks whether the person is able to, which leaves room to say no. Use it for real favours or when you're interrupting someone. Note the tensification: -ㄹ 수 is said [ㄹ 쑤]. Many speakers soften it even further with -(으)ㄹ까요?: 닫아 주실 수 있을까요? (Dada jusil su isseulkkayo? [다다 주실 쑤 이쓸까요]) – "Do you think you could…?"

Softeners: 좀, 혹시, 죄송한데

Koreans rarely make a request bare. Three small words do a lot of work:

  • 좀 (jom) – "a bit, please". Short for 조금, it makes almost any request smoother: 사진 좀 찍어 주세요 (Sajin jom jjigeo juseyo [사진 좀 찌거 주세요]) – "Could you take a photo, please?"
  • 혹시 (hoksi [혹씨]) – "by any chance". It turns a request into a gentle question: 혹시 자리 좀 바꿔 주실 수 있으세요? (Hoksi jari jom bakkwo jusil su isseuseyo? [혹씨 자리 좀 바꿔 주실 쑤 이쓰세요]) – "Could you possibly swap seats with me?"
  • 죄송한데(요) (joesonghande(yo)) – "I'm sorry, but…". The ending -ㄴ데 sets up what comes next, so it's an apology that leads into the favour: 죄송한데 조금만 조용히 해 주시겠어요? (Joesonghande jogeumman joyonghi hae jusigesseoyo? [해 주시게써요]) – "Sorry, would you mind keeping it down a little?"

Which one when?

On the train: You want someone to move their bag so you can sit. You: 저기, 죄송한데 가방 좀 치워 주시겠어요? (Jeogi, joesonghande gabang jom chiwo jusigesseoyo?) – Excuse me, sorry, would you mind moving your bag? Passenger: 아, 네. 죄송해요. (A, ne. Joesonghaeyo.) – Oh, sure. Sorry.

With a friend: You're carrying shopping bags. You: 문 좀 열어 줄래? (Mun jom yeoreo jullae?) – Can you get the door? Friend: 응, 내가 들어 줄게. (Eung, naega deureo julge. [들어 줄께]) – Sure, I'll carry some for you.

With your manager: You need a deadline moved. You: 혹시 마감을 하루만 늦춰 주실 수 있을까요? (Hoksi magameul haruman neutchwo jusil su isseulkkayo? [혹씨 마가믈 하루만 늗춰 주실 쑤 이쓸까요]) – Could you possibly push the deadline back one day?

A rough rule: the bigger the favour and the higher the person, the further up the ladder you go and the more softeners you add. But don't overdo it with small things: ordering a coffee with 주실 수 있으세요? can sound oddly stiff, and 주세요 is perfectly polite there.

Korean you can use

Korean Romanization Meaning
닫아 줘 dada jwo Close it (casual)
닫아 줄래? dada jullae? Will you close it? (casual, friendly)
닫아 주세요 dada juseyo Please close it
닫아 주시겠어요? dada jusigesseoyo? [다다 주시게써요] Would you close it?
닫아 주실 수 있으세요? dada jusil su isseuseyo? [다다 주실 쑤 이쓰세요] Could you possibly close it?
좀 jom a bit, please (softener)
혹시 hoksi [혹씨] by any chance
죄송한데요 joesonghandeyo I'm sorry, but…
문 좀 열어 줄래? mun jom yeoreo jullae? Can you get the door?

Useful pattern: 혹시 ~ 주시겠어요? (Hoksi ~ jusigesseoyo?) – Would you mind ~ for me, by any chance? Fill the gap with a verb in the -아/어 form, such as 사진 좀 찍어 (sajin jom jjigeo) or 자리 좀 바꿔 (jari jom bakkwo): 혹시 사진 좀 찍어 주시겠어요? (Hoksi sajin jom jjigeo jusigesseoyo?) – Would you mind taking a photo for me? That's a polite request for almost any situation.
