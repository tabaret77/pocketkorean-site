눈치: the Korean art of reading the room

2026-10-24

Phrase from this story: 눈치 = sensing the mood; reading the roomSearch a word

Your team leader sighs, looks at the clock and says nothing. A friend answers "괜찮아" a little too quickly. Your host mother keeps refilling your bowl. In each case, Koreans expect you to pick up what isn't being said. The word for that skill is 눈치 (nunchi), and once you know it, you'll hear it everywhere.

What 눈치 means

The National Institute of Korean Language's learner dictionary gives two senses. The first is the ability to sense someone's real feelings or a situation even when nobody puts it into words – close to English "tact", "a sixth sense" or "reading the room". The second is the look or attitude that gives those feelings away, as in 불안해하는 눈치 (buranhaehaneun nunchi) – "a worried look".

The first syllable is 눈 (nun), the eye. According to the Institute, a 17th-century form, 눈츼, is thought to be 눈 plus an old suffix, though the ending is no longer used on its own today. So 눈치 really is something you do with your eyes.

The five phrases you need

눈치가 빠르다 (nunchiga ppareuda) – to be quick to pick things up. A compliment: this person notices before you explain.

눈치가 없다 (nunchiga eoptta [눈치가 업따]) – to be clueless about the mood. Said about the friend who talks loudly about their holiday right after you've lost your job. The casual 눈치 없어! (Nunchi eopseo! [눈치 업써!]) is a teasing jab.

눈치(를) 보다 (nunchi(reul) boda) – to watch how others feel and hold back because of it. Often it's a little uncomfortable: 눈치 보여 (nunchi boyeo) means "I feel awkward, I feel watched".

눈치채다 (nunchichaeda) – to notice, to catch on. The Institute says it's written as one word. 벌써 눈치챘어? (Beolsseo nunchichaesseo? [벌써 눈치채써?]) – "You've already figured it out?"

눈치껏 (nunchikkeot) – using your judgement, "read the situation and do what's right". The suffix -껏 means "as far as it goes", as in 마음껏 (maeumkkeot), to your heart's content.

At work

A: 팀장님 오늘 기분 안 좋으신 것 같아. (Timjangnim oneul gibun an joeusin geot gata.) – I think the team leader's in a bad mood today. B: 응, 눈치 보여서 회의 때 말을 못 하겠어. (Eung, nunchi boyeoseo hoeui ttae mareul mot hagesseo. [모 타게써]) – Yeah, I feel too awkward to speak up in the meeting.

New employees are often told 눈치껏 해 (nunchikkeot hae) – "just use your judgement" – which can be very hard when you're new and nobody explains the rules.

At school

A: 민수가 지아 좋아하는 거, 다 알아. (Minsuga Jia joahaneun geo, da ara.) – Everyone knows Minsu likes Jia. B: 진짜? 지아만 아직 눈치 못 챈 거야? (Jinjja? Jiaman ajik nunchi mot chaen geoya? [몯 챈 거야]) – Really? So Jia's the only one who hasn't noticed?

Here 눈치채다 is split by 못 (mot, can't): 눈치 못 채다, "to fail to notice".

At home

A: 엄마, 나 용돈 좀… (Eomma, na yongdon jom…) – Mum, could I have a bit of pocket money… B: 너 눈치가 빠르네. 아빠 월급날인 거 알았지? (Neo nunchiga ppareune. Appa wolgeumnarin geo aratji? [월금나린 거 아랃찌]) – You're quick. You knew it was Dad's payday, didn't you?

Good 눈치, too much 눈치

Having 눈치 is a real social skill in Korea, and saying 눈치가 빠르시네요 (nunchiga ppareusineyo) is a kind thing to tell a colleague. But constantly watching others is tiring, so friends also say 눈치 보지 마 (nunchi boji ma) – "don't worry about what others think" – to encourage someone to do what they want.

Korean you can use

Korean Romanization Meaning
눈치 nunchi sensing the mood; reading the room
눈치가 빠르다 nunchiga ppareuda to be quick to pick things up
눈치가 없다 nunchiga eoptta [눈치가 업따] to be clueless about the mood
눈치(를) 보다 nunchi(reul) boda to watch others' reactions, to feel self-conscious
눈치 보여 nunchi boyeo I feel awkward / I feel watched
눈치채다 nunchichaeda to notice, to catch on
눈치 못 챘어 nunchi mot chaesseo [눈치 몯 채써] I didn't notice
눈치껏 nunchikkeot using your judgement
눈치 보지 마 nunchi boji ma Don't worry about others

Useful pattern: 눈치 보여서 못 하겠어요. (Nunchi boyeoseo mot hagesseoyo. [모 타게써요]) – I feel too awkward to do it. Put any action before 못 하겠어요, such as 먼저 못 가겠어요 (meonjeo mot gagesseoyo [몯 까게써요]) – I can't bring myself to leave first.
