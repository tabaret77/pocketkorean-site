칼퇴, 회식, 불금: Korean office slang every worker knows

2026-10-24

Phrase from this story: 칼퇴 = leaving work right on timeSearch a word

Korean offices have their own little language. Listen to colleagues in the lift or read the comments under any video about work, and you'll hear words that sum up the whole working week in two syllables. Most of them are a mix of complaint and humour, the kind of thing workers say to each other with a wry smile. Here are the ones you're most likely to meet.

First, the basic word: a salaried office worker is a 직장인 (jikjangin [직짱인]), from 직장 (jikjang), workplace. Going to work is 출근 (chulgeun), and leaving is 퇴근 (toegeun).

Leaving on time: 칼퇴

칼퇴 (kaltoe) is short for 칼퇴근 (kaltoegeun) – leaving work exactly at closing time. 칼 (kal) means knife, and 칼같이 (kalgachi) means "precisely, sharp on time", so 칼퇴 is clocking off "on the knife's edge" of six o'clock. A Seoul city survey article from 2014 already used it in its headline as the everyday word for 정시 퇴근, leaving on time.

  • 오늘은 칼퇴할 거예요. (Oneureun kaltoehal kkeoyeyo. [칼퇴할 꺼예요]) – I'm leaving right on time today.

Overtime and 각

The standard word for working late is 야근 (yageun), literally "night work". The job site Saramin's list of office buzzwords for 2017 included 야근각 (yageungak [야근깍]): "it looks like overtime again". 각 (gak) here means "it's shaping up to be", and it attaches to all sorts of words. Its happy opposite is 퇴근각 (toegeungak [퇴근깍]) – "looks like we can go home": the boss has left, the report is sent.

  • 보고서 끝! 퇴근각이다. (Bogoseo kkeut! Toegeungagida. [퇴근까기다]) – Report done! Home time.

Saramin's 2016 list also had 프로야근러 (peuroyageunreo) – a "pro overtimer", someone who works late as a habit.

Team dinners and Friday: 회식, 불금

회식 (hoesik) is a company dinner with your team, often over barbecue or a big shared meal. It's standard vocabulary, not slang, but you'll hear it constantly: 오늘 회식 있어요? (Oneul hoesik isseoyo? [회식 이써요]) – "Is there a team dinner today?"

불금 (bulgeum) is short for 불타는 금요일 (bultaneun geumyoil) – "burning Friday", a Friday night spent letting off the week's steam. 불금 보내세요! (Bulgeum bonaeseyo!) – "Have a great Friday night!" – is a friendly thing to say to colleagues as you leave.

Self-mocking words: 월급루팡, 사축

월급루팡 (wolgeumnupang [월금누팡]) combines 월급 (wolgeup), monthly salary, with 루팡, Arsène Lupin, the fictional gentleman thief. The Kyunghyang Shinmun explained it back in 2011 as someone who "steals" a salary while doing little work. People mostly use it about themselves as a joke: 오늘 나 완전 월급루팡이야 (Oneul na wanjeon wolgeumnupangiya) – "I'm totally a salary thief today."

사축 (sachuk) mixes 회사 (hoesa), company, and 가축 (gachuk), livestock: a worker who feels like the company's farm animal. Saramin named it the buzzword office workers related to most in 2016. It's self-deprecating, so use it about yourself, never about a colleague.

넵 – the "yes" of the office chat

In work messengers, many people answer 넵 (nep) instead of 네 (ne). Saramin's 2017 list called the habit 넵병 (nepbyeong [넵뼝]), "넵 disease", because 넵 feels quicker and keener. You'll also see 넵넵 and 넵! in group chats.

Korean you can use

Korean Romanization Meaning
직장인 jikjangin [직짱인] office worker
칼퇴 kaltoe leaving right on time
야근 yageun overtime, working late
퇴근각 toegeungak [퇴근깍] looks like home time
회식 hoesik team dinner
불금 bulgeum Friday night out
월급루팡 wolgeumnupang [월금누팡] salary thief (joking)
사축 sachuk company "livestock" (self-mocking)
넵 nep yes (eager, in work chats)

These words are casual. They're fine with work friends and online, but in a meeting with your manager, stick to standard words like 퇴근 and 야근.

Useful pattern: 오늘은 칼퇴하고 싶어요. (Oneureun kaltoehago sipeoyo. [칼퇴하고 시퍼요]) – I want to leave right on time today. Swap in 야근 안 하고 싶어요 (yageun an hago sipeoyo) – I don't want to do overtime.
