화이팅: how Koreans cheer someone on before a big day

2026-10-22

Phrase from this story: 화이팅 = You can do it! / Go for it!Search a word

Late autumn in Korea is exam season. On Thursday 19 November 2026, high-school seniors sit 수능 (Suneung), the national university entrance exam, as of October 2026. In the weeks before, one word is everywhere: on banners outside schools, in group chats, on convenience-store snack packs and in every parent's goodbye at the door. That word is 화이팅 (hwaiting).

It comes from English "fighting", but it doesn't mean a fight. It means "You can do it!", "Go for it!" or "Good luck!" – a burst of encouragement before something hard.

Two spellings: 화이팅 and 파이팅

You'll see it written two ways. The standard dictionary spelling is 파이팅 (paiting), because Korean usually writes English "f" with ㅍ. In daily life, though, 화이팅 (hwaiting) is at least as common, especially in texts and on signs. Both are understood instantly, and both are pronounced in roughly the same cheerful way.

The National Institute of Korean Language has tried to offer native Korean alternatives over the years, such as 아자 (aja) and 힘내자 (himnaeja) – "let's find strength". 아자 is still shouted in sports and on TV, often doubled up as 아자 아자 화이팅! (Aja aja hwaiting!), but 화이팅 remains the everyday favourite.

How to use it

The simplest way is a name or a group plus 화이팅:

  • 민지 화이팅! (Minji hwaiting!) – Go, Minji!
  • 우리 팀 화이팅! (Uri tim hwaiting!) – Come on, team!
  • 수능 화이팅! (Suneung hwaiting!) – Good luck on the Suneung!

You can also add it after a more specific wish:

  • 시험 잘 봐! 화이팅! (Siheom jal bwa! Hwaiting!) – Do well on the exam! You've got this!
  • 오늘 면접 화이팅! (Oneul myeonjeop hwaiting!) – Good luck with today's interview!

Here 잘 보다 (jal boda) – literally "to see well" – is how Koreans say "to do well on a test". It's one of the most useful exam phrases you can learn.

Koreans also use it with a raised fist, often with both hands, or as the final shout when a group puts their hands together in a circle before a game.

With older people and at work

화이팅 is friendly and slightly casual. With friends, classmates and younger colleagues, it's perfect. With a teacher, a boss or an older person, it can sound a little light, as if you're coaching them. Warmer, more polite options are:

  • 힘내세요 (himnaeseyo) – Keep your strength up / Hang in there.
  • 응원할게요 (eungwonhalgeyo) – I'm rooting for you.
  • 좋은 결과 있으실 거예요 (joeun gyeolgwa isseusil geoyeyo) – I'm sure you'll get a good result.

힘내세요 also works when someone is going through a hard time, where a cheerful 화이팅 might feel out of place.

Suneung season extras

Around the exam you'll hear a few more lines. 수능 대박 나세요! (Suneung daebak naseyo!) wishes someone a "jackpot" result. Families and friends give students sticky foods like 엿 (yeot), a chewy toffee, or 찹쌀떡 (chapssaltteok), sticky rice cake, because 붙다 (butda) means both "to stick" and "to pass an exam". The wish is 꼭 붙어라! (Kkok buteora!) – "Make sure you pass!"

Korean you can use

Korean Romanization Meaning

시험 잘 보세요 siheom jal boseyo Do well on the exam (polite)
힘내세요 himnaeseyo Hang in there
응원할게요 eungwonhalgeyo I'm rooting for you
아자 아자 aja aja Come on! (cheer)

Useful pattern: 내일 시험 잘 봐요, 화이팅! (Naeil siheom jal bwayo, hwaiting!) – Good luck on tomorrow's exam, you've got this! Swap 시험 for 면접 (myeonjeop, interview) or 발표 (balpyo, presentation) and you can cheer anyone on.
