"Between Steps" reaches its halfway point on a new ratings high

2026-10-24

Phrase from this story: 연애박사 = a love expert (literally 'doctor of dating')Search a word
Official pageDisney+ – Between StepsPoster, trailer and episode info↗

The quiet ENA romance Between Steps is growing week by week. Its sixth episode, which aired on Tuesday, October 20, 2026, recorded an average nationwide rating of 2.447%, according to Nielsen Korea as reported by Soompi. That is the show's highest figure so far, and it came at a good moment: episode 6 marks the halfway point of the 12-episode series.

Slow but steady

The drama started on October 5 with 1.762% nationwide. It jumped to 2.359% the next day, dipped slightly to 2.089% for episode 3 and climbed back to 2.250% for episode 4 (Nielsen Korea figures as listed on Wikipedia). Numbers around 2% may look small, but ENA is a cable channel, and cable shows usually draw smaller audiences than the big free-to-air networks.

On the same night, tvN's Love in Disguise aired its second episode and scored 4.7%, a small dip from its premiere but still first in its time slot among cable channels.

Between Steps airs on Mondays and Tuesdays at 10 p.m. KST on ENA, streams on Genie TV in Korea and is available on Disney+ in selected regions (as of October 2026). The final episode is scheduled for November 10.

Love in a robotics lab

The story is set in the robotics lab of the fictional Seohan University. Choo Young-woo plays Park Min-jae, a robotics PhD student and lab manager who was a competitive swimmer until he lost a leg to illness. Kim So-hyun plays Lim Yu-jin, a master's student in the same lab who has lost her way in her studies and is starting over. Disney+ sums it up neatly: Min-jae lost a leg, Yu-jin lost her way.

The series is directed by Ahn Pan-seok, known for Something in the Rain, and written by Min Hyo-jung. It is a slow-burn, slice-of-life romance rather than a fast, twist-filled drama, which may explain why its audience is building gradually.

Why it matters for learners

The Korean title is 연애박사 (yeonaebakssa [여내박싸]). It is made of two words:

  • 연애 (yeonae [여내]) – dating, a romantic relationship. It is different from 사랑 (sarang), which is love in general.
  • 박사 (bakssa [박싸]) – a doctorate, or a person with one. Note the tensified ㅅ: you say [박싸], not "bak-sa".

So the title literally means "doctor of dating." In everyday speech, Koreans put 박사 after a noun to call someone an expert, often with a smile: a friend who knows every café in town is a 카페 박사 (kape bakssa). The title plays on both meanings, because Min-jae is a real PhD student, but in love he is very much a beginner.

University words come up all through the drama. A master's degree is 석사 (seokssa [석싸]), a PhD course is 박사 과정 (bakssa gwajeong), and both students are 대학원생 (daehagwonsaeng [대하권생]), graduate students.

A fun pattern to praise someone's know-how: N + 박사예요 – "is an expert at N." For example, 제 친구는 맛집 박사예요 (je chinguneun matjjip bakssayeyo [제 친구는 맏찝 박싸예요]) – "My friend is an expert on good restaurants."
