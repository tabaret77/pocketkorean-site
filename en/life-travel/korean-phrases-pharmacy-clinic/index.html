Korean phrases for the pharmacy and the doctor

2026-10-22

Phrase from this story: 아파요 = it hurts / I'm sickSearch a word

Late autumn in Korea brings dry air, chilly mornings and the first wave of colds. Getting help is easy: pharmacies, 약국 (yakguk), are on almost every street, marked with a big green or red 약 (yak), "medicine," and small neighbourhood clinics see walk-in patients without an appointment. A few short phrases will get you most of the way. All of these use the polite -요 (-yo) ending.

The one pattern you need

[body part] + 이/가 아파요. – My [body part] hurts.

  • 머리가 아파요. (meoriga apayo.) – I have a headache.
  • 목이 아파요. (mogi apayo.) – I have a sore throat.
  • 배가 아파요. (baega apayo.) – I have a stomach ache.

Use 가 (ga) after a vowel and 이 (i) after a consonant. On its own, 아파요 (apayo) means "it hurts" or "I'm sick."

At the pharmacy

  1. 감기약 있어요? (gamgiyak isseoyo?) – Do you have cold medicine?
  2. 열이 나요. (yeori nayo.) – I have a fever.
  3. 기침이 나요. (gichimi nayo.) – I have a cough.
  4. 콧물이 나요. (konmuri nayo.) – I have a runny nose.
  5. 소화가 안 돼요. (sohwaga an dwaeyo.) – I have indigestion.
  6. 어제부터요. (eojebuteoyo.) – Since yesterday.

The pharmacist will probably ask 어디가 아프세요? (eodiga apeuseyo?) – "Where does it hurt?" – and how to take it: 하루에 세 번, 식후에 드세요 (harue se beon, sikue deuseyo), "three times a day, after meals." If you have an allergy, say [thing] 알레르기가 있어요 (allereugiga isseoyo), "I'm allergic to [thing]."

When pharmacies are closed, convenience stores sell a short list of basic medicines, such as painkillers, fever reducers and digestion aids. As of October 2026, 11 products are available this way, and the government is discussing adding more.

At a clinic or hospital

For a cold or a minor injury, Koreans go to a small clinic, 의원 (uiwon), often named after its speciality: 내과 (naegwa) for internal medicine, 이비인후과 (ibiinhugwa) for ear, nose and throat. Larger hospitals, 병원 (byeongwon), are for serious cases.

  1. 진료 받고 싶어요. (jillyo batgo sipeoyo.) – I'd like to see a doctor.
  2. 처음 왔어요. (cheoeum wasseoyo.) – It's my first visit. Reception will ask for your passport or residence card.
  3. 영어 하는 의사 선생님 있어요? (yeongeo haneun uisa seonsaengnim isseoyo?) – Is there a doctor who speaks English?
  4. 여기가 아파요. (yeogiga apayo.) – It hurts here. Point to the spot.
  5. 보험이 없어요. (boheomi eopseoyo.) – I don't have (Korean) insurance.
  6. 처방전 주세요. (cheobangjeon juseyo.) – Could I have the prescription, please? Clinics write a prescription, 처방전 (cheobangjeon), and you take it to a pharmacy, often in the same building.
  7. 진단서 받을 수 있어요? (jindanseo badeul su isseoyo?) – Can I get a medical certificate? Useful for travel insurance claims.

In an emergency

  • 119: ambulance and fire.
  • 112: police.
  • 1330: the Korea Travel Hotline run by the Korea Tourism Organization, with help in English and other languages. It can also join a three-way call with emergency services.

The key phrase: 도와주세요! (dowajuseyo!) – Help! Then 구급차 불러 주세요 (gugeupcha bulleo juseyo), "Please call an ambulance."
