Bulguksa and Gyeongju in autumn

2026-10-23

Basile Morin / CC BY-SA 4.0 (Wikimedia Commons)

Phrase from this story: 절 = Buddhist templeSearch a word

Seoul's leaves are turning now, but in the south-east the show is still to come. According to the 2026 foliage map from the Korea Tourism Organization and the Korea National Arboretum, colours in the Gyeongsang region should peak in early to mid-November. That makes the next few weeks a fine time for 경주 (Gyeongju), the old capital of the Silla kingdom, and its most famous 절 (jeol), Buddhist temple: 불국사 (Bulguksa). Details below are as of October 2026.

A temple built as a Buddhist paradise

Bulguksa means "temple of the Buddha land." A temple first stood here in the reign of King Beopheung of Silla, in the 6th century, and the great rebuilding began in 751 under the minister Kim Daeseong. In December 1995 it was added to UNESCO's World Heritage list together with nearby 석굴암 (Seokguram), a stone grotto with a seated Buddha on the slopes of Tohamsan.

What to look for:

  • 청운교 (Cheongungyo) and 백운교 (Baegungyo), the "blue cloud" and "white cloud" bridges: two flights of stone steps leading up to the main gate, the view in the photo above and the classic Bulguksa shot.
  • 다보탑 (Dabotap) and 석가탑 (Seokgatap), two stone pagodas in the main courtyard. Dabotap is richly decorated; Seokgatap is plain and elegant. Seeing them side by side is the point.
  • The stone terraces under the wooden halls, which show how carefully Silla builders fitted rock together.

In autumn the trees around the grounds turn red, orange and gold, so take your time on the walk in from the entrance gate.

Tickets, hours and getting there

  • Admission: free. The old ticket booths no longer charge.
  • Hours: open every day, 09:00–18:00, last entry 17:00.
  • Parking: a flat 1,000 won a day for a car; the rear lot is flat and easiest with children or older visitors.
  • Buses: in Gyeongju, buses 10 and 11 leave from opposite the Gyeongju Intercity Bus Terminal, and bus 700 from the Express Bus Terminal. Check the route at the stop, as numbers can change.
  • Seokguram: about 3 km uphill. Bus 12 runs from across the road from the temple and takes around 20 minutes.

From Seoul, the fastest way is a KTX train to Singyeongju Station, then a local bus or taxi.

Make a weekend of it

Gyeongju is small and walkable, and its old sights are close together. After Bulguksa, head back into town for the grassy royal tombs and 첨성대 (Cheomseongdae), an ancient stone observatory. From September to November it is surrounded by fields of fluffy pink muhly grass, one of the most photographed autumn scenes in the country.

Tip: weekend mornings in November get crowded. Arrive at opening time, see the temple first, then take the bus up to Seokguram before the tour groups.
