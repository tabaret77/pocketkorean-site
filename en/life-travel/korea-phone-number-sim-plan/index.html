Getting a Korean phone number: prepaid SIM, contract or 알뜰폰?

2026-10-24

Phrase from this story: 개통 = activation (of a phone line)Search a word

In Korea, your phone number does much more than take calls. Banks, delivery apps, online shops and even some café loyalty schemes check who you are by sending a code to a number registered in your name. So one of the first jobs for a newcomer is getting a 휴대폰 (hyudaepon), a mobile phone line. At the counter you'll hear one word again and again: 개통 (gaetong), activation, the moment your new line goes live. Rules below are as of October 2026.

Three kinds of plan

Prepaid, 선불 (seonbul). You pay first and top up when the credit runs out. This is the only option in your first weeks, before your residence card arrives, because a passport is enough. You can buy a prepaid 유심 (yusim), a SIM card, at the SIM counters in the arrivals halls of Incheon Airport Terminals 1 and 2, or at convenience stores, where you register it with a photo of your passport.

Contract, 후불 (hubul), with one of the big three carriers, SK Telecom, KT or LG U+. You pay monthly by automatic debit. Contracts with a discounted phone usually run for 12 or 24 months, a commitment called 약정 (yakjeong), and leaving early means a cancellation fee.

Budget carriers, 알뜰폰 (altteulpon), literally "thrifty phone." These companies rent space on the big three's networks, so coverage is the same, but plans are cheaper and usually month-to-month. Guides for foreign residents list many plans at around 11,000 to 30,000 won a month as of October 2026, and the comparison site Altteulpon Hub (mvnohub.kr) lets you line them up side by side. Several budget carriers let residence-card holders sign up online, and activation usually takes a day or two.

Most recent phones also take an eSIM. All three big carriers offer them for compatible iPhones and Android phones, and travel eSIMs can be set up before you land. Dial *#06#: if your phone shows an EID number, it supports eSIM. It also has to be unlocked.

What you need to bring

  • Prepaid: your passport.
  • Contract or 알뜰폰: your 외국인등록증 (oegugin deungnokjjeung [외국인등녹쯩]), residence card, your passport, and a way to pay automatically, usually a Korean bank account or a Korean-issued card.
  • Stay length: if 60 days or less are left on your permitted stay, carriers generally won't sign a standard contract, so you'll be pointed to prepaid.

The line must be in your own name, spelled exactly as on your ID. Using a number registered to a friend may seem easy, but it breaks the rules and causes trouble with identity checks later.

New ID checks in 2026

From 6 July 2026, Korea added face verification for new activations and number transfers, 번호이동 (beonho idong), at the big three and the budget carriers. The camera compares your face with your ID photo. Customers can refuse it and use other checks instead. At launch the system covered Korean ID cards; the government said it would upgrade checks of foreigners' IDs with the Ministry of Justice in phases from the second half of 2026. Don't be surprised if a shop asks to scan your face or your residence card more carefully than a friend's did last year.

Tips

  • Start prepaid, switch later. Many newcomers use a prepaid SIM for their first weeks, then move to a contract or 알뜰폰 once the residence card arrives. Ask if you can keep the same number.
  • Go to an official store. For contracts, carriers' own stores, 공식 대리점 (gongsik daerijeom), are safer than resellers pushing expensive phones.
  • Check the data. Plans are sold by data allowance; 데이터 (deiteo) is the word to look for.

이 요금제로 개통해 주세요. (i yogeumjero gaetonghae juseyo.) – Please activate it on this plan. Point at the plan on the leaflet, or swap in 선불로 (seonbullo), "as prepaid": 선불로 개통해 주세요.
