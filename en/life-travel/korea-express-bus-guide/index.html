Korea's express buses: seat classes, Seoul terminals and rest stops

2026-10-24

Phrase from this story: 고속버스 = express busSearch a word

Trains get most of the attention, but Korea's 고속버스 (gosok beoseu [고속뻐스]), express buses, go to far more places. They run non-stop on the motorway from one terminal to another, with frequent departures on busy routes, and they often cost less than the KTX. Here's how to choose a seat, find the right terminal and book a ticket. Details are as of October 2026.

Express or intercity?

There are two kinds of long-distance bus. 고속버스 run non-stop between big terminals. 시외버스 (sioe beoseu), intercity buses, cover shorter or smaller routes and may stop in towns on the way. They often share a building but are booked separately. All seats on both are reserved, so buy a ticket before you board.

Three seat classes

  • 일반 (ilban), standard: four seats across, two on each side of the aisle. The cheapest option and fine for short trips.
  • 우등 (udeung), "superior": three seats across, two on one side and one on the other. These buses usually have 28 seats with wide, deeply reclining chairs. The single seats by the window are popular with solo travellers.
  • 프리미엄 (peurimieom), premium: about 20 or 21 seats with partitions, extra legroom and electric recliners, often with a personal screen. They cost roughly 30% more than 우등. For example, when a premium service on the Taebaek–Dong Seoul route started in August 2026, the fare went from 35,200 won to 45,800 won.

Which Seoul terminal?

Check your departure terminal carefully: Seoul has several, and they serve different regions.

Terminal Subway Main destinations
Seoul Express Bus Terminal (Gyeongbu/Yeongdong lines) Express Bus Terminal, lines 3, 7, 9 Busan, Daegu and the southeast; Gangwon; Chungcheong
Central City (Honam line) Express Bus Terminal, lines 3, 7, 9 Gwangju, Jeonju and the southwest
Dong Seoul Terminal Gangbyeon, line 2 Gangwon, North Chungcheong, North Gyeongsang
Seoul Nambu Terminal Nambu Bus Terminal, line 3 Mostly intercity buses to the south

The two terminals at Express Bus Terminal Station are separate buildings next to each other, so give yourself a few extra minutes to find the right one.

How to book

  • At the terminal: ticket machines and counters sell same-day seats. Popular Friday evening and holiday buses can be 매진 (maejin), sold out, so book ahead at those times.
  • Kobus (kobus.co.kr), the express bus booking site run by Tmoney: Tmoney said in July 2026 that it would start accepting foreign-issued cards from August, without requiring membership.
  • T money GO app: covers express and intercity buses, but it's in Korean, needs an account and takes only Korean-issued cards.
  • Bustago app: from October 2026 it accepts Visa, Mastercard and American Express cards issued abroad, according to a payment partner's announcement.

Your ticket shows the departure time, seat number and 승차장 (seungchajang), the boarding bay. Buses leave on time.

The rest stop

On longer routes the bus pulls into a 휴게소 (hyugeso), a motorway service area, for a break of about 15 minutes. Rest stops are an institution in Korea, with food courts, coffee and snack stalls. Remember your bus number and where it's parked, and be back on time: the driver won't wait for latecomers.
