Korean for clothes shopping: sizes, fitting rooms and refunds

2026-10-23

Phrase from this story: 입어 봐도 돼요? = Can I try it on?Search a word

Late October is when Seoul's shops fill up with coats, knitwear and padded jackets, and the first cold mornings send everyone shopping. Whether you're in Myeongdong, an underground mall or a department store, a handful of phrases will get you through. Everything here uses the polite -요 (-yo) ending, which is right for any shop. Rules and prices are as of October 2026.

Trying things on

The most useful sentence of the day: 입어 봐도 돼요? (ibeo bwado dwaeyo?) – Can I try it on? It comes from 입다 (ipta [입따]), to wear, plus -어 봐도 돼요?, "is it OK if I try…?"

  1. 이거 다른 색 있어요? (igeo dareun saek isseoyo?) – Do you have this in another colour?
  2. 탈의실 어디예요? (taruisil eodiyeyo?) – Where's the fitting room?
  3. 좀 커요. (jom keoyo.) – It's a bit big.
  4. 좀 작아요. (jom jagayo.) – It's a bit small.
  5. 한 사이즈 큰 거 있어요? (han saijeu keun geo isseoyo?) – Do you have one size bigger? Swap 큰 for 작은 (jageun), smaller.
  6. 딱 맞아요. (ttak majayo.) – It fits perfectly.
  7. 그냥 구경하는 거예요. (geunyang gugyeonghaneun geoyeyo.) – I'm just looking.

Two fitting-room customs surprise visitors: you often take off your shoes before stepping into the curtained area, and when trying on tops you may be handed a thin face cover to keep make-up off the clothes.

How Korean sizes work

Korean labels don't always use S, M and L.

  • Men's tops are numbered by chest size: 95 is roughly a US small and 100 a US medium, the most common size. Shoulders tend to run narrow, so many Western shoppers go up to 105.
  • Women's tops use 44, 55, 66 and 77. 55 is the standard size, roughly a US 4–6.
  • 프리사이즈 (peurisaijeu), "free size," means one size only, usually cut for a fairly small frame.
  • Shoes are measured in millimetres: 240 is about a women's US 7, and 270 about a men's US 9.

If trousers are too long, ask about 수선 (suseon), alterations. Hemming typically costs a few thousand won and is often done within an hour.

Paying, exchanges and refunds

  1. 이걸로 할게요. (igeollo halgeyo.) – I'll take this one.
  2. 카드 돼요? (kadeu dwaeyo?) – Can I pay by card?
  3. 교환 돼요? (gyohwan dwaeyo?) – Can I exchange it?
  4. 환불하고 싶어요. (hwanbulhago sipeoyo.) – I'd like a refund.
  5. 영수증 여기 있어요. (yeongsujeung yeogi isseoyo.) – Here's the receipt.

Return rules depend on the shop. Department stores and large brands usually accept returns with a receipt within about 7 to 30 days, while street stalls and underground malls rarely refund and may only exchange. Ask before you pay: 환불 돼요? (hwanbul dwaeyo?), "Can I get a refund?"

Visitors can also ask 택스 리펀 돼요? (taeksseu ripeon dwaeyo? [택쓰 리펀 돼요?]) – "Can I get a tax refund?" At most tax-free shops you need to spend at least 15,000 won in one transaction and show your passport. An instant refund at the till is possible for purchases under 1,000,000 won each, up to a total of 5,000,000 won per trip.

[Item] 입어 봐도 돼요? – Can I try on [item]? For shoes and hats, which you don't "wear" with 입다 in Korean, use 신어 봐도 돼요? (sineo bwado dwaeyo?) for shoes and 써 봐도 돼요? (sseo bwado dwaeyo?) for hats.
