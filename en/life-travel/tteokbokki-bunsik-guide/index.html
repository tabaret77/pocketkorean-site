Tteokbokki and the bunsikjip: how to eat at a Korean snack bar

2026-10-24

johl / CC BY-SA 2.0 (Wikimedia Commons)

Phrase from this story: 분식집 = Korean snack barSearch a word

Every Korean neighbourhood has one: a small, brightly lit restaurant near a school or subway exit, with a long menu on the wall and a big pan of red rice cakes bubbling by the door. This is the 분식집 (bunsikjjip [분식찝]), the snack bar. 분식 (bunsik) originally meant food made from flour, but today it covers a whole family of cheap, filling comfort food that students, office workers and late-night drinkers all love. Prices below are as of October 2026.

The classic menu

떡볶이 (tteokbokki [떡뽀끼]) is the heart of it: chewy cylinders of rice cake simmered in a sweet, spicy sauce of red pepper paste, usually with fish cake, spring onion and sometimes a boiled egg. The red version is fairly young. It is said to have been first sold in 1953 in Sindang-dong, Seoul, by a woman named Ma Bok-rim, whose sauce mixed red pepper paste with black bean paste. Long before that, the royal court ate 궁중떡볶이 (gungjung tteokbokki), stir-fried rice cakes with beef and vegetables and no chilli at all. Some shops use rice cakes made of rice, 쌀떡 (ssaltteok), others wheat ones, 밀떡 (miltteok), which spread after the Korean War when flour became widely available. Fans argue about which is better.

순대 (sundae) is Korean blood sausage, a pig-intestine casing stuffed mainly with glass noodles and pig's blood. It's mild and soft, and it's sliced and served with salt for dipping. You may be asked whether you want 내장 (naejang), offal such as liver and lung, mixed in.

튀김 (twigim) means fried things: squid, sweet potato, chilli, boiled egg and 김말이 (gimmari), glass noodles rolled in seaweed and deep-fried. The best way to eat them is to dip them in the tteokbokki sauce.

김밥 (gimbap) is rice and fillings rolled in seaweed and sliced. According to the Korea Consumer Agency, one roll cost an average of 3,923 won in Seoul in September 2026, up 8.3% in a year.

You'll also see 어묵 (eomuk), fish cake on skewers in broth, and 라볶이 (rabokki), tteokbokki cooked with instant ramen noodles.

The perfect combo: 떡튀순

Order tteokbokki, a few fried snacks and some sundae together and you have 떡튀순 (tteoktwisun), short for 떡볶이, 튀김 and 순대. It's the classic set for two or three people sharing, and some shops sell it as one dish.

How ordering works

  • Pay first, often. Many snack bars have a touchscreen kiosk or ask you to pay at the counter before the food comes. Look for a menu board with numbers or a screen by the door.
  • Order by portion. Tteokbokki is sold by the plate, 인분 (inbun), and kimbap by the roll, 줄 (jul): 김밥 한 줄 (gimbap han jul), one roll of kimbap.
  • Choose the heat. Shops that let you choose usually offer 순한맛 (sunhanmat), mild, 보통맛 (botongmat), medium, and 매운맛 (maeunmat), hot. If the menu doesn't say, ask for it less spicy.
  • Help yourself. Water, pickled radish and chopsticks are often self-service. Look for a sign saying 셀프 (selpeu).

Korean you can use

Korean Romanization Meaning
분식집 bunsikjjip [분식찝] snack bar
떡볶이 tteokbokki [떡뽀끼] spicy rice cakes
순대 sundae Korean blood sausage
튀김 twigim fried snacks
김밥 gimbap seaweed rice roll
떡튀순 tteoktwisun tteokbokki, twigim and sundae set
맵다 maepta [맵따] to be spicy
순한맛 sunhanmat mild flavour
찍어 먹다 jjigeo meokta [찌거 먹따] to dip and eat

덜 맵게 해 주세요. (deol maepkke hae juseyo. [덜 맵께 해 주세요.]) – Please make it less spicy. Brave enough for more? Swap 덜 (deol), less, for 더 (deo), more: 더 맵게 해 주세요.
